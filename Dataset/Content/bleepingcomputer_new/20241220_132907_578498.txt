
	   


Chaos reigned in the Philippines yesterday as one of the country's main banks had to shut down ATM withdrawals and online transactions amid rumors of getting hacked.

Problems for the Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI) started on late Tuesday night, June 6, when users started noticing money missing from their bank accounts.

Things got worse on the next day when amid growing rumors of getting hacked, BPI officials shut down ATM withdrawals and online transactions.

Double-posting of older transactions caused the incident

Later in the day, BPI officials dispelled any rumors of getting hacked. Via a press conference and messages on its website and Twitter account, the bank announced the missing funds were because of a technical glitch in the bank's system.

According to the bank's explanation, on Tuesday, the bank's IT system double-posted some financial transactions that took place between April 27 and May 2.

The bank says it's these double-postings that resulted in new money being withdrawn from customer accounts and not a hack.

The bank said its IT system will remain down for the rest of Wednesday, as its engineers work to correct the problem and return money to customers' accounts.

BPI bank offices and branches remained opened in the Philippines and ATM and online transactions will return to normal tomorrow, on Thursday, June 8, the bank said.

Customer money going "puff" into thin air is the incident bank managers have nightmares about. These types of technical glitches rank pretty high in the worst case scenarios banks work hard on preventing.
