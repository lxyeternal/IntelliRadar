Verizon's 2019 Data Breach Investigations Report (DBIR) released today offers detailed observations stemming from the analysis of cyber incidents over the past year.

The 78-page document is the result of examining more than 41,000 security incidents, over 2,000 of them being confirmed breaches affecting various verticals in the US.

A breach is an incident acknowledged to have resulted in the disclosure of data to an unauthorized party. Conclusions rely on a set of 2,013 such events.

Government agencies most attacked

The cyber incidents affecting the public administration over the past year amounted to over 23,000, with 330 being confirmed as breaches (up by 17% compared to 2017).


Given the sheer number of incidents in this sector, you would think that the government incident responders must either be cape and tights wearing superheroes, or so stressed they’re barely hanging on by their fingernails.


According to Verizon, this sector recorded the highest number of cyber incidents. This translates to about 16% of all breaches. Runner up is the healthcare industry, with 304 such events, meaning around 15% of the total.



"Cyber-Espionage is rampant in the Public sector"

Verizon's report says that an external actor was involved in 75% of the breaches targeting public administration. In 79% of these cases, the attacker was linked to a nation-state and the purpose was cyber-espionage.

In 2017, espionage was also the number one reason for hackers to compromise computer systems used by entities in the public sector, but the change is that the number of events done for this purpose has seen a 168% increase.

As for the attack vector leading to the intrusion, Verizon says that phishing (294 cases) and a backdoor connecting to a command and control server was the most common combination.




Admittedly we do not have as much data as to what is happening beyond the deception and initial device compromise. The inclusion of keylogging malware is a good indicator that additional credential theft and reuse is a likely next step.


Espionage-related breaches are also likely to remain undiscovered for a long period, allowing the attacker to exfiltrate huge amounts of data from the compromised government systems.

Verizon's assessment of public breaches is that they are 2.5 times more likely to be discovered years after the intrusion. The harsh reality comes from the lack of external fraud detection. However, the company does not have a timeline of the incidents used to create the statistics.

Most BEC scams are unsuccessful

Additional information in the report refers to the motivation behind the attack, which is still financial profit (71% of the breach events).

Of the malware-driven attacks (28%), most threats were delivered via email (94%) and the most popular file types were malicious Office documents (45%).

Breaches that involved exploiting vulnerabilities recorded were just 6% of the entire dataset.

An interesting detail referring to business email compromise (BEC) scams is that the average amount stolen was $24,439 - calculated from 18,606 incidents.

BEC's are not that profitable, Verizon says, because the Recovery Asset Team of the Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) can recover the loss by working with the destination bank.

When this happened, "half of all US-based business email compromises had 99% of the money recovered or frozen." Failing to recover the money occurred in 9% of the cases.

Yet, these attacks remain attractive to cybercriminals because they are an easy way to make a profit without putting too much effort into it. And when the scam is successful, the payout is typically sufficiently large to make up for the failed attempts.
