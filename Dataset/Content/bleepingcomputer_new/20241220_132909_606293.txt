
	   


One Android app with spyware capabilities based on an open-source remote access tool (RAT) has twice thwarted the security of Google Play over a period of two weeks.

Radio Balouch (or RB Music) included functionality from AhMyth Android RAT, an open-source project software that became public in late 2017. Since its open release, several malicious mobile apps borrowed its code to spy on Android users.

There's a first for everything

Until now, there have not been any reports of apps built on AhMyth that enjoyed worldwide distribution through Google Play. This makes Radio Balouch the first in the bunch to bypass Google's app-vetting mechanism and make it into Android's official apps store.

The SMS function does not work though, due to recent Google Play controls that require an app to "be actively registered as the default SMS or Assistant handler on the device."

Lukas Stefanko, Android malware researcher at ESET, says that AhMyth-based spyware is available on alternative app stores and has been promoted on Instagram and YouTube. Despite reporting the campaigns to the service providers, there was no reply.

The malicious Radio Balouch app was first reported on Google Play on July 2 and removed a day later. A comeback was spotted on July 13 and Google acted quickly to boot it. In both cases, the number of downloads was above 100.



ESET's research does not mention any special measures used to get past Google's security verification, which is all the more surprising considering that AhMyth has been available for so long and does not have obfuscated code.

While 100 downloads is not much distribution, it is worth noting that the spying app was kicked from the official Android store following ESET's report.

Better screening needed

Spying-ready variants of RB Music are still present on third-party app stores, Stefanko says, and the developer set up Instagram and YouTube accounts most likely to start promoting it.


"The attackers’ Instagram account still, at the time of writing, serves a link to the app that has been removed from Google Play. They have also set up a YouTube channel with one video introducing the app; apparently, they don’t promote it as the video has a mere 21 views at the time of writing."


ESET's research shows that Radio Balouch offers the option to register an account. This, though, is just a lure for the user to type in login credentials as any input will show that the operation was successful.

Stefanko believes this functionality to be for harvesting logins to try them on accounts from other services in credential stuffing attacks.

The researcher warns that unless Google improves screening for malicious apps, clones of this app or derivatives of AhMyth are likely to appear in Play Store.


"While the key security imperative “Stick with official sources of apps” still holds, it alone can’t guarantee security. It is highly recommended that users scrutinize every app they intend to install on their devices and use a reputable mobile security solution." - Lukas Stefanko
