
	   


A large-scale payment card skimming campaign that successfully breached 962 e-commerce stores was discovered today by Magento security research company Sanguine Security.

The campaign seems to be automated according to Sanguine Security researcher Willem de Groot who told BleepingComputer that the card skimming script was added within a 24-hour timeframe. "It would be nearly impossible to breach 960+ stores manually in such a short time," he added.

Even though no information on how such automated Magecart attacks against e-commerce websites would work was shared by Sanguine Security, the procedure would most likely entail scanning for and exploiting security flaws in the stores' software platform.

While details on how the online stores were breached are still scarce given that the logs are still being analyzed, the JavaScript-based payment data skimmer script was decoded and uploaded by the security company to GitHub Gist.

As shown from its source code, the skimmer was used by the attackers to collect e-commerce customers' payment info on breached stores, including full credit card data, names, phones, and addresses.

Out of all the infected stores spread all over the world, "most victims are small, but there are a number of enterprise stores among them," as detailed by de Groot.


Data collected by the skimmer script

As RiskIQ's head of threat research Yonathan Klijnsma told BleepingComputer after this story was published, the group behind this campaign is Magecart Group 7, an outfit known to have used "automated exploits for known bugs" in previous attacks.

Also, as explained in RiskIQ's "Inside Magecart" report, Group 7 "doesn’t have a well-defined modus operandi other than compromising any e-commerce site it can find" and "does not go for top-tier websites, but it does not shy away from large medium-tier sites,"

In addition, just like in the case of these attacks, this Magecart group "does not use servers, adding the script tag directly on the website, so a victim normally encounters this skimmer as a small snippet of script on their website."



Another Magecart attack was unearthed by security researcher Micham who spotted a malicious skimmer being injected within the site of The Guardian via old AWS S3 bucket and using wix-cloud[.]com as a skimmer gate.

Malwarebytes' Jérôme Segura said yesterday that this was part of a Magecart campaign abusing Amazon CloudFront CDN discovered during June, with "a number of compromises on Amazon CloudFront – a Content Delivery Network (CDN) – where hosted JavaScript libraries were tampered with and injected with web skimmers."


Skimmer injected in The Guardian's site

Magecart groups are diversifying their attacks

Magecart groups are hacking outfits that have been active since around 2015 and they represent a continuously threat capably of launching attacks against both international companies like British Airways, Ticketmaster, OXO, and Newegg, and smaller sized businesses like MyPillow and Amerisleep.

Magecart campaigns designed to steal payment card data from online store customers are as active as ever, with their activity rarely showing any lows.

Klijnsma also said in a report detailing the expansion of Magecart activity to OSCommerce and OpenCart powered stores that "for every Magecart attack that makes headlines, we detect thousands more that we don’t disclose. A considerable portion of these lesser-known breaches involves third-party payment platforms."

"They’re currently focusing on payment data, but we’re already seeing moves to skim login credentials and other sensitive information. This widens the scope of potential Magecart victims far beyond e-commerce alone," further explained stated Klijnsma in an in-depth analysis of a large-scale operation against OpenCart online stores.

As further proof of this, threat intelligence firm Group-IB found 2,440 compromised stores during early-April that got infected with payment data skimmers by various Magecart groups.



During late-April, Segura also discovered hundreds of Magento stores being actively injected with GitHub-hosted skimmer scripts, while attackers also infected Atlanta Hawks NBA basketball team's online shop as unearthed by de Groot.

At the beginning of May, another Magecart group developed a polymorphic Magecart skimmer script that featured support for an impressive number of 57 payment gateways from all around the world that can be injected within almost any checkout page of any online shop to scrape customers' credit card info without the need of customization.

Also in May, TrendMicro found that the checkout pages of hundreds of U.S. and Canadian PrismWeb-powered campus stores were breached and compromised by a Magecart group dubbed Mirrorthief.

Update: The group behind this campaign has been identified as Magecart Group 7 by RiskIQ's Yonathan Klijnsma
