Before its short hiatus, Fallout EK used the use-after-free CVE-2018-4878 Adobe Flash Player vulnerability and the remote code execution CVE-2018-8174 targeting the Windows VBScript engine.

As discovered by security researcher Kafeine, the resurrected Fallout EK is now capable of exploiting the CVE-2018-15982 zero-day user after free vulnerability discovered and patched on December 5 by Adobe. The zero-day was used at the time as part of an APT attack against the Russian FSBI "Polyclinic #2" medical clinic and wearing the "Operation Poison Needles" label.

Kafeine also found that Fallout is the second exploit kit to add support for CVE-2018-15982 after Underminer.


Fallout exploiting CVE-2018-15982 on Windows 7 

According to the underground advert promoting Fallout EK, "The code obfuscation and landing generation mechanism has been completely redesigned" and the exploit kit now comes with "Increased performance." The same underground ad mentions the removal of the Internet Explorer CVE-2018-8373 RCE vulnerability because of its unstable flow rate.

In discussions with Kafeine, the researcher showed BleepingComputer that he observed the Fallout Exploit Kit being distributed by malvertising campaigns through Popcash, TrafficShop, RevenueHits, and others.

Another analysis by Malwarebytes' Jérôme Segura observed that Fallout EK is also being distributed via HookAds, which was previously disseminating the RIG-v EK, Fallout EK during November, and Neutrino EK in August.

As MalwareBytes details and as mentioned in the underground advert mentioned before, the new Fallout drops the payload via Powershell unlike older iterations which used iexplore.exe. Segura says that "This technique is most likely an attempt at evasion, as traditionally we’d expect the Internet Explorer process to drop the payload."


Payload delivery via Powershell

Moreover, the new and improved Fallout EK has started pushing the GandCrab ransomware again starting January 15, just as it did back in September 2018.

The other payloads now disseminated via Fallout are Smokebot which was seen installing Azorult, Tinynuke+Azorult, Dridex, the ServHelper tunnel variant, and other malware strains not yet identified. A fully detailed plot of all possible Fallout "workflows" was provided to BleepingComputer by Kafeine.


Fallout EK possible payload drop scenarios

The Fallout exploit kit has previously been observed while pushing a wide range of other malware strains and families, ranging from SAVEfiles Ransomware and Kraken Cryptor Ransomware, to GlobeImposter ransomware, the DanaBot banking Trojan, the Nocturnal information stealer, some malware downloading Trojans, and other potentially unwanted programs (PUPs).
