
	   


Security researchers have uncovered new details about the infrastructure used by LoJax UEFI rootkit used in attacks from APT28. The analysis revealed two command and control (C2) servers were still active in early 2019.

LoJax rootkit is based on LoJack, a legitimate software that tracks and locates laptop, smartphone, or tablet devices. Its campaigns likely started towards the end of 2016, according to new research.

Because it has persistence on the system, LoJack was modified by the threat actors - APT28, also known as Fancy Bear, Sednit, Strontium, and Sofacy, to report to their server.

Researchers at NETSCOUT Arbor's Security Engineering & Response Team (ASERT) identified multiple servers that were part of the LoJax infrastructure.

Using passive DNS records, ASERT was able to determine the domain names acting as C2 servers for the rootkit. Some of them have not been seen in LoJax attacks.

The research mapped some IP addresses the UK's National Cyber Security Center (NCSC) gave as indicators of compromise for malware used by FAncy bear in a report in October of last year.

		Looking at historic domain registration information, the researchers noticed that some them came online as early as 2004. This does not necessarily mean that Fancy Bear used them since that year.



ASERT believes the domains could have changed multiple hands until they came under the threat actor's control, although they do not exclude the possibility that Fancy Bear owned them before the main LoJax campaigns believed to have started in late 2016.

"The software [LoJack] hijacked by the actors had a compile date in 2008, possibly indicating that the earlier activity was erroneous or unrelated to current operations," ASERT writes in a report shared with BleepingComputer.

Based on the findings from ASERT, some LoJax campaigns may still be active despite news about the malware operations.
