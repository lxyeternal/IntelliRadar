
	   


Over the past few months, an Office vulnerability has become one of the most popular and efficient ways of delivering malware to vulnerable computers.

The vulnerability — tracked as CVE-2017-0199 — was found by McAfee and FireEye employees in April of this year, and was a zero-day at the time of its discovery.

In April, attackers were using it to deliver an RTF file to their targets, which when opened would automatically execute an OLE2link object and run malicious code to compromise users' machines.

Researchers have seen CVE-2017-0199 used to target pro-Russian separatists in Ukraine in a cyber-espionage campaign, but also to deliver the Latentbot backdoor trojan in mundane financial malware campaigns.

Later, the same exploit was used to deliver the Cerber ransomware via spam emails pushing malicious DOC files, and in supply chain attacks by the Cobalt (aka FIN7) economical espionage unit.

Attacks use CVE-2017-0199 and fail miserably

Yesterday, both Cisco and Trend Micro spotted new campaigns using this new exploit, confirming its popularity among crooks.

The attacks, spotted by Cisco, were from a group that had chained CVE-2017-0199 to CVE-2012-0158, which was 2016's most popular vulnerability.

According to Cisco, this group spread a spam campaign that delivered the same old RTF documents that downloaded an HTA file to install malware.

Crooks were trying to deliver either the Ramnit banking trojan or the Lokibot infostealer. Cisco experts say that most attempts to infect users failed because if the user's computer was patched against any of the two exploits, the infection attempt would fail.

"This attack was unsuccessful, potentially indicating poor testing or quality control procedures by the attackers," said the Cisco Talos team.

Attackers get creative with CVE-2017-0199

While the first of these new attacks with CVE-2017-0199 were poorly implemented, the second one was quite creative, and malware devs came up with a new way of exploiting the Office flaw.

Spotted by Trend Micro, these new attacks exploited the same CVE-2017-0199 vulnerability, but via PowerPoint PPTX files instead of RTF. Attackers would use the PPTX file to download another DOC file, open it, and install the Remcos RAT.

The majority of attacks targeted companies involved in the electronics manufacturing industry, suggesting the attacks were part of some sort of economical espionage campaign.

"Most detection methods for CVE-2017-0199 focuses on the RTF method of attack, the use of a new vector—PPSX files—allows attackers to evade antivirus detection," said Trend Micro.

Cybereason also saw this spike in Remcos RAT infections. The company put together a guide on how to create a file on the user's computer and prevent Remcos from taking over the user's PC.

Microsoft issued security updates for CVE-2017-0199 as part of the company's April 2017 Patch Tuesday.
