
	   
If something is too good to be true, then it probably is. Such is the case with the many scams that are advertised on the Internet such as getting free rewards by participating in surveys, unbelievable work at home offers, and what we will be discussing in this article, free Amazon gift cards.

Recently while I was examining popups displayed by adware for new malware and unwanted Chrome extension to analyze, I ran into a web site promoting free Amazon gift cards. The catch was that you had to let the page's countdown reach zero before you would get the free gift card.



I knew immediately this was scam, but as it was a new one to me, I decided to dig a little deeper. At first glance, I thought this was going to be a new in-browser miner, but when I opened Fiddler to see the network traffic, I saw instead that the browser was repeatedly connecting to and watching YouTube videos. 


Repeated Watching of YouTube Videos

It was strange, though, because ads were not being displayed, so this was not done to generate revenue. When I posted about it on Twitter, some of the responses indicated that maybe it was being done to get past YouTube's new monetization requirement of 1,000 subscribers & 4,000 hours of annual video watching time.

While this may be the intent, as some of the channels that the videos are located in only have 60 subscribers, I personally think it is being done for a different reason. My guess is that this method is being done to inflate views in the hopes that the higher view counts will make the videos rank more favorably in YouTube searches.

Regardless of the reasons, ultimately I never got the free Amazon gift card. I do not think it's because the countdown never gets to zero and continuously resets to 59 seconds, but rather than it being nothing more than a scam.

With that said, be smart and be safe. Always remember, rarely is anything free in life and if you see a web site offering something for free, it generally is too good to be true.
