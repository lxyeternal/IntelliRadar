The entries were selected based on objective criteria compared to the previous list, considering reports from security researchers along with prevalence, severity, and exploitation in the real-world.

New flaw in the top spot

A new vulnerability is now at the top of the list, a position previously held by SQL injection, which has fallen to the sixth spot.

"Weaknesses that are rarely exploited will not receive a high score, regardless of the typical severity associated with any exploitation. This makes sense, since if developers are not making a particular mistake, then the weakness should not be highlighted in the CWE Top 25" - MITRE


The vulnerability with the highest average severity score, 9.129 out of 10, is SQL injection  since it is has a high probability to be exploited and impact because it can compromise databases with sensitive data.

Cross-site scripting (XSS) takes second place on the list, despite having a low severity average score of 5.778. This position is explained by the high likelihood of exploitation that could enable an attacker to run unauthorized code or commands and its prevalence in reports.

'Improper input validation' ranks third in the list, with a severity score of  7.242. It also has high chances of being exploited and the potential for harm ranges from denial of service, exiting or restarting the vulnerable application to reading or modifying memory and executing unauthorized code or commands.

Responsible for updating the top 25 security flaws is the Homeland Security Systems Engineering and Development (HSSED) under the US Science and Technology Directorate within the US Department of Homeland Security (DHS).

The purpose of the update is to help improve cybersecurity resiliency. The list is intended as a guidance document designed to help developers build more secure software before releasing it on the market.  ChrisLevendis, says in a blog post today that the data-driven approach reflects the problems seen in the real world.
