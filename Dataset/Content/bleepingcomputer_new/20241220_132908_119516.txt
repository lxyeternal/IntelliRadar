
	   
Some clever Russian crook has found a way to register a lookalike Google domain by taking advantage of Unicode characters to create an alternative way of spelling Google.

The crook replaced the first letter of the word google, which is "g", with "ɢ", a character that's part of the expanded Unicode character set.

google.com   ----->   ɢoogle.com

Also known as "small capitals," these types of letters and many other Unicode characters can be used as valid characters in the registration of domain names.

This has been possible for the past six years, so it's surprising that nobody thought to use alternative Unicode-based spelling to register domain names for big brands until now.

It was employees of web analytics firm Analytics Edge that discovered the ɢoogle.com domain.

Analytics Edge says that the group behind this domain had used it for "referral spam," which is a term that describes the side-effect of somebody abusing a site's resources or linking back in massive numbers.

This results in fake referral mentions that contaminate Google Analytics data and hide the true location of incoming links Most webmasters cope with referral spam by adding filters to their Google Analytics dashboard in order to ignore abusive sources.

Spammers: Vote for Trump!

For this particular case, the Analytics Edge team says that spammers weren't interested in probing websites for vulnerabilities or hotlinking images.

Inside their referral links, the Russian spammer(s) left a political message that read "‘Vote for Trump," which appeared in the dashboards of several of Analytics Edge's clients.

This is when the company identified the source of this referral spam as originating from the domain secret.ɢoogle.com.

Since it looked strange that Google would resort to this tactic to convey a politically-motivated message, researchers quickly tracked down the website and identified the flawed spelling in its name.

Domain registration data later revealed the site had been registered by a person located in Russia.

This incident should serve as a warning, once again, to pay close attention to the URL you're accessing. But to be fair, in this case, it would have been very hard even for tech-savvy users to catch the oddly-spelled domain name.
