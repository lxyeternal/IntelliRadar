
	   


Users of Telegram Messengers in certain parts of the world had trouble exchanging messages through the service today. The problems were caused by a distributed denial-of-service attack aiming at Telegram servers.

The service outage affected users mainly in South and North America, although connection issues were present in other parts of the world as well.

In a Twitter announcement, Telegram informed that its servers today were the target of a DDoS attack deployed by a network of compromised computers.

The botnet sent so much useless traffic to Telegram servers that they could no longer handle the requests from legitimate users, leading to unstable connections.

According to Downdetector, a website that tracks in real time the disruptions and outages affecting various digital services, the hot spots for the DDoS attack impact were on the East Coast of the Americas, in the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, Germany, Ukraine, Russia, and China.

However, users in other locations were also affected, as some people in Australia reported problems with loading video content.



While some users speculated about who could be behind the attack saying this might be the work of Brazilian hackers, the Russian or the Chinese government, Telegram clarified that the main trait of a botnet is that it's formed of compromised computers located anywhere in the world.

Even if they were from a specific region it does not necessarily indicate that the attacker is from that location, too. Furthermore, there are DDoS services that rent botnet power, so anyone in the world can deploy an attack against a target

After explaining in ELI5 fashion what a DDoS attack is and the effect it has, Telegram assured its users that their data remained safe.



For the moment, the situation is stable and users should no longer experience issues when using the Telegram messaging service.

In March 2018, Telegram boasted serving a base of 200 million active users, with more coming to the platform as other instant messaging alternatives suffered connection problems.

For instance, Telegram service founder Pavel Durov, announced in March 2019 that three million registered on the platform in a period of 24 hours. The time of the statement coincided with outages for Facebook, WhatsApp, and Instagram.

Update [06.13.2019]: Pavel Durov said on Twitter that the attacking IP addresses were coming mostly from China.

"Historically, all state actor-sized DDoS (200-400 Gb/s of junk) we experienced coincided in time with protests in Hong Kong (coordinated on @telegram). This case was not an exception," said Telegram's founder.

For context, people in Hong Kong have been protesting since Sunday against a controversial extradition bill that would allow suspects to be sent to mainland China for trial. A vote on the bill was scheduled for Wednesday but mass demonstrations forced postponement to Friday. It appears that a vote won't take place tomorrow either tomorrow as the protests forced the government and legislative buildings to shut their doors.

Protest leaders are calling for a new demonstration in Hong Kong on Sunday.
