
	   


Malicious actors compromised the Magento installations of a few hundred e-commerce websites and injected them with Magecart skimmer scripts hosted on GitHub.

Magecart groups have been active since 2015 and represent an ever-evolving cyber threat which was behind attacks against high profile international companies like Ticketmaster, British Airways, OXO, and Newegg, as well as smaller retailers such as MyPillow and Amerisleep.

These malicious campaigns are still going strong seeing that during early April security outfit Group-IB discovered 2,440 websites which had been infected using payment card skimming scripts.

The payment card scraper script discovered on GitHub was uploaded on April 20 and it was obfuscated using hexadecimal encoding as unearthed by Malwarebytes security researcher Jérôme Segura.

After receiving Segura's disclosure report regarding the fraudulent use of the service, GitHub immediately took down the skimmer script.

As Segura further found out, the card skimmer script was uploaded to GitHub by user momo33333 who joined the platform during the same day.


Injected MageCart script

The researcher was able to observe the malicious actor during the process of setting up the scraper script, starting with a couple of tests and ending up with the final obfuscated skimmer payload, ready to be injected into compromised Magento stores.

"Just like with any other kind of third-party plugins, compromised Magento sites are loading this script within their source code, right after the CDATA script and/or right before the tag," says Segura.

At the moment, according to urlscan.io and PublicWWW scans, there are at least a couple of hundred compromised websites with links to GitHub-hosted MageCart skimmer. 


MageCart skimmer hosted on GitHub

Continuation of a campaign from August 2018

As further detailed by security consultant and malware hunter Willem de Groot, this campaign seems to be part of a larger "MagentoCore/MagentoName skimming campaign that infected 7K+ stores last year."
‏
The MagentoCore card scrapping campaign from August 2018 mentioned by de Groot was behind a series of attacks against Magento e-commerce sites that managed to infect around 7,339 Magento stores during a time interval of roughly six months.

As the expert said at the time, "The victim list contains multi-million dollar, publicly traded companies, which suggests the malware operators make a handsome profit. But the real victims are eventually the customers, who have their card and identity stolen."

Compromised websites not yet safe

Segura also notes that despite having their card skimming scripts removed from GitHub, the attackers behind this MageCart campaign can easily inject a new skimmer hosted on servers they control or on other legitimate hosting services. 

"It is critical for e-commerce site owners to keep their CMS and its plugins up-to-date, as well as using secure authentication methods. Over the past year, we have identified thousands of sites that are hacked and posing a risk for online shoppers," states the researcher.

"While payment data is currently the focus, the move to skimming login credentials and other sensitive information has already been seen, which widens the scope of potential Magecart victims far beyond just e-commerce," also said RiskIQ threat researcher Yonathan Klijnsma as part of a MyPillow and Amerisleep breach analysis.

Two days ago, threat actors also abused the GitHub Pages service to host phishing kits on the GitHub platform using free repositories to deliver them to their targets via github.io domains.

Proofpoint's research team which was behind the discovery stated that GitHub took down all the accounts determined to be associated with the malicious activity as of April 19, 2019, while also being "extremely responsive in addressing this abuse of their systems."
