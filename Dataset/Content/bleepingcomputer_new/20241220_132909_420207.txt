
	   


Facebook updated the terms of its bug bounty program for third-party services integrating with the platform to increase the rewards received by researchers.

A year ago, Facebook announced that it would pay researchers that find security issues in third-party apps that exposed Facebook user access tokens, which can be used to log into the account.

Researchers rewarded twice

The scope of the program has expanded and now researchers can also expect a payout from Facebook for security issues discovered through authorized pen-testing in external apps and websites.

Issues discovered through passive observation are still rewarded.


"To be eligible, we ask that researchers comply with the third-party’s vulnerability disclosure or bug bounty program before submitting their findings to Facebook" - Dan Gurfinkel, Security Engineering Manager


The move is designed to improve the privacy and security of Facebook users, even when the flaws are not related to Facebook's code. Another effect may be better engagement with app developers.

"With this expansion, we believe we can not only enhance the security of Facebook users but also the larger app developer ecosystem," Gurfinkel says in a blog post on Tuesday.

No change in payout limits

The amount of a reward is limited by its impact and the terms of the bug bounty program. The minimum payout of $500 still applies for valid reports, but there is no reward ceiling.

Last year, Facebook paid $1.1 million in bounties issued for more than 700 valid reports, rounding the average payout to close to $1,600. In total, the reports received amounted to about 17,800.

2018 was also the year when the company increased the average reward for account takeover bugs to $40,000 if user interaction is not required, and $25,000 if minimum user interaction is necessary.

With this recent modification, Facebook does refresh the incentive for researchers to find bugs and encourages them to also focus on apps and websites that offer smaller rewards through their bug bounty programs.
