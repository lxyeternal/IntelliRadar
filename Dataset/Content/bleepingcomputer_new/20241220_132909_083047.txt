The main worries among 1,250 decision makers on cloud-related security issues interviewed for a study commissioned by Symantec were data breaches and malware injection, yet statistical attack data tells a different story.

Account takeover is an underrated risk

A survey in spring about the main reasons for cross-cloud attacks reveals that a small number of individuals calling the shots on cloud and IT security at companies with at least 250 employees considered takeover attacks an actual risk.

These hazards offer a way to deploy cross-cloud attacks, cloud orchestration strikes, or side-channel attacks, Symantec says.

Of all respondents, only 7% gave account takeover as one of the biggest risks for their company, while Symantec's statistics describe a landscape where 42% of the risky behavior indicated a potential compromise of cloud accounts.

As per data from the security company, "unauthorized access accounts for the bulk of Cloud security incidents (64 percent), which encompasses a variety of both sophisticated and simple exploits."

Not all SaaS apps are business-ready

Among the multiple points of entry an adversary can use to breach a target are software-as-a-service (SaaS) apps, which in many cases lack adequate security features.

Symantec's internal assessment on about 33,000 apps showed that 39% of such apps are not suitable for business use and fewer than 1% came with the necessary defenses an organization would need to minimize the attack surface.

93% of the respondents to the survey said that a big problem for them were users oversharing cloud files with sensitive data. More than half of them pointed to increase usage of cloud apps as a serious issue that needed remediation.

More worryingly, 68% of the respondents said that they saw evidence that their data was being traded on the dark web.



These difficulties, however, can be overcome by enforcing stricter security controls through data loss prevention (DLP) and Cloud Access Security Broker tools.

The cloud infrastructure has become a common target for some time as attackers speculate all angles, including mistakes made when configuring services for different cloud platforms used for reducing the workload.


"Survey respondents say that nearly two-thirds of security incidents under investigation in the last twelve months have occurred at the cloud level, and nearly one-third of all incidents has been classified as cloud-only."


Employing cloud-level security tools like Cloud Workload Protection (CWP) and Cloud Security Posture Management (CSPM) can keep the defenses up against by addressing faulty configurations and running assessments of the cloud infrastructure to determine vulnerabilities and compliance level.

BYOD is still a problem

Inside threats are just as bad as outside ones, with personal gadgets used on enterprise networks creating a risk that can be easily exploited by hackers.


"With employees accessing websites from anywhere and BYOD bringing massive amounts of devices into the enterprise ecosystem, it’s critical to implement total endpoint security to protect devices, apps and networks against malware, ransomware and other emerging threats."


Control points and strict BYOD policies that do not allow external devices that have not been sanctioned to run on the enterprise network should help reduce the exposure.

To keep risks at bay, Symantec advises towards a zero-trust model, where granular protections and controls are implemented at every point of access, be it a mobile device, related to cloud workload, or the corporate network.

Symantec's study focused on cloud security decision-makers in companies located in the US, Canada, Brazil, Mexico, France, Germany, Italy, the UK, Australia, Japan, and Singapore.
