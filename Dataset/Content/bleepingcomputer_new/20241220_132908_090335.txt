
	   


More than a dozen vulnerabilities, collectively named Ripple20, affecting the TCP/IP communication stack used in hundreds of millions of embedded devices paint a grim scenario for connected gadgets.

Some of the flaws are critical and can be exploited to gain remote control of all vulnerable devices on the network. They impact such a wide spectrum of products from so many vendors that it is easier to count those that are not affected.

Affected companies and products

Ripple20 is a collection of 19 vulnerabilities discovered by Israel-based security research group JSOF in the proprietary TCP/IP stack from Treck, a company in Ohio that’s been developing low-level network protocols (ICMPv6/v4, IPv6/v4, TCP, DNS, UDP, ARP, DHCP) for embedded devices since 1997.

Two of them (remote code execution CVE-2020-11896 and CVE-2020-11897) have the highest severity score (10 out of 10) and the other two are rated 9.0 (CVE-2020-11901) and 9.1 (an information leak, CVE-2020-11898).

Altogether, the flaws in the bundle impact products from companies such as Baxter US, Digi International, Hewlett Packard Enterprise, HP, Intel, Maxlinear, Sandia National Labs, HCL Tech. Some, of them (Caterpillar, Rockwell Automation, Schneider Electric, Green Hills, and B.Brown) have already issued security bulletins for the issues.

These are makers of medical devices, printers, uninterruptible power supplies (UPS), construction and mining equipment, and software products. Industrial control systems, enterprise, energy (oil and gas), telecom, retail and commerce are relying on products vulnerable to the Ripple20 bundle.

Determining all the products impacted by Ripple20 is difficult, especially since some of the impacted vendors distribute software based on Treck’s network stack.

It is very likely that these vulnerabilities will haunt the world of embedded devices for a long time and will remain unpatched in some products.

Butterfly effect

The companies mentioned above just those confirmed to have products affected by Ripple20. Treck has notified its customers and provided patches for all the flaws reported by JSOF.

A list of vendors from the U.S. CERT Coordination Center (CERT/CC) shows more than two dozen companies with products that have an “unknown” status for Ripple20 impact.

The Ripple20 vulnerability report from the consultancy firm lists 66 vendors with the vulnerability status pending. Scripts have been developed to identify devices on the network that run Treck’s software library and are available upon request.


“The interesting thing about Ripple20 is the incredible extent of its impact, magnified by the supply chain factor. The wide-spread dissemination of the software library (and its internal vulnerabilities) was a natural consequence of the supply chain “ripple-effect”. A single vulnerable component, though it may be relatively small in and of itself, can ripple outward to impact a wide range of industries, applications, companies, and people” - JSOF


The researchers have also published a technical paper detailing the exploitation of the critical bugs CVE-2020-11896 (remote code execution) and CVE-2020-11898 (information leak). The target is a Digi Connect ME 9210 device - an Ethernet component embedded in various products to enable internet communication.

In a future report, to be released after BlackHat USA (virtual conference) in August, the researchers will provide details for exploiting a DNS vulnerability tracked as CVE-2020-11901 in a UPS device from Schneider Electric.
