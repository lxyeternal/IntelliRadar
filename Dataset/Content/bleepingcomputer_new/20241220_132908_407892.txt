Eric Taylor, known online under the nickname of Cosmo the God, was sentenced on Friday last week to three years probation for a series of hacks the teenager committed in 2011 and 2012.

Taylor was part of the infamous UGNazi (Underground Nazi Hacktivist Group) hacking squad, one of the many hacktivist groups that opposed the CISPA and SOPA bills using DDoS attacks, website defacements, and high-profile data leaks.

The group became popular for leaking data from the Washington Military Department, defacing the UFC website, and DDoS attacks on HostGator, 4Chan, WePay, the CIA, the DOJ, and many others.

Taylor at the heart of the Exposed.su website

The group also hosted a website at exposed.su, where they leaked personal records for Donald Trump, Michelle Obama, Hillary Clinton, Joe Biden, CIA Director John Brennan, Attorney General Eric Holder, former FBI Director Robert Mueller, and high-profile celebrities, such as Mel Gibson, Ashton Kutcher, Jay-Z, Beyonce, Paris Hilton, Britney Spears, Kim Kardashian, and others.

Snapshot of exposed.su website

Following this very public stunt, authorities arrested Taylor in 2012, who was only 15 at the time of his arrest.

Taylor was initially sentenced to two years probation in a juvenile court. He was forced to hand over account logins, and banned from contacting other UGNazi and Anonymous members. He was also banned from using the Internet for anything outside school work.

His trial continued in the following years, and concluded on Friday, when he received a sentence to three years probation, according to a source with knowledge of the yet-to-be-public sentencing hearing, cited by the Washington Times.

Second UGNazi member already sentenced, free

Authorities didn't manage to arrest all UGNazi members, and the group continued to operate during the next two years, until late 2014.

The second UGNazi member authorities arrested was Mir Islam, a 22-year-old man from New York. In 2016, a judge sentenced Islam to two years in prison for cyber-stalking and swatting a young woman, and swatting over 20 other victims, including high-profile figures such as NRA executive Wayne LaPierre, Assistant Attorney Stephen P. Heymann, and infosec reporter Brian Krebs.

Islam is currently out of prison. Taylor was also involved in some of the swatting incidents.
