
	   


With the United States tax season in full swing, attackers are utilizing a variety of realistic methods to lure victims into opening malicious documents or submit sensitive information.

In a new report released today, ProofPoint researchers illustrate how campaigns are targeting the tax season with realistic phishing emails and malicious attachments. These emails are used to install malware on victim's computers or convince them to submit sensitive informaton on servers that are under the attacker's control.

Malicious documents install the Remcos RAT

In January 2019, ProofPoint observed a campaign targeting accounting firms that pretended to be an email from a fictitious taxpayer named Timothy. These emails pretended to contain information tha was requested by the accounting firm in order to prepare Timothy's tax return.

Spam Email

This email contained numerous fake documents such as a W-2 form, a 1099-R from UBS, and a mortgage interest 1098 form. These fake documents are all types of information that a U.S. tax preparer would typically request and indicates that the attackers were well researched with what would normally be required during the U.S. tax season.

The attached documents were further customized with the fake professor's information to make them look more realistic. When the recipient enabled macros, though, the document would download and install the Remcos RAT on the computer.


Fake Tax Form

As these emails were targeting accounting firms, once the RAT was installed, the attackers would potentially have full access to all of the information for tax returns that the accountant is working on. This would allow the attackers to gain access to numerous individuals information by infecting a single computer.

Tax seasons is perfect for phishing scams

Unfortunately, as the tax system increasingly moves online, tax payers have become used to submitting sensitive information to web sites. Phishers take advantage of this by creating realistic web pages that impersonate the IRS in order to collect login and personal information from tax payers.

For example, ProofPoint discovered a phishing campaign that directed users to a fake IRS login page in order to steal login credentials. This page even included a notice at the top about an upcoming system outage, which are commonly shown on government run web sites.



Another campaign discovered by the researchers would direct tax payers to a fake IRS page used to steal information such as identity theft protection pins, contact information, salary, DOB, and social security numbers. 



Take extra precaution during the tax season

While it is always important to be cautious when submitting information online and opening attachments, it is especially important during the tax season.

As tax preparation requires sensitive information that can be used for identity theft, further spear phishing, and the filing of fraudulent tax returns, it is especially important to be careful when submitting this information.

To be safer, it is always advisable to bring tax related documents and information directly to your accountant instead of sending via email. This way you eliminate the potential for attackers gain access to your information and make it so you are not susceptible to phishing scams.

If you are required to submit information online, always go to directly to the site in question rather than clicking on a link in a document or email. Furthermore, be sure to research the address for the page you are on before submitting any information to make sure the site address is correct for the intended entity.
