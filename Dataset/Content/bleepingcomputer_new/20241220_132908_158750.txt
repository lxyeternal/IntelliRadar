While you cannot view the individual files in the archive, you can see what they are named, which can cause recipients to become scared enough to make a payment.

The main goal of a sextortion scam is to scare the recipient of the email into making a payment in order to avoid the embarrassment of an alleged video being sent to their friends and family.

While those of you who read BleepingComputer or are involved in computer security know that these emails are scams, I have received many emails and calls from people who are concerned that these emails may be true. The attackers know this too, so are doing everything they can to push the recipients into being concerned enough to make a payment.

Sextortion email with password protected zip file

Attached to these emails is a password protected zip file that contains numerous files that pretend to be the proof that the hacker has gained access to your computer. Some of the files in the password protected ZIP attachment are named Camera-Vid.avi, contacts.txt, debt.txt, Google_Chrome_Default.txt, information.txt, and screenshot.jpg.


Contents of password protected zip file

These files, though, cannot be accessed unless you purchase the password from them. Enclosed in the spam email will be a link that when clicked on will  bring you to a site called cryptonator.com. This site allows you to purchase the alleged password for the zip file for $50 USD.


Purchase Password 

Even though these emails can be scary to those who receive them, it is important to remember that they are just scams.

They are being sent to many people, they are just trying to scare you into making a payment, and you should just mark them as spam and delete them.
