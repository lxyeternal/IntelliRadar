The spam emails that are being sent as part of this campaign do not particularly stand out in any way. From a sample sent to BleepingComputer by EpicLPer (Stefan Kern), you can see that they come from a user named Gell with the email address info@reeedirect.ru and have a subject consisting of random names as shown below.

If a user clicks on the link, they will first be sent to http://94.250.254.43/, which then sends the user through a series of redirects that eventually land them at an adult site. One of the redirects commonly seen is http://r2.red123.ru/, which contains an interesting hidden link that is titled "follow the white rabbit" as shown below.


Fiddler Traffic

You may recognize the phrase "follow the rabbit" from Lewis Carroll's Alice's Adventures in Wonderland where Alice follows the white rabbit down a hole to begin her journey of self-discovery or from the Matrix where it's used to help Neo find the truth.  In more current times, it's used by the conspiracy theory group QAnon.

This site ultimately redirect the user to a variety of adult sites, including affiliate sites for Ashley Madison as shown below. Notice how the domain  below user the letters r and n together to make it look like an m in the word madison.


AshleyMadison Affiliate Site

The other sites that a visitor is redirected to are fake adult dating sites that try to harvest your contact information to use in future phishing or spam campaigns. Many of these adult dating sites are NSFW and would be problematic if displayed on a work computer.

Below is an example of the tamest one I could find.


Fake Adult Dating Site

Like any email you receive, you should always be wary when opening them. If they look at all suspicious, are from unknown users, or simply contain links to other sites, just delete the email and go on with your day.

Otherwise, you may wind up encountering malware or NSFW sites that could land you in trouble at your job.

Researcher links spam network to 7 IP addresses

Security researcher DanielGallagher who focuses on finding correlations between different types of data researched the IP addresses associated with this spam and came up with some interesting insights.



He further stated that by blocking 7 IP addresses you would be blocking access and redirects to over 4,600 spam and malware related domains. The IP addresses to block are:

34.194.20[.]115
52.211.95[.]198
34.210.90[.]78
52.32.148[.]184
52.27.20[.]17
52.5.47[.]11
52.30.14[.]56

To protect your network, you can add these to your firewall and block lists in order to prevent your users and devices connecting to them.
