
	   


Romanian national Bogdan Viorel Rusu was sentenced on Monday to 65 months in prison for running a multi-state ATM card skimming operation that caused losses close to $1 million.

For over two years between August 3, 2014, until November 14, 2016, Rusu was able to steal payment card information from over 531 people using ATMs in the states of Massachusetts, New York, and New Jersey.

Simple ATM skimming

The data was stolen using skimming devices and pinhole cameras or keypad overlays captured the PIN when customers would use a bank's ATM.

He was arrested in 2016 and charged by complaint and remained in custody since. In September 2018, Rusu pleaded guilty "to conspiracy to commit bank fraud, bank fraud, and aggravated identity theft in connection with a multi-state card skimming scheme."

In a federal court in Springfield, Massachusetts, Rusu received 65 months of jail time in federal prison and five years of supervised release. He is also to pay restitution and forfeiture of $440,130, the Department of Justice informs.

It is unclear what kind of skimmers Rusu and his co-conspirators were using, but it appears that it was the cheaper one, that did not allow sending the captured information over the air as they had to retrieve the devices from the ATMs to get the payment data. Better variants exist and the Russian underground market for these contraptions has offers that accommodate all needs.

Long ATM fraud history

Rusu is not at the first offense of this kind. Originally from Bacau, he was part of a gang that engaged in ATM skimming using skimmers crafted by themselves.

Back in November 2005, when Rusu was about 25 years old, the Romanian police caught him and the leader of the gang (Viorel Chiriac) when they were trying to move from one car to another electronic devices used for ATM fraud.

Romanian press at the time described the two individuals as being at the top of the criminal hierarchy in the city. The equipment confiscated by the police included a pencil with a miniature camera that recorded the card's PIN and sent the video another device.

The intention was to send the electronic pieces to a country in Europe for testing purposes, as this apparently was the beginning of the ATM fraud business.

According to the publication that informed of the arrest, the two also had a ninja sword, a common weapon among Romanian criminals.

Another Romanian newspaper paints a more clear picture of the company Rusu had back home. Along with his partner in crime, Viorel Chiriac, he was involved in ATM fraud since at least 2002.

Although they had a clean wrap home, the two were arrested in the UK for possessing ATM skimming components. Chirica would use a shady company to send the money home as salaries. In a 5-year period, starting April 7, 1999, Chiriac made multiple transactions totaling over 660,000 GBP.

Prosecutors believed at the time that Chiriac had transferred close to $2 million stolen from abroad. The money would be laundered through investments in real estate. Chiriac's gang was arrested in 2006 for money laundering, organized crime, and use of fraudulent payment instruments.
