Netgear has expanded the list of routers vulnerable to a simple yet dangerous exploit that came to light last week, and which is trivial to weaponize and allows attackers to take control over affected devices.

According to the Netgear security team, besides Netgear R6400, R7000, and R8000 router models, which were initially found to be vulnerable, the company has discovered that the exploit also works against other router models. The updated list below:

    R6250
    R6400
    R6700
    R7000
    R7100LG
    R7300
    R7900
    R8000

The company has also released beta firmware versions for R6400, R7000, and R8000, the three router models for which it initially knew the exploit worked.

Netgear says that its engineers are already working on releasing firmware updates for the new router models it found to be vulnerable over the weekend.

A person with the name "AceW0rm" published this exploit online, which prompted the United States Computer Emergency Readiness Team (US-CERT), an organization within the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), to issue a security alert, late Friday afternoon.

US-CERT took the extreme step to recommend that users unplug all vulnerable routers until a firmware update comes out.

The initial alert said that only Netgear R6400 and R7000 models were vulnerable, but users reported on Reddit that Netgear R8000 routers were also affected.

Netgear acknowledged the issue over the weekend, and today expanded the list and issued firmware updates. The company's staff moved rather quickly, knowing that many router and IoT device vendors take months to fix similar issues.

A researcher that goes by the name of Kalypto Pink has published research showing that other Netgear router models may be affected, such as R7000P, R7500, R7800, R8500, and R9000. Netgear has not confirmed his findings.
