"If you pay 10 percent of your current credit balance, the issue will be dropped without further consequences," the hackers wrote in one of their emails. "We would love to remove you from the list. Just pay the demanded amount as soon as possible."

The hackers are obviously acting on the presumption that clients have something to hide and attempting to extort the group of people who used the bank as a secret, off-shore hidden account.

According to German newspaper Bild am Sonntag, the targeted bank is Valartis Bank, who holds €3.3 billion ($3.5 billion) in funds from customers in over 20 countries.

Up to March 2016, the bank was part of Valartis Group AG, which holds banks of the same name in Austria and Switzerland. In March, Valartis Group AG sold the Liechtenstein branch to Citychamp Watch & Jewellery Group Ltd, a Chinese company based in Hong Kong.

Somehow, somewhen, hackers had managed to breach the bank's internal network, and steal a full copy of its database.

Bank admitted getting hacked last week

The hack came to light last week when the hackers started emailing customers with their ransom demands. Following several reports, the bank admitted to suffering a security breach.

Bank officials told German media that that attacker had taken information on payment orders executed via its e-banking system. Officials said all transactions had been carried out before May 2013 by corporate customers.

The hackers gave victims one week to pay the ransom. Valartis Bank customers include many high-profile figures such as politicians, celebrities, and more.
