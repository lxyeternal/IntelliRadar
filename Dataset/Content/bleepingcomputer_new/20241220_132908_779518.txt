
	   


As revealed by Google in its 2018 Google Play Store yearly review, the company has rejected 55% more Android applications than it did in 2017, and also increased the app suspension rate by approximately 66 percent year-over-year.

Andrew Ahn, Product Manager for Google Play, said that "continued efforts to tighten policies to reduce the number of harmful apps on the Play Store, as well as our investments in automated protections and human review processes that play critical roles in identifying and enforcing on bad apps" were behind these YoY rising trends.

Also, according to Ahn the Google Play Store was able to protect Android users from a wide range of new abuse trends, to identify and remove developers of malicious apps a lot faster, and to stop more malicious applications from entering the official Android market than it ever did.

While Ahn's review of 2018 does not provide the exact number of malicious apps removed from the Play Store (or as Google calls them "bad apps"), the 2017 yearly review said that the company "took down more than 700,000 apps that violated the Google Play policies, 70% more than the apps taken down in 2016." Just to put things into perspective.

The Google Play Protect system which went live during July 2017 is "over 50 billion apps on users' devices each day to make sure apps installed on the device aren't behaving in harmful ways."

Unfortunately, at the time it was first deployed, the system designed to detect and block malicious apps from being published on the Play Store was able to detect "only 65.8% of new malware samples and only 79.2% of one-month-old malware."



What's more important though is that, as reported by Ahn, apps installed from the Google Play Store are "eight times less likely to harm a user's device" when compared to applications obtained from unchecked sources.

The Google Play Store 2018 yearly review also pointed out the main areas of focus for 2019, with user privacy protection, developer integrity checks, and fighting harmful applications and behaviors being on the short list.

Android malware protection is still a work in progress

On the user privacy front, Google is planning to continue enforcing a policy it introduced during late 2018 which restricts SMS and Call Log permissions by removing apps who violate it from the store, as well as to put in place "additional policies for device permissions and user data throughout 2019."

The Google Play Store team also stated that their newly improved "clustering and account matching technologies" will allow them to ban developers with very serious policy violations a lot easier.

While Ahn concludes that the Google Play Store team was able to find more malicious apps than ever before with the help of machine learning-based tools and human reviews, he also admits that the fight against bad actors will never end and the only thing to do is to improve detection and blocking mechanisms.

This is definitely true seeing that, multiple Android applications published on the Google Play Store have been found spreading info stealers and pushing malicious ads [1, 2, 3] to hundreds of thousands or tens of millions of users.
