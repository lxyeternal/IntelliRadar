The Lyceum group first came to public attention at the beginning of the month, when ICS security firm Dragos released a brief report on the activity of this new actor, calling it Hexane.

SecureWorks released today its own report on Lyceum, offering details on the tools and tactics used by the group.

Common tactics prove efficient

SecureWorks researchers say that Lyceum relies on password spraying and brute-force attacks to compromise email accounts of individuals working for a targeted organization.

Following the initial stage, the hackers send spear-phishing emails to people in higher positions in the company. The messages carry malicious Excel spreadsheets that install DanBot - a remote access trojan (RAT) with basic capabilities.

Another tool is 'Decrypt-RDCMan.ps1,' a password decryption tool included in the PoshC2 framework for penetration testing. This is used on passwords stored in the configuration file of RDCMan, a remote desktop connection manager.

Lyceum uses a second PowerShell script - 'Get-LAPSP.ps1' - to collect data from the Active Directory via LDAP. This is launched immediately after getting initial access to the target environment.

Apart from using its own toolset, Lyceum does not use any fancy tactics to reach their objective. They rely on macros, social engineering, and security testing frameworks, which is all common. Nevertheless, it is effective in its operations, as it's been running campaigns since April 2018.

Targeting execs, HR, and IT stuff

According to the researchers, Lyceum's targets include executives, human resources and IT staff. Individuals in these positions are the recipients of spear-phishing messages delivered from compromised internal accounts.


"Compromising individual HR accounts could yield information and account access that could be used in additional spearphishing operations within the targeted environment and against associated organizations. IT personnel have access to high-privilege accounts and documentation that could help the threat actors understand the environment without blindly navigating the network to find data and systems of interest."


It appears that industrial control systems (ICS) and operational technology (OT) staff are not among the intended targets of this group, although the researchers cannot exclude the possibility "that the threat actors could seek access to OT environments after establishing robust access to the IT environment."
