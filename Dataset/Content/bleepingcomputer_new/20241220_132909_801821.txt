
	   


Cybercriminals are exploring all avenues to get the most out of stolen payment card information. Access to an online banking account opens the door to a new revenue stream, and services providing it have grown more attractive.

As banks and financial services came up with mechanisms that combat unauthorized transactions, cybercriminals developed their own card enrollment services that put them in control of a victim's banking account.

Crooks turn to paid and public services to collect the latest information on card holders and use it to create online banking accounts. If an account  already exists, they use the details to take control of the account.

This level of access allows fraudsters to modify the personal account holder information and contact details (email, phone) so cybercriminals have complete control of the account, ATM extraction limits, and the notifications that may alert the victim of unauthorized activity.

There's a variety of benefits stemming from these changes, and they all lead to a larger profit. One is bypassing 3D Secure checks (used by Visa, MasterCard, American Express) for online purchases.

Some merchants check that the billing and shipping addresses are one and the same as this is one marker that could be used to detect fraudulent transactions; so cybercriminals make sure that the two addresses are for the pickup location for the goods they bought.



Card enrollment services have existed since at least 2007 but gained in popularity over the past couple of years, according to security researchers monitoring the Russian-speaking underground forums.

Yelisey Boguslavskiy, director of security research at Advanced Intelligence (AdvIntel) says in a report today that there are several notable enrollment services operating on multiple underground forums.

Manuals that describe how such an enroll service works are also circulating on clearnet and messaging platforms. Most of them have are rely on details available in carding training sessions on underground forums.

"For instance, in October 2018, a Russian-speaking threat actor “LaChe” who leverages an entry-level carding forum tenebris[.]cc to promote their carding training materials, developed a 3-stage guide on enroll, adding a discussion of potential issues with anti-fraud checks to the WWH-CLUB training," informs the report from AdvIntel.

A card enrolling process starts from the personally identifiable information associated with a card. The data can be obtained from card skimming attacks that make use of a malicious script added to e-commerce websites specifically to capture payment and customer info.

The typical details include card number, expiration date, and CVV, along with card holder's first and last name, and address. From this, the card fraudulent enrollment service can dig deeper on the web for more details.

The details used are all legitimate and fraudsters use them on legitimate services just like the true owner would.


Legitimate bank service leveraged by cybercriminals

According to AdvIntel's findings in training manuals, "comprehensive personal information should be obtained through underground checking services, often charging $6-7 USD per check;" these services may also provide social security numbers, dates of birth, and updated home address info.

To make it all look legitimate, card enrolling service operators rely on remote desktop servers and SSH tunnels to create a fingerprint for the connection so that the request does not raise any suspicions to the bank. Once access to the online account is obtained, they sell it to a customer on the underground forum.

The researchers found an article published to a Telegram channel about card enroll services saying that "all commercial card enroll services will always provide their customers with access to the SSH tunnel or remote desktop server (RDP), through which the card was initially enrolled. This creates consistency between digital fingerprints of merchant initially accessing the bank for enrollment and its customer accessing the online banking."

AdvIntel says that their observations indicate that cybercriminals are interested in customizing enroll solutions and increase the amount of cards they can offer for sale.

"This may include creating a list of “golden” BINs for specific banks, finding glitches in online registration systems for a specific institution, or developing a targeted approach for each bank," the researchers say.
