
	   


French security researcher Robert Baptiste found the API of the 63Red Safe mobile application known as "Yelp for conservatives" wide open, with no authentication needed to access and view the data stored within the app's database.

According to its official description, 63Red Safe is an iOS and Android mobile app designed for "keeping conservatives safe," and to help its users "Find great restaurants nearby, and see how expensive, how far away, and best of all, whether they are safe for conservatives."

To be more specific, the 63Red Safe app will allow its users to know beforehand if it's safe to wear Make America Great Again (MAGA) apparel when dining at a particular restaurant or when shopping at various locations.

As Baptiste told BleepingComputer in an interview, 63Red Safe's API endpoints needed no API key needed and there were zero authentication mechanisms, which, basically, allowed anyone to "block/unblock everybody," get all the user details, and even create new profiles. 

Furthermore, the developer of the app hardcoded his credentials and left the entire list of API endpoints in the app's source code, making it possible for anyone who knows what they're doing to access and view any data stored within the app's database.

Baptiste also said that one could access every profile's id, profile creation date, update date, username, ban status, hotscore, profile picture, following count, followers count, uid, and email address.



While the researcher stated that he did not attempt to extract data from the database, he did manage to find out that 4466 individuals already created profiles and were using the 63Red Safe app on their Android or iOS devices.

As detailed in the tweet that started the whole thread in which Baptiste exposed 63Red Safe's lack of security, the researcher was also able to find the entire list of users with names, photos, personal messages, and security tokens of the Donald Daters dating app for Trump supporters in "less than 5 minutes."

The researcher was also the one who discovered a hidden web server always running in the background in the ES File Explorer Android file manager with over 100,000,000 installs on its Google Play store page.

Following Baptiste's reports, 63red issued an official statement saying that they "take security very seriously, and have already taken action to additionally protect our data."



The 63red team also said that:

We see this person’s illegal and failed attempts to access our database servers as a politically-motivated attack, and will be reporting it to the FBI later today. We hope that, just as in the case of many other politically-motivated internet attacks, this perpetrator will be brought to justice, and we will pursue this matter, and all other attacks, failed or otherwise, to the utmost extent of the law. We log all activity against all our servers, and will present those logs as evidence of a crime.
