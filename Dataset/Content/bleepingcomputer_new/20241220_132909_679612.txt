The FTC says that the three companies controlled by Thomas Henry Fred, Jr., were behind a large scale campaign that "sent thousands of businesses mailers designed to look like invoices from a government agency for labor law posters."

While the settlement requires the defendants to pay over $8 million, these judgments are suspended because of an inability to pay and the offenders will pay only $1.2 million to account for their targets' losses.

The government imposter scheme

"Defendants have generated more than $800,000 from in excess of 9,000 consumers through their scheme," says the FTC complaint filed in October 2018.

The letters delivered to the victims' snail mail inboxes titled 'LABOR LAW COMPLIANCE REQUEST FORM' were used to threaten them that 'Failure to comply with posting regulations can lead to fines of up to $17,000' the complaint also adds.

To further instill a sense of urgency with their fictitious direct mail scam messages, the defendants included response deadlines as well as 'Business ID' numbers, directing their potential victims to send over $84 as money orders or checks, as well as confirm company info currently on file.

The problem is that these posters are available for free from government agencies.

"Just because an invoice looks official doesn’t mean it is," Director of the FTC’s Bureau of Consumer Protection Andrew Smith stated.

"If you get an official-looking bill that you don’t understand, call the government agency directly using the number you find online or in a local directory, not the one on the mailer."

Previous FTC settlements in 2019

In July 2019, Facebook also agreed to pay a staggering $5 billion penalty as part of a settlement agreement with the FTC after the investigation launched following the Cambridge Analytica events concluded.

That was the largest ever consumer privacy violation penalty paid by a company and one the largest ever imposed by the U.S. Government for any type of violation.

Also in July, Taiwanese networking equipment manufacturer D-Link settled a lawsuit that followed an FTC complaint from 2017 alleging that it failed to properly secure its Internet-connected cameras and wireless routers.

Office Depot and Support.com, Inc, a tech support software provided from California, agreed to pay $25 million and $10 million respectively in March for supposedly tricking customers into paying for millions of US dollars worth of computer repair services backed by fake malware scans.

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) also issued a warning regarding an IRS impersonation scam campaign targeting taxpayers and tax professionals during August with the end goal of infecting their computers with malware.
