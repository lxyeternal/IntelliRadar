
	   


Ten members of the GozNym cybercriminal group which used the Avalanche malware distribution network to launch malware attacks against businesses and financial institutions were indicted today for computer fraud conspiracy, wire and bank fraud conspiracy, and money laundering.

"The Avalanche network provided services to more than 200 cybercriminals, and hosted more than twenty different malware campaigns, including GozNym," says the Europol.

According to Europol's press release, the GozNym group were charged for attempting to "steal an estimated $100 million from more than 41 000 victims, primarily businesses and their financial institutions."

Map of defendants

Out of the ten defendants, five have already been arrested while the five Russian nationals charged in the indictment — including the GozNym malware developer — are on the run. 

The FBI says that the five Russian who haven't yet been apprehended "allegedly conspired to infect victims’ computers with GozNym malware designed to capture victims’ online banking login credentials; use the captured login credentials to fraudulently gain unauthorized access to victims’ online bank accounts; and steal money from victims’ bank accounts and launder those funds using United States and foreign beneficiary bank accounts controlled by the conspirators."

The GozNym criminal network's leader together with his technical assistant are being prosecuted in Georgia by the Ministry of Internal Affairs of Georgia and the Prosecutor’s Office of Georgia.



The GozNym malware

The malware used by the defendants in the attacks is a "Trojan hybrid spawned from the Nymaim and Gozi ISFB malware," a malware strain used "in attacks against more than 24 U.S. and Canadian banks" as detailed by IBM X-Force Research back in April 2016.

GozNym was dropped on the targets' machines via large scale malspam campaigns which targeted hundreds of thousands of individuals and companies, and it was used by the cybercrime group to steal banking credentials from their victims' compromised computers.

The spam emails were designed to look like legitimate business emails which contained either a malicious attachment or a malicious link that would redirect the victims to domains controlled by the attackers and configured to download the GozNym malware on their computers.

The malicious domains and the GozNym banking Trojan were hosted on the infrastructure of the Avalanche malware distribution network which was dismantled during 2016 when law enforcement seized, sinkholed, and blocked more than 800,000 domains spread over 60 registrars.

Avalanche malware distribution network

While it was functional, the Avalanche network was used to host the following malware families according to a US-CERT alert from December 2016:


Windows-encryption Trojan horse (WVT) (aka Matsnu, Injector,Rannoh,Ransomlock.P)
	URLzone (aka Bebloh)
	Citadel
	VM-ZeuS (aka KINS)
	Bugat (aka Feodo, Geodo, Cridex, Dridex, Emotet)
	newGOZ (aka GameOverZeuS)
	Tinba (aka TinyBanker)
	Nymaim/GozNym
	Vawtrak (aka Neverquest)
	Marcher
	Pandabanker
	Ranbyus
	Smart App
	TeslaCrypt
	Trusteer App
	Xswkit


The network was also used as a "fast flux botnet," providing communication infrastructure for a number of other botnets including:


TeslaCrypt
	Nymaim
	Corebot
	GetTiny
	Matsnu
	Rovnix
	Urlzone
	QakBot (aka Qbot, PinkSlip Bot)


The GozNym criminal network's leader (a Georgian citizen from Tbilisi) was the one who leased access to the malware used in the attacks that led to financial losses of roughly $100 million, and he also recruited other cybercriminals based on their skills and services advertised on Russian-speaking criminal forums.

The "cybercrime as a service" group provided various criminal services, ranging from "bulletproof hosters, money mules networks, crypters, spammers, coders, organizers, and technical support," says the Europol.



The indictment of the ten members of the GozNym cybercrime group is the result of the international law enforcement cooperation between "EU Member States (Bulgaria and Germany) as well as Georgia, Moldova, Ukraine and the United States (in alphabetical order)."

The investigation also received extensive support from the "Europol, the European Agency for Law Enforcement Cooperation as well as Eurojust, the European Union's Judicial Cooperation Unit."
