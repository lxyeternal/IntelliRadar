
	   


An advanced breed of information stealer called Baldr is being peddled on cybercrime forums. It appears that it is in the running for the long haul as it relies on short development cycles to iron out the wrinkles and get new capabilities.

Baldr is sold for $150. It does not have persistence and it is built to exfiltrate sensitive data from key locations on the infected computer. Its targets include browser profiles, cryptocurrency wallets, records from VPN clients, FTP programs, text documents, and Telegram sessions.

Baldr peddlers committed to the game

The malware was spotted towards the end of 2018 and it does not persist on the computer, which makes it almost invisible to the victim, so it lifts all the information in one go.

According to a post from Overdot on a closed-registration forum, LordOdin collaborates with the developers of another info-stealing malware called Arkei, helping each other, but the two are separate projects.

Malware researchers at Malwarebytes say that everything about Baldr suggests commitment from its authors. They describe it as being "skillfully crafted" for long term business.

"Whether we are talking about its packer usage and payload code structure or even its backend C2 and distribution, the authors spent a lot of time to try and make this a long-running campaign," they write in a techincal analysis shared with BleeepingComputer.

Baldr can look for regular SQL databases as well as custom formats and taps into the following locations and application data folders to steal information:


AppData\Local\Google\Chrome\User Data\Default
AppData\Local\Google\Chrome\User Data\Default\Login Data
AppData\Local\Google\Chrome\User Data\Default\Cookies
AppData\Local\Google\Chrome\User Data\Default\Web Data
AppData\Local\Google\Chrome\User Data\Default\History
AppData\Roaming\Exodus\exodus.wallet
AppData\Roaming\Ethereum\keystore
AppData\Local\ProtonVPN
Wallets\Jaxx
Liberty\
NordVPN\
Telegram
Jabber
TotalCommander
Ghisler


The malware also searches for document files (DOC, DOCZ, LOG andTXT) in default locations and takes all the data in them.

During the exfiltration stage it seems that there is no effort to disguise or hide the process. The researchers noticed that no matter the number of files, they are all sent in "one large and obvious network transfer."

Baldr is non-persistent and does not have any spreading mechanism, so it targets every victim individually, without trying a re-infection.

Customers of the malware manage all the information via an administration panel, where they get details about the harvest, operating systems infected, and the geographical location.



According to Malwarebytes researchers (William Tsing, Vasilios Hioureas and Jérôme Segura) that tracked the activity of Baldr, the malware is a solid stealer actively pushed by its author and distributor.
