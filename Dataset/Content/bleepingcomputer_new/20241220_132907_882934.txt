
	   


Microsoft's development team is currently working on adding recommended security profiles for Exchange Online Protection (EOP) and Office 365 Advanced Threat Protection (ATP) later this month.

"Although we empower security administrators to customize their security settings, there are two security levels in Exchange Online Protection and Office 365 Advanced Threat Protection that we recommend: Standard and Strict," says Redmond on the Microsoft 365 roadmap entry.

These profiles will allow EOP admins to easily configure their users' accounts with Microsoft recommended security settings and fend off spam email reaching their inboxes.

Recommended profiles enabled automatically for some customers

Microsoft also says that profiles will automatically be switched in situations where users have chosen to have their settings automatically managed. 

"We will be automatically updating profiles for our smaller customers who want us to manage these settings for them, but for others, we want to enable the admins to easily configure these for their users," Redmond says.

The company also provides detailed information on the EOP and Office 365 ATP Standard and Strict security levels admins can set up for defined sets of users on the Microsoft 365 security documentation website.

Admins can also use Windows PowerShell to apply EOP configuration settings to multiple tenants using scripts or cmdlets until the new recommended profiles will be generally available later this month.

Other Office 365 security-focused changes

Microsoft enabled Authenticated Received Chain (ARC) for all for Office 365 hosted mailboxes to check authentication results within Office 365 and to improve anti-spoofing detection.

Microsoft Office 365 admins and users were also urged not to bypass the built-in spam filters in a support document that also comes with guidelines for cases when it can't be avoided.

In November, Microsoft announced the release of a new Office 365 ATP feature dubbed enhanced compromise detection and response and designed to help detect breaches.

This new Office 365 ATP capability also enables Security Operations (SecOps) teams to remediate hacked accounts, as well as automatically detect and investigate suspicious users.

The hacked account remediation feature was included in September with the addition of a new Automated Incident Response playbook that triggers "automatic investigations for users with abnormal sending patterns or users restricted from sending emails."
