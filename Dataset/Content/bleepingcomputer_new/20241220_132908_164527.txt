The Federal Bureau of Investigation is warning of online romance scams, an ongoing online fraud trend that can lead to large financial losses, as well as devastating emotional scars.

The scammers behind this type of fraud are using fake online identities to gain their victims' trust on social media and dating websites.

$475 million lost to romance scams in 2019

The 2019 Internet Crime Report published by FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) says that romance scams (also known as confidence fraud) are behind higher financial damages when compared to other reported online crimes.

"In 2019, almost 20,000 complaints categorized as romance scams were reported to IC3 (about 1,000 more than the previous year), and the losses associated with those complaints exceeded $475 million," the FBI said.

The amount of financial losses is drastically increasing each year as shown by victims' reports, with losses of more than $211 million in 2017 and more than $362 million in 2018.

The only other type of cybercrime with a higher return on investment for threat actors is Business Email Compromise (BEC) which was the cause of almost $1.8 billion in losses according to 23,775 recorded complaints received by IC3.


Image: FBI

Last year, the FBI also warned of romance scam victims facing the danger of being recruited as money mules and persuaded to transfer money illegally on the scammers' behalf.

"Acting as a money mule—allowing others to use your bank account, or conducting financial transactions on behalf of others—not only jeopardizes your financial security and compromises your personally identifiable information, but is also a crime," the FBI said in May.

The U.S. Department of Justice's website lists hundreds of cases where scammers were indicted or found guilty of running large-scale romance scam fraud schemes targeting U.S. citizens.

How to protect yourself against scammers

Even though the actors behind romance scams are experts at social engineering and at making sure that they don't fail to reach their goal, there are signs you can watch for to identify such scam attempts.

According to the FBI's warning, you should watch out for any of the following indicators that could reveal that you're targeted by a scammer:

• Research the person’s photo and profile using online searches to see if the material has been used elsewhere.
• Go slow and ask questions.
• Beware if the individual seems too perfect or quickly asks you to leave a dating service or social media site to go “offline.”
• Beware if the individual attempts to isolate you from friends and family or requests.
• Beware if the individual promises to meet in person, but then always comes up with an excuse why he or she can’t. If you haven’t met the person after a few months, for whatever reason, you have good reason to be suspicious.
• Never send money to anyone you don’t know personally.

If you have fallen victim to a romance scam, the FBI recommends stopping all contact immediately and filing a complaint with the IC3 at www.ic3.gov.

Last but not least, you should also reach out to your financial institution to see if financial transactions you might have made after the scammer contacted you can be stopped or reversed.
