
	   


Apex Legends fans who want to play the game on mobile devices are being actively targeted by scam and malware campaigns which promise to deliver a playable version of the game ready to install on iOS and Android devices.

For those who aren't yet up to speed, Apex Legends is a free to play battle royale game set in the Titanfall universe (without titans), a game developed by Respawn Entertainment and published by Electronic Arts on the Windows, PlayStation 4, and Xbox One platforms.

As discovered by ESET's malware researcher Lukas Stefanko, a fake Apex Legends application for Android is currently being peddled by multiple channels on YouTube in the form of an APK download link which has been clicked roughly 100,000 during the past five days.

In light of these facts, it's not surprising that the link pointing to that fake Apex Legends for Android APK has amassed 100K clicks in under a week.



On the other hand, Phishing AI, a Twitter account powered by cybersecurity company Lookout, spotted dozens of domains used to run phishing campaigns by pushing apex coin scams, fake aimbots and a variety of other game cheats, and even a free Apex Legends download (a game which is free-to-play in the first place).

One of the malicious websites unearthed by Phishing AI is also used to push fake Flash updaters, a technique used by threat actors for some time a now to peddle all sorts of malware, from Trojans and backdoors to less dangerous but highly annoying adware.



Another malicious domain targeting Apex Legends fans attempts to trick them into installing the Net Safety by Safely Chrome extension developed by safelyprotection.online, which seems to be the clone of apparently legitimate Safe Browsing by Safely offered by safelyonline.net.



Phishing AI also assembled and shared a collection of 39 domains used by scammers to host scam websites that target Apex Legends players who look for apex coins offers online.



Scam promises Apex Legends mobile version

BleepingComputer looked into one of the scams going around on YouTube and being promoted through multiple channels and videos, with some of the videos having above 240K views at the time of this publication.

These scams redirect viewers to the almobile.pw or getapex.mobi domains which ask users what mobile version of Apex Legends they need, redirecting the users straight to a set of offers hosted HERE and HERE (for the second variant) that will show them a video of Apex Legends gameplay when successfully completed.


YouTube Apex Legends Mobile Scam Videos

If the users go for the Android platform, the scam websites will start an APK download which, after being installed, will open the offer domains mentioned above and will show the same gameplay video after the offers are successfully completed.

Basically, while no malware is involved in this case, the scammers will trick the Apex Legends fans to complete two of the offers by marking them as "Human Verification" and promising that "Unlocking will allow access to download "APEX LEGENDS Mobile", allowing them to collect the commissions.

Gamers targeted whenever their games are getting attention

This is not the first or the last time that scammers will jump at the opportunity to take advantage of gamers who want to play their favorite games on their smartphone or tablet.

Back in May 2018, a group of 15 individuals was arrested in China after they developed, promoted, and sold game-cheating apps for the PUBG (PlayerUnknown's BattleGrounds) game riddled with the Huigezi backdoor Trojan.

During March 2018, after the team behind Fortnite announced that they will start pushing out invites for the Fortnite for iOS release, social media platforms started being flooded with offers promoting fake invites that could be bought with follows, retweets, paypal payments, or PSN and Amazon gift certificates.

Further back, during the summer of 2016, a World of Warcraft scam used social engineering to try and trick victims into entering a special command, allowing the attackers to take control of their victims' in-game WOW interface, make unauthorized trades, and find the victims' location anywhere in the game.
