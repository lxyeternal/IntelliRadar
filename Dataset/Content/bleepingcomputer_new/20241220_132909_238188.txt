
	   


Although Coinhive shut down and its cryptominer dropped down on the sixth place in Check Point’s latest Global Threat Index, coinminers continue to lead the pack with Cryptoloot, XMRig, and Jsecoin taking the first, third, and fifth place.

After the Coinhive cryptomining service which offered web devs a JavaScript-based Monero miner ceased all operations on March 8 and rendered its coinmining script useless, its place was quickly taken by its direct competitor, the CPU/GPU-based Cryptoloot Javascript web miner for Monero.

This wasn't at all surprising given that Cryptoloot was already on the heels of Coinhive when Check Point published its February 2019 Global Threat Index and another criptominer was the most logical choice to quickly fill the gap in ongoing cryptojacking campaigns.

Cryptojacking moving to the cloud

Also, Horowitz stated that "cybercriminals will find ways to earn from more robust cryptomining activities, such as mining on Cloud environments, where the built-in auto-scaling feature allows the creation of a larger haul of cryptocurrency."

This is definitely a wake-up call given that Check Point's researchers already encountered companies that were asked to pay bills of hundreds of thousands of dollars after their cloud environments were breached and were used in cryptojacking campaigns.

		The mobile malware rankings see only a switch between the first and second place, with the Hiddad Android malware used to deliver ads on compromised devices and steal sensitive info replacing the Lotoor hacking tool designed to mobile devices.

The third place, just like last month, is taken by the Triada Android modular backdoor which allows attackers to "super user privileges to downloaded malware, as helps it to get embedded into system processes. Triada has also been seen spoofing URLs loaded in the browser."

Check Point also examined the most exploited security vulnerabilities during March 2019, arriving at the conclusion that the CVE-2017-7269 (Microsoft IIS WebDAV ScStoragePathFromUrl Buffer Overflow) was the most popular one with a 44% overall impact.

The two other security flaws filling up the rest of the spots in the Top 3 "Most Exploited" vulnerabilities of last month are the Web Server Exposed Git Repository Information Disclosure (CPAI-2015-0991) and the OpenSSL TLS DTLS Heartbeat Information Disclosure (CVE-2014-0160; CVE-2014-0346).

 


March 2019 global risk index

As proof of cryptojacking still being an essential tool in the arsenal of malicious actors, a set of eight apps were found in the Microsoft Store  dropping malicious Monero cryptomining scripts on their users' computers, as well as by hundreds of exposed and vulnerable Docker hosts being actively abused by cryptojackers.

A new malicious coin miner strain using the XMR-Stak Cryptonight cryptocurrency miner and a Backdoor Trojan dubbed SpeakUp used by threat actors to infect their targets with XMRig miners were observed as part of a campaign that targeted servers running multiple Linux distributions during February 2019.

During March, a modular malware with worm capabilities discovered by 360 Total Security's research team exploited known vulnerabilities in ElasticSearch, Hadoop, Redis, Spring, Weblogic, ThinkPHP, and SqlServer servers, spreading between servers to mine for Monero (XMR).
