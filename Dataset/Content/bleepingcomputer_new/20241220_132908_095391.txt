According the Keen Security Lab researchers, they analyzed Tesla cars for several months and discovered multiple vulnerabilities, including a remote attack vulnerability that worked on a Tesla Model S in both Parking and Driving Mode. These vulnerabilities allow them to remotely perform actions that include unlocking doors, bringing the car to a full stop, turning on wind shield wipers, and moving seats. Even scarier is that they were able to do some of these activities, such as braking the car, from 12 miles away. 



Keen Security followed responsible disclosure and disclosed these vulnerabilities to Tesla. Working with Tesla Security Team lead Chris Evans,  they were able to fix all of the vulnerabilities in a new firmware release that is now available.
