
	   


Several vulnerabilities impacting twenty-nine models of GPS trackers designed to monitor the location of children, pets, and valuable possessions expose real-time location data as Avast Threat Labs researchers found.

The security flaws were found in GPS location trackers manufactured by the Shenzhen i365 Tech, with over 600,000 users from countries all over the world and resold under multiple brands on various e-commerce platforms like Amazon or eBay.

While these trackers are being marketed to keep one's kids or pets safe, the flaws they come with are exposing all the data they collect and send to the cloud.

Impacted GPS tracker models

Vulnerabilities discovered in the GPS trackers

The security issues were initially discovered after the Avast Threat Labs researchers took a closer look at the T8 Mini GPS tracker. The first issue they found was the exposure of users' information caused by the mobile app used with the tracker being delivered via an unsecured website.

All the communication with the trackers' online portal is unencrypted, with the data being sent in plain text over HTTP and exposing it to interception attacks.

"Further security issues involved user account information, which comes with an assigned ID number and default password of 123456," Avast found.

To make things even worse, "Design flaws in the trackers can also enable third-parties to “spoof” (or fake) the user’s location, or access the microphone for eavesdropping."

Given that the default password is used by all the trackers and the username they use is each device's serial number, one can easily increment it and easily access all the tracker accounts. 



Some GPS trackers are jam-packed with security flaws 

Martin Hron, senior researcher at Avast, the one who led the team behind this research, encourages all consumers to choose GPS trackers that come with built-in security into the product's design.

For those who already bought one of these vulnerable trackers, Avast suggests changing the default user password although, even in this case, the unencrypted data the device sends to the cloud can still be intercepted.

Although the security vulnerabilities were found in the devices and mobile apps of a single manufacturer, the Avast researchers added that "fifty mobile applications on both Google Play and iOS App Store share the same unencrypted platform."

"We have done our due diligence in disclosing these vulnerabilities to the manufacturer, but since we have not heard back after the standard window of time, we are now issuing this Public Service Announcement to consumers and strongly advise you to discontinue use of these devices,” Hron added.

More details on how the vulnerabilities were found, as well as the exact number of impacted devices for each of the vulnerable models and the possible attack vectors are available in an in-depth write-up on Avast's Threat Labs blog.


Map with last GPS position of trackers

This is not the first time children's location has been exposed by buggy GPS trackers, with researchers having discovered just last month that LeapFrog's LeapPad tablets for children could be used by hackers to easily locate and interact with the kids using them, as well as phish their parents for sensitive info.

Back in 2017, the European Consumer Organisation (BEUC) also issued a public service announcement warning that most children's GPS-tracking smartwatches are stuffed to the brim with various security flaws exposing their location or allowing potential attackers to take control of the devices.
