
	   


A new twist in the scam extortion emails is a new email campaign originating from Russia that contains a bomb threat stating they will detonate a bomb if the recipient does not send a $20,000 USD bitcoin payment.

There are reports of these emails being received throughout the U.S.A and contain subject lines similar to "My device is inside your building", "No need to be heroic", or "Rescue service will complicate the situation".

These emails then proceed to state that a mercenary created an explosive device made of "lead azide", "trinitrotoluene" [sic], or some other material and has placed it in the recipient's building. It goes on to further state that if the recipient does not pay a $20,000 USD bitcoin payment, the device will be detonated by the end of the day.

Example Bomb Threat Scam Email

These emails start with a threat similar to the following text. The full text of one these scam emails can be found below.

I write you to inform you that my man has hidden the bomb (tronitrotoluene) in the building where your company is located. My mercenary built a bomb according to my instructions. It is compact and it is hidden very carefully, it is impossible to destroy the structure of the building by my bomb, but in the case of its detonation there will be many wounded people.

My mercenary is controlling the situation around the building. If he sees any strange activity or cops he will blow up the device.

I can call off my mercenary if you make a transfer. You pay me 20'000 usd in Bitcoin and explosive will not detonate, but do not try to fool me -I assure you that I have to call off my recruited person solely after 3 confirmations in blockchain. 

A security researcher named Defender on Twitter told BleepingComputer that they have currently captured over 15,000 samples of these scam emails since approximately 12:48 PM EST, but have since started to slow down. Defender further told us that these emails are originating from Russia with the majority of them coming from the 194.58.x.x IP range and some from 193.124.x.x. More specifically, the IP addresses 193.124.44.210, 194.58.38.102, and 194.58.103.231.

Defender has also told us that these emails are not only being sent to the U.S.A, but have also been sent to recipients in Canada, UK, NL, Switzerland, and Sweden

These scam campaigns are using numerous bitcoin addresses and the ones shared with BleepingComputer have only received one bitcoin payment of $0.18. 


Payment to bitcoin address associated with email

While these are just another variant of the scam email campaigns that have been going around lately, such as the sextortion email campaigns, due to their threat they need to be taken seriously. The New York Police Department has already started to investigate these threats and will investigate each call they receive. At this time they have found the threats to be not credible so far.



While the threats in sextortion emails to release a video of of the recipient using a porn site can be scary and humiliating, bomb threats have a high a much higher cost. Law enforcement has to investigate each report, businesses have to shut down during the investigation, and threats of physical harm can cause extreme stress for the recipient, their coworkers, family, and friends. Due to this, expect to see a large law enforcement investigation started to track down the senders.

This is not lost on the scammers as they have been ending their scam emails with the following statement.

If an explosion occurred and the authorities notice this message:
We arent the terrorist organization and dont take any responsibility for explosions in other places.


It important to remember that these types of email scams are created to instill fear through extreme threats so that the recipient sends them the payment demand.  With that said, due to the recent bombs sent to CNN and various political figures, if you receive an email containing a bomb threat you should immediately report it to the authorities to be safe.

This is a developing story.
