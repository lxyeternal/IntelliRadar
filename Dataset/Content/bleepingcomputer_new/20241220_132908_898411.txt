
	   


The 3Fun dating mobile app for "curious couples & singles" exposed the precise location of its users along with personal details like date of birth and pictures that should have been protected by built-in privacy settings.

Geo-location details in the form of latitude and longitude were available with high precision, pinpointing the user wherever they happened to be at the time of using the app.

The app collects real-time location data from its users and this can be shown to potential matches, if the user so desires. There is also the option to hide this detail, but this block is just at application level, as it still reaches the 3Fun server.

In a blog post today, researchers at Pen Test Partners note that because filtering of the data was limited to the mobile app, they could query the 3Fun server for the location of other users.

Requests made this way revealed 3Fun users spread across the UK. The level of detail is impressive as users could be tracked to a house or a building.



The researchers found some three-way loving individuals in the White House, 10 Downing Street, and in the building of the US Supreme Court. 

But the app does not encrypt traffic from the app to its server, so this information can be easily spoofed with software that allows intercepting and modifying the data.

Additional details that can be gleaned from the 3Fun server includes birth dates, gender, and sexual orientation.



"This data can be used to stalk users in near real-time, expose their private activities and worse," says Alex Lomas of Pen Test Partners.

However serious this may be, leaking private photos added to a private album is more worrisome. According to the description of the app, these images are available only to the user's matches.

Yet, they are sitting unprotected and anyone with a link to them can access and copy them; and finding the path is disclosed in the API responses.


"We think there are a whole heap of other vulnerabilities, based on the code in the mobile app and the API, but we can’t verify them."


3Fun claims over 1.5 million users, although we could not find statistics to support the information. On Android, the app has over 100,000 installations and a total of 9,260 reviews. On iOS there are about 25,700 ratings.

Pen Test Partners informed the developer of their findings on July 1 and a fix came "fairly quickly," says Lomas. The latest update for the Android app is from July 21, while the newest version for iOS is from July 8.
