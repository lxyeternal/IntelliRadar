
	   


The number of stolen payment card details collected from South Korea has soared over the past two months more than one million records have been offered for sale on the dark web.

Drastic increase of South Korean card records

In June, researchers noticed 230,000 records peddled around, an abnormal increase from the 42,000 seen in May, which was in line with the normal amount observed. The next month, 890,000 more records became available.

The origin of the compromise remains unknown at this time, but the data is from card-present transactions at numerous businesses in the country.

Demand almost doubles the price

Researchers at Gemini Advisory monitoring card-related activities on cybercriminal forums say that South Korean payment records were in low demand last year as the crooks had a large supply available. This changed in 2019 when the supply remained the same but demand increased.

The current context also caused the average prices for a card to move up. Last year, South Korean card-present records sold for a median $24.

Most likely in an attempt to capitalize on the growing demand this year, the records from one payment card became more expensive averaging at $40.

US cardholders also affected

Another observation from the researchers is that 3.7% of the compromised South Korean records were from US-issued cards.


"One of the most affected US financial institutions was a credit union that primarily serves the US Air Force; the Air Force maintains multiple air bases in South Korea." - Gemini Advisory


A closer look at the compromised data revealed that many of the cards belonged to US owners that were visiting South Korea. 



Stealing payment details from card-present transactions is typically done through planting malware on systems that connect to PoS devices. In many incidents, the attack vector was a remote desktop connection protected by default or easy-to-guess password.

The malware installed this way can then copy the payment data as it gets stored in RAM (memory scraping) when the customer uses the card to make a purchase. This is possible because the card information is not encrypted in RAM.
