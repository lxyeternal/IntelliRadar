The unnamed marketplace's frontpage (now redirecting to the Dutch Police site on the Tor network) is plastered with three columns listing active vendors, arrested vendors, and identified buyers, all tracked by Dutch authorities.

The message continues:

If you are a resident of the Netherlands and you bought a small user’s quantity of drugs, no legal action will be taken right now. If you bought larger quantities of drugs, or took up other goods or services, then it is possible an investigation will be initiated. Vendors of illicit goods and services through Dark Markets are actively tracked down and prosecuted. Under the heading "Arrested” you’ll find a number of examples.

That's one hell of a way of deterring Tor newcomers from using Dark Web marketplaces ever again.

Marketplace takedown was part of Operation Hyperion

Authorities say the now-closed Dark Web marketplace was used to sell illegal drugs, weapons, and other illegal services.

According to Dutch laws, suspects face up to twelve years in prison for international drug trafficking and up to eight years in prison for arms trafficking.

The marketplace's takedown by Dutch authorities was part of an international effort known as Operation Hyperion.

According to a press release from the US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), law enforcement from the UK, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, the Netherlands, France, Finland, Sweden, Span, and Ireland participated in Operation Hyperion, which resulted in countless of arrests and the takedown of multiple Dark Web marketplaces used for trading illegal products.
