Last year, the losses from romance and confidence scams added to over $362 million (70% more compared to the previous year), based on 18,000 complaints. However, not all victims report the deceit.

Romance scammers typically sweet-talk the victims for months to establish trust and then ask for favors like opening a bank account, mediate money transfers, or sending fake checks.

In an advisory on Monday, the Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) warns that threat actors "groom their victims over time and convince them to open bank accounts under the guise of sending or receiving funds."


"Grooming is defined as preparing a victim to conduct fraudulent activity on their behalf through communications intended to develop a trust relationship. These accounts are used to facilitate criminal activities for a short period of time."


Another method is to tell the victims about a lucrative business opportunity that requires a U.S. bank account for investors to wire funds for the project.


"The victim is asked to open a bank account or register a limited liability company in the victim’s name and then to receive and send money from that account to other accounts controlled by the actor."


Scattered Canary, one group of scammers whose activity the Agari Cyber Intelligence Division (ACID) has been tracking for 11 years is mixing various types of scams (romance, fake jobs, tax, and social security fraud) with BEC attacks to increase their profits and mature their operations.

ACID's investigation of the group revealed that Scattered Canary started as a one-man operation running Craigslist scams. In a few years, the actor expanded into romance scams.


"Once they had secured enough mules via their romance scams to launder their stolen money, they shifted from targeting individuals to targeting enterprises, and the group’s BEC operation was born." ACID.




Romance scam victims soon became the main source for money mules for Scattered Canary as this strategy decreased the risk of getting caught and had a positive impact on revenue.

To exemplify the level of trust the scammers achieved from their victims, ACID shared with BleepingComputer the the story of one victim, who had been converted into a money mule, unbeknownst to her.

She opened five mule account for the threat actor and handed over the credentials. While she was still under the spell of the online boyfriend, she used passwords like " weare4ever" and "2hearts1love."

The victim passed away in September 2017, but a member of the group continued to use her information for an auto loan.

Scattered Canary grew to at least 35 members since it emerged in 2008, with members having specific tasks.



According to Agari, Scattered Canary is just one organization of the hundreds currently running operations from West Africa and around the world.

Scarlet Widow is another criminal gang (based in Nigeria) in the romance scam business, which targets individuals likely to be more lonely, such as farmers, the elderly, the disabled, and divorced.

Operating from the UK and targeting companies across the world is London Blue, a threat actor discovered when it targeted Agari's CFO.
