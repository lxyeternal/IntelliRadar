
	   


A 30-year old Russian national named Aleksey Yurievich Burkov was sentenced today to nine years in prison for running Cardplanet and Direct Connection, two sites that facilitated payment card fraud, computer hacking, and other cybercrimes.

Burkov (also known as Aleksey Yurevich Burkov) pleaded guilty to identity theft, computer intrusions, wire fraud, money laundering, and conspiracy to commit access device fraud in January 2020, facing a maximum of 15 years of prison time.

He was arrested at the Israelian Ben-Gurion airport in December 2015 and was extradited to the United States in November 2019.

Since his sentence also includes the time he already served, Burkov will not have to spend the full nine years in prison before being released.

150,000 stolen payment cards sold

The Cardplanet site controlled by the defendant between at least early 2009 through at least August 2013 was a card shop (also known as a CVV shop and carding site) where he supervised the selling of payment card (debit and credit card) numbers stolen from hundreds of thousands of individuals, many of them U.S. Citizens.

Cardplanet was used to sell data from over 150,000 stolen payment cards according to the indictment and this criminal activity resulted in estimated fraud losses and fraudulent purchases of more than $20,000,000.

"The price for stolen data from one card varied between $2.50 and $60, depending on the card type, country of origin of the card, and the availability of the card holder's personal identifying information, such as the card holder's name and address.

"The compromised cards included at least tens of thousands of cards which had been issued to cardholders in the United States [..]"


Cardplanet's homepage in 2015

Burkov also advertised his Cardplanet site on other underground carding forums as the only such service that would refund the money paid for invalid card data through Liberty Reserve, WebMoney, Western Union, or MoneyGram.

Cardplanet also provided a fee-based "checker" service that allowed customers to validate payment card numbers they purchased, with the promise that Burkov would replace all invalid card numbers.

Some of the customers who bought stolen payment card data from Cardplanet would later encode it within counterfeit cards and used them to pay for services and goods throughout the United States and elsewhere.



Invite-only cybercrime forum

Burkov was also the owner and operator of the Direct Connection cybercrime forum, an invite-only club for elite cybercriminals who used it to advertise stolen goods, such as stolen personally identifiable information (PII) and malicious software, and criminal services, such as hacking services and money laundering.

Those who wanted to become Direct Connection members were required to have "three existing members to 'vouch' for their good reputation among cybercriminals and to provide a sum of money, normally $5,000, as insurance." 

These extreme joining requirements were designed to block law enforcement agents from gaining access to Burkov’s cybercrime forum, as well as to ensure that the members honored the deals they made while conducting business on the Direct Connection forum.

This is part of a series of instances where individuals involved in carding schemes were charged and later sentenced to years of imprisonment.

For instance, a New York City man is facing 25 years in prison after being charged last month with hacking, credit card trafficking, and money laundering for allegedly stealing payment card data using SQL injection attacks.
