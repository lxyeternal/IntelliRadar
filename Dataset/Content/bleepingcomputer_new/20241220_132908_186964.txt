
	   


Some hardware-based password managers lack proper protections for the sensitive data they store and allow reading it in plain text, even after they've been reset.

The information was retrieved through physical access to the electronic board inside the device and connecting directly to the flash chips used for storage.

Stealing data from the safes

Using a Raspberry Pi microcomputer, it was possible to read and extract the information available on the flash chip in RecZone Password Safe, which is still available for prices around $35.

Apart from storing the details in plain text, Eveleigh also found that resetting the device did not affect the storage.

Dumping the data on the chip after this action showed that the only thing that had changed was the master PIN that unlocks the device, which was recoverable in the same way. "The data was still there!!" writes Eveleigh.



This could pose a problem for an owner looking to sell the device. The researcher says that a software reset might have been available on the device but he completely destroyed the screen when removing it, making the gadget unusable.

The issues found in RecZone Password Safe are not specific to this gadget. Looking at Royal's Password Vault Keeper, Eveleigh noticed it was relying on a CMOS chip.


RecZone Password Safe, source: Amazon

Unlike RecZone's product where the sensitive data could be extracted with low-cost equipment, in the case of Password Vault Keeper the researcher needed more expensive tools to read the info. Some protection was also available but it could be bypassed with some effort.

The data was encoded but the researcher applied cryptanalysis techniques to decipher it. Eveleigh believes that the encoding is not unique for each device, once the secret is out for one, data on all of them can be read.


Royal Password Vault Keeper, source: Amazon

A third hardware-based password manager might also have problems keeping the credentials secure. Eveleigh analyzed passwordsFAST, which sells for $24.99 and found that it relied on the I2C serial communication protocol.


passwordFAST manager, source: Amazon

Retrieving the passwords from this product is not possible as easy as for the other two because everything on it was encrypted. Eveleigh told BleepingComputer that while him and his colleagues could not break the encryption, the microcontroller is accessible. With more effort, this leaves the possibility open to read the data, the researcher told us.

PenTestPartners contacted the makers of RecZone Password Safe and Password Vault Keeper to report their findings but neither replied. It is worth noting that although both products can still be purchased online, they are no longer available from their vendors.

Physical password managers

Typically, these gadgets are advertised as an alternative to writing passwords on paper or in digital documents on the computer for easy access.

Hardware-based password managers may seem like a good idea to keep credentials in a safe place that hackers can't easily reach; and even if they do, getting the data would still be unavailable.

There are some disadvantages to using this sort of device, though. Because there is no backup to recover the information from, once the device is stolen or destroyed, your login data is also gone with it.

Other downsides include having to type the credentials manually to log in to an online service, not being able to use a larger set of characters for passwords, length limitations, or a cap for the number of accounts it can hold.

Despite these constraints, some users still see hardware password managers as a valuable tool for protecting sensitive information.
