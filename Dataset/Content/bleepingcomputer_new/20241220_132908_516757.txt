
	   


UK's National Cyber Security Centre urges organizations worried about Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks to implement mitigation measures following a worldwide Wikipedia outage caused by Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attacks.

Wikipedia was down for some users in the U.S., Europe, the UK, and the Middle East throughout September 6 and September 7 after massive DDoS attacks that crippled the Wikimedia servers hosting most Wikipedia projects according to Wikimedia Deutschland.

Wikipedia's site was down for roughly 9 hours according to NetBlocks, an NGO which tracks Internet disruption, with the DDoS attacks having targeted Wikimedia infrastructure in the US and Europe.

"As one of the world’s most popular sites, Wikipedia sometimes attracts 'bad faith' actors. Along with the rest of the web, we operate in an increasingly sophisticated and complex environment where threats are continuously evolving," the Wikimedia Foundation also stated in a press release.



Wikipedia outage recovery

"Today, Wikipedia was hit with a malicious attack that has taken it offline in several countries for intermittent periods," tweeted the official Wikimedia Foundation account, the non-profit that operates Wikipedia. "The attack is ongoing; our Site Reliability Engineering team is working hard to stop it and restore access to the site."

"Because of this, the Wikimedia communities and Wikimedia Foundation have created dedicated systems and staff to regularly monitor and address risks. If a problem occurs, we learn, we improve, and we prepare to be better for next time," also says Wikimedia Foundation's press release.

Cloudflare also gave Wikimedia a hand to get everything up and running again, adding direct communication lines into the mix as the foundation's chief executive officer and executive director Katherine Roberts Maher tweeted.



Wikipedia's worldwide outage was the result of a botnet's test rounds according to some reports, with Netblocks saying that the bots were actually compromised IoT devices used to amplify the attack's output.



DoS attack mitigation

The NCSC urges all organizations troubled about being the target of similar attacks in the future to read and implement the mitigation measures detailed in its DoS guidance.

"The collection of guidance published by the NCSC helps organisations mitigate against DoS attacks and outlines the importance of understanding your service, creating a response plan, scaling and monitoring," says the UK government organization.

NCSC also provides detailed info on the steps to include as part of a minimal DoS response plan that all orgs should have in place just in case they aren't sufficiently prepared to deal with a large scale DDoS attack.

As the NCSC says in its DoS guidance, first published in March 2016 and updated in November 2018, if an attack begins:


 1. Confirm that you are under attack
 2. Understand the nature of the attack
 3. Deploy the mitigations you can quickly put in place
 4. Monitor the attack and recover
