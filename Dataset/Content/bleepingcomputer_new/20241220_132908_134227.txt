
	   


An Android app with over 10 million installations on Google Play attempts to trick Samsung phone users into paying for their firmware updates, which are available free of charge from the vendor.

The app is called Updates for Samsung and pledges to deliver "any OS update for any Samsung device ever released." It also offers to unlock phones regardless of the network operator and provides Android-related content from the developer's website, updato[.]com.

Bells should be ringing from the get-go, especially since the developer is a third party clearly stating in the app's description that they are "not affiliated in any way, shape, or form with Google Inc or Samsung Electronics Co. Ltd."

Speed limit frustration

The app Updates for Samsung provides three methods to get the latest firmware from the South Korean maker, according to CSIS Security Group. One of them is a free-of-charge option for registered users. At the opposite end is a $34.99/year premium subscription that ensures fast downloading of the packages.


CSIS Security Group

Researchers at CSIS Security Group found that the app from Updato makes it its purpose to push users into buying a subscription by capping the download speed to 56KB.

With this limitation, "a typical firmware ROM of ~700 MB would take at least an infuriating wait of 4 hours," they write in a blog post on Thursday, adding that an uncomplaining user would have a hard time seeing the task complete due to timeouts and failures.

"During our tests, we too have observed that the downloads don’t finish, even when using a reliable network," a tactic designed to create frustration and drive users into paying for the fast connection.

In a reply to BleepingComputer, Updato says that their app addresses the needs of phone users that want to install on their devices firmware releases specific to a country, carrier, or release date.

Updates for Samsung simply offers an easy way to search for various firmware versions and is not intended for the "typical Android phone user," the developer told us.


"So we aggregate them for the convenience our audience.  Our database allows people to easily search for firmware in any location for any version for any device.  Saving them many hours of potential searches and uncertainty of origin/safety of file" - Updato


Updato further recommends regular users to update their phones using the official Samsung Kies software or install them via the standard over-the-air package delivery.

After the reports regarding Updates for Samsung behavior came out, thedeveloper pulled the app from Google Play and told us that they would take the following action:

Remove the firmware service portion and non Google payments
	Ensure that there is clarity on the purpose of the application
	Ensure that we live up to our overall 4 Star rating in the store now and into the future
	Focus on our great content

"Regarding the official payment, that was an honest ignorance on our part which we will remedy immediately." - Updato


Phone unlock service

This is not the only ploy the Updato developer uses to swell revenue. Unlocking phones is another service available through their app, for prices starting at $19.99.


CSIS Security Group

We could not test this service because a tool was unavailable for unlocking a device with the details we provided, even if they were real. No payment was required before the verification step.

It should be mentioned that the phone unlock process starts on Updato but it continues to mobileunlocked[.]com, a site mentioned in a TechRadar article in 2013 as an online solution for this type of operation.

Updates for Samsung app is still in the official Android repository and garnered some 86,000 reviews that aligned its rating to four stars. However, most ratings visible on the first few scrolls of the screen expose it for the trouble it creates.

Some of the issues mentioned by the reviewers refer to high battery usage, crashes, pushing ads excessively, or impossibility to complete a firmware download or installation.



Some of the reviews are as old as 2013 showing that the app has been on Google Play for a long time and explaining the huge number of installations.


"Although not malicious in the traditional meaning of that term 'Updates for Samsung' does not seem to offer users much of value besides a lighter wallet and as such highlights the risks of ignoring the fine print." - CSIS Security Group


Update [07/06/2019]: Article updated with statements from Updato clarifying the nature of the app and the measures they would be taking to remediate the situation.
