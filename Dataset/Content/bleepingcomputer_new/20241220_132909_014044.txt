Despite absconding UK authorities in the middle of legal procedures, the suspect's trial continued, and a jury found the suspect —Alexandru Sovu, 39, of Romania— guilty. A Crown Court judge sentenced the man to 11 years in prison earlier this week.

Sovu's gang built and sold ATM skimmers

Sovu is no ordinary crook, but the ringleader of a cyber-criminal gang that designed, manufactured, and sold ATM skimming devices and false ATM fronts that allowed other crooks to record credit and debit card numbers from ATM users.

Sovu and others built the devices in a garden shed in Colchester, England. They used now-defunct websites such as cardreaderfactory.com, msrskimmer.com, and goskimmer.com to sell the devices. These websites were registered and operated from under a UK company named CardReaderFactory LTD.

When Sovu and his partners had to ship ATM skimmers abroad, they broke down the device and hid parts as electronic circuitry inside remote controlled toys so that airport safety employees wouldn't get suspicious.

All of Sovu's partners are in jail... except him

Sovu and his gang, all Romanian nationals, were arrested last year. Four men and another woman already received prison sentences for their parts in the operation. The wife of one of the gang's members was cleared of any involvement.

Sovu and another female gang member —Anca Fuioaga, 37, were the last two members still facing trial.

Fuioaga was sentenced to 30 months in prison. She worked as Sovu's UK representative, selling devices to local fraudsters. It was because of one of these fraudster's operations that UK police tracked down the group to the Colchester garden shed.

Sovu was released on bail earlier this year and is believed to have fled the country, now believed to be hiding in Romania or China.

Sovu's previous criminal history

Sovu's life as a cyber-criminal didn't start in the UK. In January 2003, Romanian police arrested Sovu, his brother, and another female suspect on fraud charges after using stolen credit card numbers to purchase products from online stores in Australia, Canada, and the US. Police believed Sovu's first gang worked with other criminal gangs located in China, from where they received the stolen credit card numbers to use in illegal online transactions.

Criminal proceedings dragged for years until 2011, when Sovu was sentenced to seven years in prison, but he later contested and won an appeal.

Sources in the threat intelligence community tell Bleeping Computer that Sovu moved to the UK soon after. In the UK, Sovu worked as a computer programmer but was fired after a few months. In 2012, Sovu founded his UK company and set up his first website for selling ATM skimmers.

Sources tell us that Sovu's devices were considered top-of-the-line products circa 2012-2013, were extremely popular on Russian-speaking cyber-crime forums, but the operation started to wind down in recent years.

Sovu uploads ATM skimmer blueprints online

Journalists from UK newspapers who attended Sovu's sentencing on Monday in London cited the judge as saying that he would not use an ATM again following the revelations in the trial, and that Sovu "shown breathtaking arrogance and put two fingers up to law and order" by fleeing legal procedures.

Threat intelligence sources also said that after being released on bail and disappearing from the UK police's radar, Sovu appears to have uploaded the blueprints of his ATM skimming devices online, as free resources.
