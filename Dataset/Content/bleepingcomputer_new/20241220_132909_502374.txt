US authorities arrested Culbertson in the summer of 2015 when FBI agents shut down infamous underground hacking forum Darkode, in a series of raids conducted all over the US and across the world with the help of fellow law enforcement agencies.

Authorities arrested Culbertson in July 2015, and the suspect pleaded guilty a month later, immediately expressing regret for his actions. At the time, Culbertson faced a sentence of up to 10 years in prison, a fine of $250,000, or both.

Culbertson was one of eight to be arrested in the US. At the time of his arrest, Culbertson was a student at Carnegie Mellon University and an intern at leading US cybersecurity firm FireEye.

On Darkode, the suspect went by the nickname "Android," and had made a name for himself by designing and then selling the Dendroid RAT.

According to reports from Symantec and Lookout, in 2014, Dendroid was one of the most sophisticated Android RATs on the market, with the ability to record phone calls, steal browsing history, take photos remotely, record audio and video via the microphone and camera, intercept SMS messages, show phishing overlays, and steal files from the user's phone storage.

At the time, both security firms said that crooks had the ability to bind Dendroid with Android apps and distribute them via the official Play Store, as the malware could bypass Google's security scans.

Dendroid rented for $300/month

This made Dendroid a favorite tool in the hacking underground, despite its steep monthly rental price of $300. For $65,000, Culbertson was also willing to sell buyers Dendroid's full source code.
