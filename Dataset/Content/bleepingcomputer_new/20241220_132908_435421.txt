
	   


The U.S. Coast Guard issued a marine safety alert including cybersecurity guidance following a cyber incident experienced by a deep draft vessel during February which affected the ship's entire network.

An investigation conducted by an interagency cyber analysis team led by the Coast Guard "concluded that although the malware significantly degraded the functionality of the onboard computer system, essential vessel control systems had not been impacted."

Despite this, the interagency team discovered that the ship impacted by the cyber attack did not have effective cybersecurity measures to counteract such attacks, thus exposing "critical vessel control systems to significant vulnerabilities."

Prior to the incident, the security risk presented by the shipboard network was well known among the crew. Although most crewmembers didn’t use onboard computers to check personal email, make online purchases or check their bank accounts, the same shipboard network was used for official business – to update electronic charts, manage cargo data and communicate with shore-side facilities, pilots, agents, and the Coast Guard.


The Coast Guard also released a Marine Safety Information Bulletin (MSIB) on May 24 about cyber adversaries targeting commercial vessels [PDF] via malware intrusion attempts and email phishing attacks.

"Cyber adversaries are attempting to gain sensitive information including the content of an official Notice of Arrival (NOA) using email addresses that pose as an official Port State Control (PSC) authority such as: port @ pscgov.org," says the May MSIB. "Additionally, the Coast Guard has received reports of malicious software designed to disrupt shipboard computer systems."

Maritime stakeholders were advised via the MSIB to closely check the validity of the email sender prior to responding to any unsolicited email messages and immediately contact the Port State Control (PSC) authority if anything seems out of place.

The Coast Guard also urged them to implement appropriate cyber defense measures designed to block or at least reduce the effects of a cyber attack on the targeted commercial ships.



Among the updated cybersecurity best practices suggested for all commercial vessels as part of the newly released maritime safety alert, the Coast guard recommends [PDF]:


• implementing network segmentation to "make it harder for an adversary to gain access to essential systems and equipment,"
• installing and updating antivirus software to "stop incidents before they impact operations,"
• be wary of external media that wasn't scanned for malware "before being plugged into any shipboard network,"
• creating network profiles for employees to limit privileges and "eliminate the use of generic log-in credentials for multiple personnel,"
• always keep the software up to date to patch any found vulnerabilities.


The Coast Guard concluded the safety alert by strongly encouraging "all vessel and facility owners and operators to conduct cybersecurity assessments to better understand the extent of their cyber vulnerabilities."

In related news, the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) also issued two cybersecurity alerts during June, one about an increase in cyber attacks utilizing destructive wiper tools targeting U.S. industries and government agencies by Iranian-backed actors.

The second CISA warning was published to alert Windows users to patch the critical severity Remote Desktop Services (RDS) RCE security flaw known as BlueKeep after the agency achieved remote code execution on a system running a vulnerable version of Windows 2000.
