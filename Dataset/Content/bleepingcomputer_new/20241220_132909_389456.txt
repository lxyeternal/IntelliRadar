The individual is considered a “rising star” in the Russian-speaking underground, their specialty being attacks against banks, financial institutions, and government agencies.

Cheap eToro accounts

Using the alias “Sheriff,” the actor advertised on July 6 an auction for 62,000 accounts belonging to users of the eToro social trading platform.

Sheriff mentions in the post that all accounts are active and that all credentials work, meaning that buyers could withdraw funds or dabble in trading that is beneficial to them.

Bank Security, a researcher focused on banking threats and fraud, found another actor advertising eToro accounts on multiple cybercriminal forums and providing proof of access.



The researcher says that this second seller has a long list of offers for accounts for payment/money transfer systems, cryptocurrency exchange and online trading platforms.

		Sheriff of Citrix RDP breaches

Although Sheriff may look like a low-level actor, they’ve been interacting with the public-facing persona of the high-profile ransomware operator REvil.

New research published today by boutique cybersecurity firm Advance Intelligence (AdvIntel) reveals that Sheriff is deeply involved in the network intrusion game.


"One of their main accomplices operates under the alias “Sheriff”; they are a rising star of the Russian-speaking underground and specialize in attacks against banks, financial institutions, and government agencies" - Advanced Intelligence


The hacker has been offering access to large companies, some with billions in assets, after exploring them and stealing data. AdvIntel says that Sheriff breaches are mainly based on brute-force attacks and credential-stealing malware.

The researchers say that Sheriff’s targets include a major investment fund; a private U.S. cybersecurity company; multiple universities in Australia, Canada, and the U.S.; firms providing long-distance passenger transportation, warehouse materials, and cloud computing.

In June, the hacker advertised access to a European construction company and 3,200 cPanel accounts. Their hacking capades go further back as Sheriff announced in May that they had admin access to about 815,000 orders from an e-commerce organization.


source: AdvIntel

Access to the networks of these victims advertised over the past few weeks and the breaches relied on exploiting open remote desktop connections to Citrix servers, which aligns with REvil’s latest choice of intrusion method.

AdvIntel found that Sheriff interacted with REvil through the ransomware gang’s UNKN persona. This and the use of Citrix exploits suggests that the two are working together, at least occasionally.


source: AdvIntel

The researchers paint Sheriff as “an expert in the financial sector and Citrix breaches” and put them in the “top talent” category of hackers that REvil has been recruiting since late last year.

The report from AdvIntel provides a more detailed view of REvil's collaborators, all of them high-profile hackers that have been either endorsing the gang on underground communities or providing access to targets.

Apart from Sheriff, a new name is Energydrinkkk, who specializes ion access to companies in the energy and oil and gas sectors. Apart from this, REvil may be using a new alias for their contact person, which appears to be "Unknown" instead of the previously used "UNKN."

Sheriff and Unknown have been in direct contact as they previously communicate with UNKN.



Energydrinkkk recently offered RDP access to an energy company's Microsoft Online space, AdvIntel says. The actor boasted that they could access internal communications and company email through employees' personal computers., as well as having copied passwords and contact information.

According to the research, in May they "expressed a desire to enable a ransomware attack against the company," a solicitation that was met with interest for buying Energydrinkkk's access for $1,500 from an individual that is likely a REvil affiliate.

The connections established by AdvIntel show that top-hackers on Russian-speaking cybercriminal underground make various deals to increase their profits. Either by selling access to ransomware gangs or by auctioning accounts for various services, they are the cogs that keep the wheels of big-money cybercrime rolling.
