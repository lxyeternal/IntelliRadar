
	   


US authorities reported they have arrested Jonathan Powell, 29, of Phoenix, Arizona, for hacking over 1,000 email accounts for at least two US universities and attempting to hack email accounts for 75 other universities across the country.

Investigators say that between October 2015 and September 2016, Powell abused password reset functions for university email services in order to gain access to email accounts.

Once Powell found a loophole into the password reset system, he used it to change the password of student email accounts. He then used used these hacked email accounts to reset the password of other services where the email address was used to register user accounts, such as Facebook, Apple iCloud, Google, Yahoo, or LinkedIn.

Additionally, Powell also searched hacked university and non-university email accounts for sensitive information. Court documents reveal that for one account, Powell had searched for terms such as "password," "horny," or "naked."

The Powell criminal complaint names only two universities as the hacker's victims, one located in New York, and the other in Pennsylvania.

Authorities say that for the New York-based university, Powell had attempted to change the passwords for 2,054 unique email addresses. He was successful in hacking 1,035 accounts, for which he changed login credentials 18,640 times because victims kept wrestling with the suspect for control over their email accounts.

Powell was less prodigious when targeting the university located in Pennsylvania, where during the month of September 2016, he hacked 15 out of 220 targeted email accounts.

Powell faces up to five years in prison

Authorities arrested Powell in October and raided the suspect's home and working place, seizing storage and backup storage equipment from both locations. Data found on these devices dates as recent as September 30, and showed that Powell had targeted 75 other US universities with the same scheme.

For most, the suspect created files and folders where authorities said he stored what appeared to be login credentials for other compromised email accounts.

Authorities charged Powell with one count of fraud in connection with computers. The maximum sentence for this charge is five years in a federal prison.

UPDATE [January 31, 2017]: Powell was sentenced to six months in prison.
