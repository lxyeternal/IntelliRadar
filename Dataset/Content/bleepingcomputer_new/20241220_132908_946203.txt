
	   


The backdoor found in the firmware of various low-end Android smartphones, which was traced back to a Chinese company, was active since July this year, according to mobile security firm Trustlook.

The existence of this backdoor came to light in mid-November when security firm Kryptowire published a report revealing that Chinese company Shanghai Adups Technology Co. Ltd. (Adups in this article) had included malicious code in the firmware it sold to numerous Android phone manufacturers.

Adups backdoor used to steal data behind users and OEMs' back

The smartphone vendors had used the Adups firmware as a Firmware Over-The-Air (FOTA) update system for their firmware, included with the Android phones they were manufacturing and selling.

According to Kryptowire, Adups engineers would have been able to collect data such as SMS messages, call logs, contact lists, geo-location data, IMSI and IMEI identifiers, and would have been able to forcibly install other apps or execute root commands on all devices.

Besides naming US Android smartphone vendor BLU as one of the affected brands, the original Kryptowire report didn't feature any other information.

Over 43 smartphone vendors used Adups firmware

Trustlook researchers have now released an in-depth report that expands on Kryptowire's findings, after analyzing 1,904 device firmware images where the Adups FOTA system had been included.

The mobile security firm says it identified clues that the user data collection system had become active without authorization in July this year.

Ironically, the list of affected vendors also includes Ragentek, a company that also developed and included its own backdoored firmware, a fact that came to light one week after the Adups discovery.

Trustlook believes that around 700 million Android devices may have Adups firmware installed. The company has also updated its mobile security solution to scan and detect Adups firmware.

A general information page is also available here. Trustlook also played an important role in bringing to light the BadKernel Android vulnerability.
