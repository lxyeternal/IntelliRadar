
	   


Vulnerabilities discovered in the PremiSys IDentity access system could allow attackers to bypass its building entrance security. The vendor was warned about the flaws but still hasn't released the necessary patches.

Developed by IDenticard Systems as a comprehensive photo ID software, PremiSys ID can be used for designing and printing building access badges, and for managing cardholder data. The mobile app allows identifying employees by scanning their badges.

Hardcoded, deafault creds, weak encryption

Examining version 3.1.190 of the software that powers the PremiSys IDentity identification and building access management system, James Sebree of cybersecurity company Tenable found four vulnerabilities, some more severe than others.

It is a hardcoded backdoor, in essence, that "allows attackers to add new users to the badge system, modify existing users, delete users, assign permission, and pretty much any other administrative function," Sebree explains.

An attacker could steal the information in the badge system database or alter it without any hurdle in their way.



Looking at the software, the researcher discovered that the encryption used for storing sensitive information, like user credentials, relied on a weak method Base64 encoded MD5 hashes - salt + password). This flaw is now identified as CVE-2019-3907.

Another oversight (CVE-2019-3907) from the developer is keeping the card backups in a format very similar to a password-protected ZIP archive. The trouble here is that the unlock key is coded into the application.

A fourth issue relates to the protection of the database that holds the IDenticard service information. Unlocking this database gives an attacker full access to the sensitive contents.

Tracked as CVE-2019-3909, the flaw consists in using a default username and password pair to restrict access to the database.

Tenable says that "users cannot change this password without sending custom passwords to the vendor directly in order to receive an encrypted variant to use in their configurations."

No patches in sight

Tenable tried to coordinate the disclosure of these vulnerabilities with PremiSys, but they say their effort was not met by a reply from the vendor. The cybersecurity firm also notified the US Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT).

The researchers delayed making their findings public for more than 90 days, giving the vendor a chance to remedy the issues or at least make contact. Tenable's disclosure deadline ended on January 3.

On January 9, the quartet of flaws remained unpatched. This presents a risk to organizations relying on PremiSys system for access into their buildings.

Under these circumstances, users should try to mitigate the issues themselves. One recommendation is to use network segmentation to keep the vulnerable system isolated. Another is to restrict access to the system from outside the network.

Update 1/15/19:

In statement given to BleepingComputer today by John Fox, a Senior Product Manager for IDenticard Systems, they are taking the research seriously and apologize for overlooking Tenable's communication attempts. They further stated that they expect to release updates soon to address these vulnerabilities.


We take the issues identified by Tenable, a leading third party cyber security research company, seriously and are looking to incorporate their feedback into our ongoing product development cycle. PremiSys™ System software is constantly evolving and we appreciate the diligence Tenable outlined in their messages to us. 

At IDenticard, we pride ourselves in listening and responding to our customers. Regrettably, we overlooked the communication attempts from Tenable. This is unacceptable for us and we are currently reviewing our inbound communication practices to ensure it does not happen in the future. We welcome any engagement from Tenable regarding this matter.

The safety and security of our customers is our first priority. As a global leader in security and identification solutions, IDenticard is committed to continuous improvement and addressing customer concerns. As part of our ongoing agile software development process, we anticipate releasing improvements in the near term and will keep our customers updated with how those improvements address Tenable’s concerns.
