
	   


Insider attacks are becoming more and more prevalent each year as 73% of information of IT professionals told Bitglass, while 59% also stated that their companies have also gone through at least one such incident during the past year.

The Cloud Access Security Brokering (CASB) company Bitglass got these results after surveying 437 IT professionals in partnership with the Cybersecurity Insiders community of 400,000 information security professionals.

While organizations are always aware of the threats coming from malicious external actors, Bitglass found that a large percentage of them don't see it as important to put in place countermeasures to discover and stop insider attacks.

"Insider attacks are harder to identify and remediate than those that originate from outside the enterprise," said Rich Campagna, CMO of Bitglass. "This is caused by a number of factors highlighted throughout the report, including insufficient authentication, inadequate user behavior monitoring in the cloud, and a failure to properly secure personal devices. If organizations want to prevent insider attacks, they have to address these security gaps."

Insider attacks are especially dangerous since they are harder to detect in comparison with attacks coming from external sources, with the fact that enterprise security controls poorly implemented make it a lot easier for insiders to perform such attacks.

Bitglass’ 2019 Insider Threat Report also discovered that "only 50 percent of organizations provide user trainings about insider threats, and a mere 31 percent implement secondary authentication to defend against them."

Despite this, 56% of professionals who got interviewed said that insider threats are usually detected in their organizations during the day occur, while 50% stated that attack recovery will also be done by the end of the day the attack happens.



Cloud migration is seen as a factor which makes insider attacks even harder to detect and defend against, with organizations not implementing suitable tools for monitoring "abnormal user behavior across their cloud footprints" according to 41% of the survey respondents.

In addition, insider threats will become harder to detect one an organization migrates its IT infrastructure to the cloud said 56% of the IT professionals interviewed by Bitglass.

To make matters even worse, according to the survey's results, insider threat training designed to make it more straightforward for security professionals to be aware of and detect malicious insiders easier is available in just 50% of organizations, while only 31% "implement secondary authentication to defend against them."


BYOD insider threats

Additionally, when asked by Bitglass if their companies can detect insider threats stemming from personal mobile devices, a mere 12% of respondents said that they have the tools and training needed to do it. 

This confirms the answers provided another question in the survey where "respondents identified endpoints in general (59%) and mobile devices in
particular (46%) as the two assets most commonly used to launch insider attacks."
