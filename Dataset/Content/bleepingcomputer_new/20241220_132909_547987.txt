
	   


To mark the fifth year of its Security Bug Bounty program, GitHub announced a number of updates, including an expanded scope with more GitHub products covered, increased reward amounts, and new Legal Safe Harbor terms for full legal protection for researchers.

GitHub stated that it paid over $165,000 to security researchers during 2018 in rewards as part of the company's public bug bounty program, and $250,000 in total after adding up researcher grants, private bug bounty programs, and live-hacking events.

Also, the 2019 GitHub Security Bug Bounty program will also include all first-party services under the employee-facing github.net and githubapp.com domains, since the "security of our users’ data also depends on the security of our employees and our internal systems."

Increased reward amounts

The reward amounts at all levels of the Security Bug Bounty program have also been increased by GitHub and critical vulnerabilities no longer have a maximum reward amount set, allowing the company to add extra compensation for security researchers who have been able to come up with "truly cutting-edge research."

GitHub's new reward amounts are now:

Critical: $20,000 - $30,000+
High: $10,000 - $20,000
Medium: $4,000 - $10,000
Low: $617 - $2,000

Legal Safe Harbor terms

GitHub "added a robust set of Legal Safe Harbor terms to our site policy" designed to make it possible for security researchers to always feel safe from legal risks.

The new research safe harbor terms based on the Legal Bug Bounty repo of EdOverflow, with multiple additions and modifications based on best-practice safe harbor examples from Dropbox’s updated vulnerability disclosure policy, Amit Elazaril research, and Bugcrowd’s disclose.io project, as well as on discussions with security researchers involved with the program.

According to GitHub's new Legal Safe Harbor terms:

• Your research activity remains protected and authorized even if you accidentally overstep our bounty program’s scope. 
• We will do our best to protect you against legal risk from third parties who won’t commit to the same level of safe harbor protections.
• You won’t be violating our site terms if it’s specifically for bounty research.
