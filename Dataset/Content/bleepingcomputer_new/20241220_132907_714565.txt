
	   


Hospitality chain Millennium Hotels & Resorts North America (MHR) is warning customers that malware may have affected food and beverage point-of-sale (POS) systems at 14 of its locations.

MHR first learned about a possible malware infection, which is believed to have occurred in early March 2016, from the United States Secret Service. Federal agents worked with MHR to take the affected POS terminals offline and isolate the affected card processing elements in mid-June 2016.

Some time thereafter, the hotel chain's POS provider reached out to inform MHR it had found malicious code on certain legacy point-of-sale systems, including the same type of terminals deployed at some of its locations.

As quoted in a statement:


"We urge customers who visited our U.S. hotels between early March and the end of June this year to check their payment card records and to report dubious transactions to their card operators immediately. We also urge customers to take advice from their card operators on any further recommended security precautions. The Millennium Hotels & Resorts team, above all, values its customer relationships and is committed to protecting customers’ payment card information. Since being informed of this incident, we have taken a number of steps to ensure that this commitment is met in full."




MHR hasn't elaborated on what those "steps" entail. The company has revealed, however, that it hired digital security forensic experts to investigate this incident. As of this writing, those experts have yet to find any trace of malware on the company's POS systems.

Strange...but then again, this incident is a bit different from other hotel POS breaches. For example, in the POS malware attack that affected 20 Starwood, Marriott, Hyatt, and Intercontinental locations, operating chain HEI Hotels & Resorts learned about the breach from its card processor. The supplier had detected suspicious activity on HEI customers' payment cards and decided to reach out to HEI.

MHR has made no mention of having received reports indicative of payment card fraud from its customers. To be fair, the card processor might be working with the Secret Service, whose agents have taken the lead in informing restaurants and hotels of POS breaches in the past.

But that still leaves the question: where is the malware?

As forensic experts look into this matter more deeply, MHR customers can rest assured that the affected POS systems served only the hotels' food and beverage stations. The breach did not affect other POS locations, unlike the incidents at Hyatt Hotels and Hard Rock Hotel & Casino.

The breach also didn't affect any other MHR systems, including those responsible for hotel property management and booking, as the hotel chain keeps those services separate from its point-of-sale network.
