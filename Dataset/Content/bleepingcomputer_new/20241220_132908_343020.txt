Attributed to the least sophisticated division of the Gaza Cybergang threat actor, SneakyPastes carried through early 2019 and used politically-themed phishing emails to target individuals and organizations particularly interested in Palestinian problems. It claimed over 240 victims in 39 countries.

Popular targets include government entities, education, media outlets, journalists, activists, political parties or personnel, healthcare, and banking. Almost half of the victims were from the Palestinian territories.

The attacks started with a phishing mail that either carried the initial stage malware or provided a link to a paste site to download it. To hide its tracks, the threat actor used disposable email addresses and domains for sending malicious messages.

To avoid detection and extend the lifetime of their command and control (C2) server (192.169.7.250), Gaza Cybergang hosted malware on free file sharing services  The malware was hosted on several public storage services: Mailimg, Github, Pastebin, dev-point.co, a.pomf.cat, and upload.cat.



"We have identified several implants that leveraged PowerShell, VBS, JS, and dotnet for resilience and persistence," threat experts from Kaspersky's GReAT (Global Research and Analysis Team) write in a blog post today.

The malware in the final stage was a persistent RAT that collected documents and spreadsheets (PDF, DOC, DOCX, XLS, and XLSX), compressed, encrypted and uploaded them to the C2.

The campaign is currently inactive and a large part of the infrastructure disabled through collaborative efforts between law enforcement agencies and Kaspersky Lab.

Three divisions, each more proficient than the other

Gaza Cybergang, also known as Gaza Hackers Team, is an Arabic-speaking, politically-motivated threat actor focused on the Middle-East. It appears to have three operational groups united by a common goal and targets but distinct in terms of the sophistication level, tools, and techniques.

Group 3 is the most adept of them all, responsible for activities known as Operation Parliament. Group 2, linked to Desert Falcon attacks, is less sophisticated and relies on malware of its own creation.

Kaspersky threat experts say that Group 1 of the Gaza Cybergang hacker collective is the least sophisticated, a "classical low-budget group"  with a small infrastructure and a largely open-source toolset.

It is also known for the MoleRATs campaign back in 2012 when they used the freely available RAT XtremeRAT, as well as the Poison Ivy RAT, typically associated with Chinese threat actors. Despite the scant resources, the group managed to achieve reasonable success.

Kaspersky says that Gaza Cybergang keeps evolving and adapting to the Middle-East North Africa (MENA) region and can access more advanced tools as well as use more complex techniques and procedures, which are expected to be put in use in the future.
