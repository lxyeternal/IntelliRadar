
	   


Microsoft says that a state-sponsored Iranian cyber-espionage group tracked as Phosphorus by the Microsoft Threat Intelligence Center (MSTIC) attempted to get account info on over 2,700 of its customers, attack 241 of them, and compromised four accounts between August and September.

The attacks conducted by the Phosphorus hacking group (aka APT35, Charming Kitten, or Ajax Security Team), tracked by MSTIC since 2013, were observed by Microsoft for over 30 days as they targeted accounts related to U.S. politics, Iranian expats, and journalists.

Phosphorus is a threat group known for using spear-phishing attacks against government targets for espionage purposes, as well as journalists with a focus on Iran or global politics.

The targeted accounts are associated with a U.S. presidential campaign, current and former U.S. government officials, journalists covering global politics and prominent Iranians living outside Iran," says Microsoft Corporate Vice President for Customer Security & Trust, Tom Burt.

"Four accounts were compromised as a result of these attempts; these four accounts were not associated with the U.S. presidential campaign or current and former U.S. government officials."

The company notified all the customers that were targeted in the recent Phosphorus attacks and worked with all of the ones that requested assistance to secure their accounts and data.

"Phosphorus used information gathered from researching their targets or other means to game password reset or account recovery features and attempt to take over some targeted accounts," adds Burt.

"For example, they would seek access to a secondary email account linked to a user’s Microsoft account, then attempt to gain access to a user’s Microsoft account through verification sent to the secondary account."

The APT group also collected phone numbers belonging to their targets and used in later stages of their attacks to authenticate accounts password resets.

Thousands of other customers attacked by nation-backed hackers

Microsoft also stated in July that it alerted almost 10,000 of its customers in the past year that they've been either targeted or compromised by nation-state sponsored threat groups.

"About 84% of these attacks targeted our enterprise customers, and about 16% targeted consumer personal email accounts," said Burt at the time.

This shows the current reliance of nation-states on cyber attacks for collecting and extracting intelligence, as well as "influence geopolitics or achieve other objectives."

APT groups from Iran, North Korea, and Russia were found to be behind the vast majority of these nation-state attacks against Microsoft customers

The most notable activity came from threat actors such as "Holmium and Mercury operating from Iran, Thallium operating from North Korea, and two actors operating from Russia we call Yttrium and Strontium."

Domains used in attacks seized

In related news, court documents unsealed in March showed how Microsoft’s Digital Crimes Unit blocked some of the cyber attacks conducted by the Phosphorus group by taking over domains they used as part of their core operations.

By seizing 99 domains previously controlled by Phosphorus, Microsoft effectively took over parts of the hacking group's operations and was able to "redirect traffic from infected devices" to its sinkholes, collecting important info on the group's activity.

This is not something unique given that Microsoft previously filed 15 similar cases against the Russian-backed APT group Strontium (aka Fancy Bear or APT28) in August 2018, leading to the seizure of 91 domains controlled by the hacking group.

Secure your Microsoft account

Microsoft advises customers who observe suspicious activity on their accounts to change their passwords and enable two-step verification, and to follow these recommendations on how to keep their accounts secure.

"However, if you are part of a political campaign, a political party committee or an NGO or think tank working on issues related to democracy, you are eligible for Microsoft AccountGuard, an offering from our Defending Democracy Program, and can sign up here," says Burt.

"There are currently 60,000 accounts in 26 countries protected by AccountGuard, which provides monitoring and unified threat notification across the Office 365 accounts you use for work and the personal accounts of your staff and others affiliated with your organization that opt-in for this protection."

Since AccountGuard is generally available, Microsoft has issued over 800 notifications regarding attempted nation-state attacks targeting AccountGuard customers.
