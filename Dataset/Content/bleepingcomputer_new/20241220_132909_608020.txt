
	   


Google expanded the scope of its Google Play Security Reward Program (GPSRP) to include all Android apps from the Google Play Store with over 100 million installs.

Security researchers who report vulnerabilities in one of these apps will be able to collect rewards from Google and one from the app's developers if they also run their own bug bounty program on the HackerOne platform.

"This opens the door for security researchers to help hundreds of organizations identify and fix vulnerabilities in their apps," says Google.

We are increasing the scope of GPSRP to include all apps in Google Play with 100 million or more installs. These apps are now eligible for rewards, even if the app developers don’t have their own vulnerability disclosure or bug bounty program. — Google

Notifications will also be sent to the developers when an in-scope vulnerability is disclosed for their apps including info on the security flaw and instructions on how to patch it.

The alerts will be sent via Play Console as part of the App Security Improvement (ASI) program, a service providing Google Play app developers with advice on how to boost their apps' security.

"Over its lifetime, ASI has helped more than 300,000 developers fix more than 1,000,000 apps on Google Play," adds Google.


ASI notification example

"In 2018 alone, the program helped over 30,000 developers fix over 75,000 apps. The downstream effect means that those 75,000 vulnerable apps are not distributed to users until the issue is fixed."

Until now, over $265,000 in bounties have been paid by Google through GPSRP, with both scope and reward increases resulting in $75,500 being awarded in bug bounties across July and August alone.

Developer Data Protection Reward Program also launched today

Google also launched the Developer Data Protection Reward Program (DDPRP) in collaboration with the HackerOne platform, a bug bounty program designed to reward researchers who help "identify and mitigate data abuse issues in Android apps, OAuth projects, and Chrome extensions."

DDPRP accepts contributions from hackers willing to report any and all apps violating Google Play, Google API, or Google Chrome Web Store Extensions program policies.

The program aims to reward anyone who can provide verifiably and unambiguous evidence of data abuse, in a similar model as Google’s other vulnerability reward programs. In particular, the program aims to identify situations where user data is being used or sold unexpectedly, or repurposed in an illegitimate way without user consent. — Google

If the data abuse issues reported through DDPRP are confirmed, the involved apps and extensions will be subsequently removed from Google Play or Google Chrome Web Store.

If the developers also abuse Google service APIs to access information out of the restricted scopes, their API access will also be revoked.

Even though Google does not yet provide a maximum reward or a reward table, a single report could net researchers rewards as large as $50,000 depending on the reported issue's impact.
