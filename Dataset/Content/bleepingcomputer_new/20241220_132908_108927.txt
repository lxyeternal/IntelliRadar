The bill was passed by the lower house of the Russian parliament after 327 deputies voted for, 40 voted against, and one abstained during a public session which was broadcasted on the lower chamber's website.

According to the newly introduced amendment to Article 20.1 of the Code on Administrative Offenses of the Russian Federation, the administrative detention punishments can reach up to 15 days and the administrative fines up to 100,000 rubles ($1,509) :

The supporters No. 606596-7 Bill insist that this the new law does not implement a form of censorship into the Russian Federation's laws but it only adds appropriate penalties for individuals who do not show the proper respect deserved by authorities and state symbols in content posted online.

Andrei Klishas, a senator from Putin’s ruling United Russia party, 

However, as reported by The Guardian, Sergey Shvakin, a Moscow-based lawyer, wrote on his Facebook profile that "Soon we’ll be telling jokes about the authorities in whispers in the kitchen."

Before voting to pass the bill, the Russian Duma refused to allow the inclusion and deleted the record of an amendment proposed by the Information Policy Committee which would allow for citizens who first published an “insult” falling under the law not to be prosecuted.

Putin to sign the law during the next few weeks

This happened right after The Federal Service for Supervision of Communications, Information Technology and Mass Media (Roskomnadzor) requested for the amendment not to be taken into consideration, as reported by RBC.

The law was approved by the lower chamber of the Russian Parliament after polls showed that confidence in President Vladimir Putin fell to an all-time low of 33% after another bill on retirement age was signed into law.

Putin is expected to sign the law during the coming few weeks after the bill also gets approved by the Federation Council, the Federal Assembly's superior chamber.
