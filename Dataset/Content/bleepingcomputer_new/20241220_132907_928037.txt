This innovative new approach to run a phishing campaign observed by Doctor Web's researchers also has the advantage of circumventing the spam filters built into email servers and clients, besides tricking the receivers into thinking they're receiving official newsletter subscription messages.

Email-based phishing is used by bad actors to trick their targets into going to websites designed to collect various confidential information, downloading attachments containing malware, or clicking links redirecting to malware-laden attachments.

Example phishing emails

As detailed by the Dr.Web report the crooks behind this novel phishing campaign are sending emails that come from official addresses from companies with a worldwide presence such as Audi, Austrian Airlines, and S-Bahn Berlin, with a 'money for you' header in Russian.

"At the beginning of the email, a link leads users to the hacked page of a dating website. Then due to malicious code embedded into the website’s stub page users are redirected through several other websites to a phishing one," says Dr.Web.

After landing on the phishing page, the victims are told that they can enter a promo dubbed "The lucky e-mail" which requires them to fill in a number of survey questions to be able to win the prizes that range from €10 to €3000. Fake testimonials from both hypothetic prize winners and losers are also included on the phishing page to make the whole scheme a lot more credible.


Phishing pages

Once the survey questions are filled in and extra info regarding the promo, reward size, and withdrawal conditions, the scammers will also ask the potential "prize winners" for a EUR to RUB exchange commission required to enter the international promo.

"To pay the commission, victims are redirected to a fake payment page where they are supposed to enter their credit card information. Once complete, victims are asked to provide the verification code sent by SMS. When all the steps are completed, the victim’s bank account is debited and their credit card data is left to the hackers. Additionally, no funds are credited to the victim’s bank account," also says Dr.Web.

The most interesting part, however, is how the miscreants use the newsletter signup form from the official websites of various companies to send their phishing emails by adding links to their phishing websites within the "Last Name" field and the "Money for you" phishing campaign header in the "Name" field.

Google Translate and tax season also used as phishing vectors

Just last week, ProofPoint researchers illustrated how phishing campaigns are targeting the tax season with realistic-looking phishing emails coupled with malicious attachments used to drop malware on victims' computers or to convince them to reveal sensitive info with the help of maliciously crafted pages.

During March, two other phishing campaigns were also actively targeting Netflix and American Express (AMEX) customers to steal both credit and debit cards, as well as social security information as discovered by the Microsoft Office 365 Threat Research team.

In addition, in February, another phishing campaign was spotted while attempting to steal Google and Facebook credentials utilizing Google Translate as camouflage on mobile browsers unearthed by Larry Cashdollar, a member of Akamai's Security Intelligence Response Team (SIRT).
