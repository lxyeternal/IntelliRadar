
	   


A study released by researchers from Newcastle University shows that it could take an attacker around six seconds to guess payment card details, which he could later use to carry out fraudulent transactions.

The research paper titled "Does The Online Card Payment Landscape Unwittingly Facilitate Fraud?" describes a technique called brute-forcing, or guessing attack, in which an attacker tries a sequence of numbers until he guesses the correct one.

While brute-forcing is a common security risk and most banks, payment systems, and online stores protect against them, researchers say they've found a unique way of carrying out such attacks by distributing "guesses" across multiple websites.

The research team says they've used the Alexa website ranking system to create a list of the 400 most popular online stores. After further analysis and after they removed websites with proper protection, they narrowed down the list to 342, but an attacker could compile lists much larger than this.

The team then took a valid payment card number and attempted to guess its expiration date. Since most cards are valid 60 months, it took researchers a second to discover the valid expiration date by asking one of the 342 websites at a time, if the expiration date was valid, until they got the correct answer.

They used the same distributed guessing method to determine the CVV code inscribed on the back of a payment card, used to validate transactions. Since these are three-digit numbers, it took an attacker a maximum of 1,000 guesses to determine the correct CVV.

Most stores ask for these three details to initiate a transaction. The bare minimum is to ask for the payment card number and CVV, while some websites ask for the user's address. Nevertheless, an attacker can use distributed guessing to determine the basic minimum necessary to initiate a fraudulent transaction.


One of the many carding sites available online

There are numerous websites online where crooks can buy dumps of payment card numbers, complete with names, stolen either via ATM skimmers or PoS malware.

Nevertheless, researchers say that an attacker doesn't necessarily need to buy stolen payment card details because he could use the same distributed guessing method to determine if a debit or credit card number is valid

Takes around six seconds to guess payment card details

Researchers say that an attacker needs around six seconds to guess the entire payment card number, its expiration date, and the CVV number, after which he can initiate fraudulent transactions.

Only Visa cards are vulnerable

According to the research team, only Visa payment cards are vulnerable to this attack.

Mastercard has implemented system-wide protections to detect repeated invalid payment requests for the same card coming from different locations (online stores).

Additionally, all payments secured with the 3D Secure technology (Verified by Visa, American Express SafeKey, or MasterCard SecureCode) are also safe. Payment card owners are urged to turn on 3D Secure details for their accounts, which in most cases are provided for free.

The research team says they've notified both Visa and a portion of the affected websites about their research in advance of publishing their work.
