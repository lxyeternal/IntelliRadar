
	   


Russian authorities arrested nine hackers they suspect of being involved in the distribution of the Lurk malware. This is the second wave of arrests after authorities apprehended 50 suspects in May 2016.

The second arrests were reported today by Russian Interior Ministry spokesperson Irina Volk.

Volk said Russian police arrested nine new persons on January 25, in the Moscow, St. Petersburg, Krasnodar, Tver, and Sverdlovsk regions.

Not all suspects are the malware creators, as police said many participated in various roles to the group's operations. A Russian court ordered the nine to be jailed.

Total Lurk suspects: 55

Russian police and the country's secret service (FSB) cracked down on the Lurk gang in May 2016, when they announced they detained 50 persons.

Authorities later revealed they jailed 19 suspects and brought charges against 27 others who were not detained.

The recent arrests bring the arrest total to 55 suspects currently under investigation for their role in the creation and distribution of the Lurk malware, which authorities said caused damages of $45 million (3 billion rubles) to Russian banks and other financial companies in Russia alone.

Volk said the attackers also compromised several enterprises activating in Russia's critical infrastructure sector.

Lurk gang also developed the Angler exploit kit

The Lurk gang has been active since 2011 when they started distributing the Lurk trojan. Toward 2015, as Russian banks upgraded their security measures, the Lurk crew specialized in renting out the Angler exploit kit.

According to a Kaspersky report, the Lurk gang initially developed the Angler exploit kit as an internal tool to automate Lurk infections via browser exploits and drive-by downloads. Years later, as revenue from the Lurk malware died down, the group discovered they could supplement their "revenue" by renting Angler to other crooks.
