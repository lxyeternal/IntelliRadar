Operators of a known campaign that compromises websites still vulnerable to the Drupalgeddon 2 security bug targeted ‘worldwish.org’ and planted a script that uses the computing resources of the visitor’s systems to mine for cryptocurrency.

The risk of an outdated CMS

The mining script was hosted at ‘drupalupdates[.]tk,’ a location tied with cybercriminal activities leveraging the vulnerability affecting outdated installations of the Drupal content management system.

According to research from Trustwave SpiderLabs, the attackers injected the Make-A-Wish website with CoinIMP, a free JavaScript Web Miner created as an alternative method to generate profit from websites than displaying advertisements.

The investigation revealed that the attackers relied on multiple techniques to avoid static detections; one of them was to change the domain name hosting the JavaScript miner.

Blacklisting identification solutions would also fail because of the WebSocket use of various domains and IP addresses.



In April this year, proof-of-concept code emerged for the Drupalgeddon 2 vulnerability (CVE-2018-7600), which impacts all Drupal CMS versions of the past ten years.

Hackers immediately started to adapt it for their operations. Most of the attacks used it to infect servers with backdoors and cryptocurrency malware.

Cryptomining can also do good

“The Cryptojacking phenomenon is so widely spread that it is sometimes hard to tell whether a website is infected with malware or the mining code was genuinely added by the site owner,” Trustwave says in a report shared with BleepingComputer.

The practice of replacing revenue sources with cryptomining is particularly popular with smaller websites. In most countries, this is legal as long as visitors are duly notified that their computer’s resources are used for browsing an ad-free website.

Even larger organizations used this tactic to raise money to carry out their activities. Back in May, BleepingComputer reported about Unicef AU taking the decision to add the CoinHive in-browser miner into  TheHopepage.org, in the hope that visitors will donate some of CPU cycles for the cause.

Trustwave reached out to Make-A-Wish Foundation to alert them of the compromise, but received no response. Despite this, the cryptomining script is no longer present on their website.
