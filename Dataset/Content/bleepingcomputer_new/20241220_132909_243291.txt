
	   


For over 57% of the 150 cybersecurity professionals surveyed by Exabeam the concept of cryptojacking is not something they are acquainted with, while roughly 65% said that they are also unfamiliar with shadow mining.

The professionals were interviewed by the security information and event management (SIEM) company Exabeam at the 2019 Cloud and Cybersecurity Expo in London.

Shadow mining is a concept describing the process through which a malicious insider takes advantage of a company's computing resources to surreptitiously mine for cryptocurrency, while cryptojacking describes the exact same process but performed by a malicious actor not employed by the organization.

Exabeam also discovered after its London interviews that 47% of the surveyed professionals said that they are no convinced the cryptocurrency mining activity detection tools and policies they use are as effective as they should be



In addition, bring your own device (BYOD) threats (28 percent) and ransomware (40 percent) were seen as the two most high profile security threats for companies, with shadow mining (10 percent) and cryptojacking (9 percent) barely being mentioned as a security threat worth considering.

"It is eye-opening to learn that so many professionals still see their biggest IT threats coming from outside of their organizations," stated Barry Shteiman, VP, Research and Innovation at Exabeam. "Given the rise of cryptocurrencies, IT teams need to realize that shadow mining is a significant threat and should be given more consideration."

The fact that both cryptojacking and shadow mining can be quite profitable when using adequate computing resources should put them a lot higher on the list of threats faced by organizations, especially considering the readily available info one can find on the Internet with just a couple of mouse clicks.

"The results of the survey suggest that many organizations should be looking at technologies that monitor who is on their network, and what they are doing," Shteiman continued. "This is one the most effective ways of mitigating both internal and external security threats like shadow mining and cryptojacking."

Cryptojacking actively used in malicious campaigns

In related news, while cryptojacking experienced a downward trend with a 36% decline between January and December 2018 following the drop in value experienced by cryptocurrencies towards the end of last year, cryptojacking malware campaigns infected around ten times more organizations during 2018 than ransomware did according to a Check Point Research report.

The fact that cryptojacking is still in the arsenal of malicious actors was shown by a batch of eight apps found to be dropping malicious Monero cryptomining scripts on Microsoft Store users' computers and by hundreds of vulnerable and exposed Docker hosts being actively abused in cryptojacking campaigns.

Also, a new coin miner malware strain using the XMR-Stak Cryptonight cryptocurrency miner and a Backdoor Trojan dubbed SpeakUp that infects its victims with XMRig miners were detected while targeting servers running multiple Linux distributions in February 2019.

Even more recently, a modular malware with worm capabilities was discovered by 360 Total Security and observed while exploiting known vulnerabilities in ElasticSearch, Hadoop, Redis, Spring, Weblogic, ThinkPHP, and SqlServer servers, spreading between servers to mine for Monero (XMR).
