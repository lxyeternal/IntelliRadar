
	   


Cybersecurity firm Imperva disclosed today a security incident that led to data exposure affecting a subset of customers using its Cloud Web Application Firewall (WAF) product, previously known as Incapsula.

Imperva's Cloud WAF is a managed service designed to protect cloud services "against known and unknown threats, including all OWASP top 10 and zero-day threats."

The data exposure incident is limited only to the Cloud WAF As the company's President and Chief Executive Officer Chris Hylen said in a blog post published today.

The security breach was discovered on August 20, 2019, after a third party reported the data exposure affecting some Cloud WAF customers with accounts through September 15, 2017.

"Elements of our Incapsula customer database through September 15, 2017" were also exposed according to Hylen, including customer email addresses together with hashed and salted passwords.

Also, for some Incapsula customers through September 15, 2017, API keys and customer-provided SSL certificates were also exposed to third party access.

"We continue to investigate this incident around the clock and have stood up a global, cross-functional team," adds Hylen.

Forensics experts hired to help with the investigation

After discovering the security incident impacting some of their Cloud WAF (Incapsula) customers, Imperva took the following actions:


• We activated our internal data security response team and protocol, and continue to investigate with the full capacity of our resources how this exposure occurred.  
• We have informed the appropriate global regulatory agencies. 
• We have engaged outside forensics experts.
• We implemented forced password rotations and 90-day expirations in our Cloud WAF product. 
• We are informing all impacted customers directly and sharing the steps we are taking to safeguard their accounts and data, and additional actions they can take themselves.


Imperva also recommends the following measures to all of its customers "as a matter of good practice:"


Change user account passwords for Cloud WAF (https://my.incapsula.com) 
	Implement Single Sign-On (SSO) 
	Enable two-factor authentication
	Generate and upload new SSL certificate
	Reset API keys


Imperva's CEO did not disclose more info on what data was exposed or lost as part of this security breach or what third parties might have accessed the exposed info, if any.

BleepingComputer has reached out to Imperva for more details on this security incident but had not heard back at the time of this publication. This article will be updated when a response is received.

Update: An Imperva spokesperson told BleepingComputer that only "accounts up to September 15, 2017" were affected by the breach. Also, no other details are available given that this "is an ongoing investigation and the company is sharing only the information in that post."
