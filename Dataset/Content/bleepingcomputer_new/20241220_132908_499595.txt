
	   


 

The personal information of almost 5 million parents and 200,000 children were obtained after a hacker was able to access the servers of children's toy and gadget maker VTech. This information included names, email address, passwords, and addresses of parents as well as children's first names, genders and birthdays. The severity of this attack becomes even worse when the hacker told Motherboard this weekend that the breach also included pictures of children who use the VTech Kid Connect service and the chat logs between them and their parents.

VTech's tablets include a feature called Kid Connect that allows parents and their children to send text  messages, audio messages, and pictures to each other. For some reason,  VTech decided to store the logs and media that was texted instead of deleting it after it was received. The hacker told Motherboard.com that there were some messages almost a year old still stored on the VTech servers.

The hacker has also stated that using the information they obtained they would easily be able to link a kids information to their picture and information about their parents. Though the hacker says they have no plans on selling or releasing any of the stolen data, as you can imagine this information could be used for some seriously dangerous and scary purposes. 

At this time VTech has taken down the sites affected by this breach until the vulnerabilities are fixed. According to NBC News, VTech is also working with Hong Kong regulators to perform a "compliance check" to determine if VTech was properly safeguarding its data and what it plans on doing to protect it in the future.
