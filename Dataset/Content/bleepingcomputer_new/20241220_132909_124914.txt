
	   


The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) on Wednesday released an alert about phishing attacks targeting various government organizations to steal logins for the Small Business Administration COVID-19 loan relief accounts.

This campaign was observed at the end of July and targets Federal Civilian Executive Branch and state, local, tribal, and territorial government organizations.

In a newer phishing attack that started in August, security researchers saw the threat actor using convincing tricks to fool potential victims into providing personal and financial information.

The alert from CISA contains indicators of compromise that should help recipients detect the phishing attack and take active measures against it.

The malicious email has the subject line "SBA Application - Review and Proceed" and comes from the spoofed email address "disastercustomerservice@sba[.]gov."

A link in the email body promises to take the recipient to the account sign-in page on the SBA website. Credentials entered on this page end up with the attacker.



The fraudster uses multiple domains to host the phishing page, some of them with the top-level domain of Brazil. A downloadable list of IoCs in XML format is available here.

Jérôme Segura of Malwarebytes noticed this campaign, too, along with variations of it. Apart from stealing credentials, the researcher also saw that emails from the same spoofed address above delivered GuLoader, a piece of malware that loads other payloads.

This campaign occurred in April and it was the first wave of phishing targeting the SBA. The malware came attached in a small (1MB) IMG disc-image file to evade detection.



In another wave, Segura observed a higher level of social engineering, which was "convincing enough to fool even seasoned security experts."

The goal here was to trick recipients into filling highly personal information that included bank account details. These details could then be used to steal funds or launch an advance-fee scam, the lure being the SBA program.



Segura provides some simple technical tricks that could help users spot fake emails luring them into disclosing sensitive data or urging to visit a particular page.

Checking the source of the message for the sender address will reveal the real one. Simply comparing it with the legitimate email will show the fraud attempt.

Paying attention to the URL in the address bar should also ensure that you don't fall for a trick and are on the genuine page.

CISA recommends organizations include warning banners for messages from an external source. Even if the message bypasses email defenses, users may act with more caution.
