
	   
A Russian-speaking hacker has breached the servers of the US Election Assistance Commission (EAC) and is now selling access to these accounts.

The intrusion was detected by threat intelligence firm Recorded Future, which identified online chatter related to the hacker's auction of EAC accounts.

The hacker, going by the name of Rasputin, was selling access to over 100 accounts, including some with administrator privileges.

Hacker used SQL injection to breach EAC servers

Further evidence discovered by Recorded Future showed that Rasputin had gained access to EAC servers using an SQL injection flaw.

Recorded Future says it approached the hacker and verified the security breach. The hacker provided a series of screenshots depicting the EAC server backend, as proof of his intrusion.


Systems status report page (Image provided by Rasputin to Recorded Future)

US law enforcement agencies have been notified of the intrusion. The full extent of the EAC compromise remains unknown.

"It’s unclear how long the EAC vulnerability has been active," said Recorded Future's Andrei Barysevich, "however, it could have been potentially discovered and accessed by several parties independently."


EAC portal login page [image provided by Rasputin to Recorded Future]

The Election Assistance Commission plays a crucial role in the US election process. The Commission's responsibilities include:

Test and certify voting equipment
	Accredit testing laboratories
	Manage the National Mail Voter Registration Form
	Manage the Voluntary Voting System Guidelines
	Distribute information for voters
	Provide other resources to improve elections
	Manage federal funds
	Conduct financial audits
	Report to Congress every two years
Recorded Future said they didn't find evidence to link Rasputin with state-sponsored actors. All clues point to an opportunistic hack.
