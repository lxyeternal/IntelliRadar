
	   


The Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC) published today the official number of complaints and data security breaches reported to the Data Protection Authority (DPA) since the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) was enacted on May 25, 2018.

While 3,188 data breaches were reported to each EU member's DPA on average since the GDPR came into effect in the European Union, the Irish data watchdog says that it received an almost double 5,818 valid data security breach notifications.

The average number of breach reports per EU member state is based on the official stats collected by The European Data Protection Board and released by the European Commission on May 22 to mark "the anniversary of Europe's new data protection rules."

The new GDPR stats [PDF] released by the European Commission also show that 144,376 complaints were filed with data protection authorities in Europe over the last 12 months, while a total of 89,271 data breach notifications were recorded during the same period of time.

Since the GDPR enactment the European watchdogs issued fines of up to 4% each infringing company's annual turnover, with Google, for instance, being fined € 50,000,000 for lack of consent on advertisements in France, while a data brokering firm receiving a €220,000 financial penalty for "failing to inform citizens that their data was being processed by the company."

The Irish Commissioner for Data Protection Helen Dixon said that "The GDPR is a strong new platform from which we can all demand and drive higher standards of protection of our personal information."

"As the national supervisory authority, the Data Protection Commission (DPC) is firmly committed to its role in public enforcement of the new law, while also working hard to provide guidance to sectors as they seek to comply with the new requirements."



Just last week, the Irish DPC also announced that it has started investigating if Google's processing of personal data collected by the company as part of Ad Exchange online advertising transactions is breaching GDPR regulations.

During November 2018, multiple GDPR complaints were also filed against Google by multiple consumer groups for deceptive practices on tracking user location.

According to a DLA Piper report, 91 fines have been imposed under the GDPR regulations until the start of February 2019 across the EU, with the mention that not all of them were related to breaches of personal data.
