"We are rolling out a series of updates to Office 365 Message Encryption aimed at creating a more authentic OME email experience," Microsoft says on the Microsoft 365 roadmap.

"The capabilities rolled out in this update include improvements to reduce the likelihood of mail servers marking OME OTP mails as spam."

OTP emails sent to the junk folder

The OME online service is built on Microsoft Azure Rights Management (Azure RMS) and it enables Office 365 customers to send and receive encrypted email messages via Outlook.com, Yahoo!, Gmail, as well as other email services that come with encryption, identity, and authorization policies.

OME-encrypted emails sent by Office 365 users are delivered to their recipients as HTML attachments which required the recipients to authenticate to a viewing portal either by signing in with an Office 365 associated account or by entering a one-time passcode.

Those who don't have an account associated with Office 365 receive a limited-time web-view link that allows them to read the encrypted message, together with an OTP.

However, as Microsoft points out, some of these messages might land in the recipients' spam or junk folder due to the DKIM and DMARC settings used by their organizations.


OME encryption and decryption process (Microsoft)

Office 365 Message Encryption update highlights

In January, Microsoft enhanced the way emails encrypted with the Office 365 Message Encryption (OME) service are seen by mail servers by making it less likely that they were marked as spam and sent to the junk folder.

Redmond also updated the OME service during December with several new capabilities including support for PDF attachments and Shared Mailboxes, and Mac pre-licensing.

Starting with October 2019, Microsoft rolled out 'Unverified Sender', a new Office 365 feature designed to help users identify potential spam or phishing emails that land in their Outlook client's inbox.

Authenticated Received Chain (ARC) is also being enabled for all for Office 365 hosted mailboxes to boost anti-spoofing detection and to check auth results within Office 365, with this new capability currently being rolled out to all environments.
