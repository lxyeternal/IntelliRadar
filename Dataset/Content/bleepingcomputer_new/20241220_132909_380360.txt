
	   


Reporters from a Dutch television station said today they've discovered files from Europol investigations into possible terrorist suspects on an unprotected hard drive connected to the Internet.

Europol is the law enforcement agency of the European Union (EU) that handles criminal intelligence and combating serious international organised crime by means of cooperation between the relevant authorities of the member states.

Reporters found over 700 files from 54 different Europol investigations on a NAS (Network-attached storage) hard drive that was connected to the Internet without a password.

The data appears to have been exposed when a former employee had broken standard procedures and took work files home. An Europol spokesperson described the former employee as "an experienced police officer from a national authority."

Files exposed via Lenovo Iomega hard drive

This employee had apparently transferred the files to a Lenovo Iomega NAS hard drive, which he apparently forgot he had connected to the Internet.

The files weren't encrypted and ZEMBLA reporters say they contained information on past and current investigations.

Leaked information included names and phone numbers of suspected terrorists, but also details on past attacks such as the Madrid bombings, the Hofstad Network, and several attacks on airplanes with liquid explosives that Europol agents managed to stop.

Investigators aren't sure if someone else other than ZEMBLA reporters may have had accessed the NAS hard drive.

At the start of October, US authorities arrested Harold Thomas Martin III, age 51, of Glen Burnie, Maryland for taking home classified documents. Martin was an NSA contractor working for Booz Allen Hamilton, the same company where Snowden worked.
