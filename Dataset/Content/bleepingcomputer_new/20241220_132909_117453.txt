
	   


The CIA extortion scams continue to evolve in order to squeeze as much money out of a victim as they can. In a new variant discovered by researchers, the extortion emails are now selling alleged proof on Satoshi Box for $500 that show you are part of the CIA investigation.

Like other CIA extortion emails that have been popular lately, these scams pretend to be from a CIA technical collection office who noticed your name as part of an open investigation into underage pornography. The email goes on to state that the collection office can remove you from the investigation if you send them a payment of $5,000.

Yesterday we reported how these extortion emails were now putting the payment instructions into password protected PDFs. In a new variant discovered by Trustwave SpiderLabs, the emails contain password protected zip attachments, with the PDF instructions enclosed in them.

CIA Extortion Email

When the PDF opens, a user will be given instructions to transfer $5,000 USD to a listed bitcoin address by March 27th, 2019. What makes this variant interesting is that the PDF also includes a link to supposed evidence that the user is part of a CIA investigation. This link is titled" The evidence is posted here >>" and when clicked on will open a browser to an URL on Satoshibox.com.


PDF Instructions

Satoshibox is a service that allows users to upload digital content or information that is then released when a person purchases it using bitcoins.  In this extortion attempt, the attackers are selling this alleged evidence for $500.


Satoshi Box

Karl Sigler, Threat Intelligence Manager at Trustwave SpiderLabs, told BleepingComputer that they did not purchase the document in order to see what it contained, but felt it would have been nothing more than the continuation of the scam.

"Indeed we did not purchase the so-called evidence, but I imagine that payment would result in no further communication, or if there was it would be an attempt to milk the victim for even more money," Sigler told BleepingComputer.com.

As email extortion campaigns are constantly evolving in order to generate revenue in any manner they can, users must always be on guard and suspicious of any attempts to "sell" them something for cryptocurrency.

As I always say regarding these extortion attempts, while they may be scary, they are just scams. Numerous people have received them and the CIA is not investigating you.

If you receive an email stating it's from the CIA and they are trying to extort money from you, just hit the spam button and delete the email.
