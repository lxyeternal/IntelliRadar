
	   


British police are warning drivers to check their doors after they use their remote key to lock their car because thieves may be using jammers to block door locking signals, leaving the vehicles unlocked.

Thames Valley Police says that thieves have entered and stolen goods from 14 cars parked at motorway (highway) services stations.

"In many of the cases there was no obvious sign of a break in and no damage was caused to the vehicles as items were stolen from them," police said.

As the driver is inside the service station, thieves come by the car, open the door, and steal any valuable possessions left inside the car. Drivers reported that thieves stole wallets, cash, luggage, laptops, and others.

Thefts occurred during a four-day period in late November

All 14 thefts have been reported in a period of four days along motorway M4 in Berkshire, in Southeast England, at the Chieveley Moto Services, Reading Moto Services, and Membury Welcome Break.

Besides suggesting drivers check the car door by hand, UK police is also recommending that they take valuable objects with them.

Further, police have also put out an appeal for dashboard camera footage from other drivers who have visited the service stations. More details about the robberies and the dates are available on the Thames Valley Police website.

Technology used for car thefts in the past

In August, Houston police arrested two suspects on suspicion of stealing over 100 cars using only a laptop.

Also in August, a team of researchers from the University of Birmingham, the UK, found two flaws in the remote key system used by cars manufactured by the Volkswagen Group. Over 100 million cars were allegedly affected.

As cars get smarter, car thievery will evolve from using a crowbar and brick to laptops and radio jammers. Reports of car thieves using radio jammers have been around as early as 2014.
