
	   


Google added a new Admin console option for G Suite admins designed to help them disable telephony options as two-factor authentication (2FA) methods for G Suite accounts in their domain, preventing users from using SMS and voice codes when authenticating.

According to a G Suite Help Center support document, 2FA—also known as 2-Step Verification (2SV)—"requires users to verify their identity through something they know (such as a password) plus something they have (such as a physical key or access code delivered to a device). It’s also called multi-factor authentication (MFA) or 2-factor authentication (2FA)."

Once enabled for an account, 2FA (configured to work with text/voice message codes, the Google Authenticator app, or with hardware second factors like security keys) will protect it from unauthorized access by creating an extra defense designed to block malicious actors from logging in using stolen credentials.

"Cybercriminals are increasingly targeting small businesses. If a hacker gets into your administrator account, they can see your email, documents, spreadsheets, financial records, and more. A hacker might be able to steal or guess a password, but they can’t reproduce something only you have," also says Google.

Despite this, some 2FA methods are not recommended by Google, case in point the telephony options—text messages and voice verification codes—which rely on external carrier networks that can be intercepted by potential attackers.

Google recommends businesses to use security keys as part of their 2FA process, and the Google prompt or the Google Authenticator app as more secure alternatives when compared to phone-based verification methods.

The insecure 2FA options can now be disabled by G Suite admins for the entire domain from the Admin console:


As awareness of the potential vulnerabilities associated with SMS and voice codes has increased, some admins asked us for more control over the ability to use phone-based 2-Step Verification methods within organizations. The present release does just that - admins get a policy that can enforce the use of multi-factor authentication without permitting SMS and voice verification codes. 


By enabling this new G Suite policy on their domains, admins are able to boost the overall security of all user accounts and provide better security to all the associated data.

How to get started:

Admins: Apply the new policy by changing the setting at Admin console > Security > Advanced security settings > Allowed two-step verification methods. 
	End users: No action needed unless admin changes configuration. 

G Suite 2FA options

Seeing that after the 2FA voice and text verification codes will be disabled for the entire domain and the users who currently use them will be unable to sign in, Google provides admins with a detailed procedure designed to help them avoid login failures on the "Set up 2-Step Verification" support page.

Besides communicating the changes to all users before enabling them, together with an enforcement date, admins can also provide users with "extra time to enroll by putting these users into an exception group where 2SV isn’t enforced until they can add a 2SV method," as detailed in the "Avoid account lockouts when 2-Step Verification is enforced" document.

The new Admin console 2FA options are available to all G Suite editions but they will not be enabled by default, with admins having to "explicitly choose to apply this policy on a OU / Group basis, like the other existing 2SV enforcement policies."

Rollout details:


Rapid Release domains: Gradual rollout (up to 15 days for feature visibility) starting on March 14, 2019. 
	Scheduled Release domains: Gradual rollout (up to 15 days for feature visibility) starting on March 14, 2019 


In related news, as unearthed by a Proofpoint report released today, a large number of IMAP-based password-spraying attacks were used by malicious actors to successfully breach Microsoft Office 365 and G Suite accounts protected with multi-factor authentication (MFA).

The IMAP-based password-spraying method took advantage of the fact that IMAP is a legacy authentication protocol which bypasses MFA, making it possible for attackers to launch credential stuffing attacks against assets left unprotected.

According to Proofpoint's Information Protection Research Team, during a "recent six-month study of major cloud service tenants, Proofpoint researchers observed massive attacks leveraging legacy protocols and credential dumps to increase the speed and effectiveness of brute force account compromises at scale."

Additionally, around 60% of the total number of G Suite and Office 365 tenants which were monitored were targeted by the IMAP-based attacks, and approximately 25% of these were also successfully breached.
