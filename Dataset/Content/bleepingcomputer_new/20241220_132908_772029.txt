
	   


Google has made changes to Google Play Protect in order to better protect Android users from malicious apps. Will these protections, though, be enough?

Google Play Protect is a feature that launched in 2017 that aims to protect Android users from unwanted and malicious apps found in the Google Play store and through third parties. When activated, Google Play Protect will perform the following functions:

It runs a safety check on apps from the Google Play Store before you download them.
	It checks your device for potentially harmful apps from other sources. These harmful apps are sometimes called malware.
	It warns you about any detected potentially harmful apps found, and removes known harmful apps from your device.
	It warns you about detected apps that violate our Unwanted Software Policy by hiding or misrepresenting important information.
In a blog entry posted today, Google stated that Google Play Protect is a scanning engine that runs in the background that looks for malicious apps uploaded to the Play Store and protects over 2 billion devices every day.

"It is made up of a giant backend scanning engine to aid our analysts in sourcing and vetting applications made available on the Play Store, and built-in protection that scans apps on users' devices, immobilizing PHA and warning users."

Google has added some new features over the past year to Google Protect that they hope will do a better job of protecting Android users from what appears to be an endless amount of harmful and scam apps on Google Play.

To start, Google Play Protect will now be enabled by default for all Google Play users. You can confirm that Google Play Protect is enabled by going into the Play Store, tapping , and tapping Play Protect.


Status of Google Play Protect

Today's announcement also states that last year Google added a feature that will have Google Play Protect display a warning when users open a new or rare Android app. As Google Play Protect becomes more familiar with the app and there are no complaints against it, the warning will no longer display.

Android security researcher Lukas Stefanko has told BleepingComputer that he believes this is a machine learning algorithm for rarely seen apps in Google Play ecosystem.

"This looks like a ML algorithms to me that tries to make decisions - before opening app and if app was rarely seen in ecosystem."

Google has also recently enabled a new feature that displays a warning when a user is about to launch a potentially harmful app. It is not known why an app that could "damage your device, add hidden charges to your mobile bill, or steal your personal information" wouldn't just be blocked instead.


Google Play Protect Warning

Are these changes enough?

With the constant reports of malicious apps in the Google Play Store, one has to ask if these changes are enough to protect an Android user?

While using automated procedures to scan for, block, and warn users of malicious apps are always welcome, with the amount of malicious apps found in Google Play, it appears more needs to be done.

Whether it's the need for a more stringent review process or more manual reviewers is unknown, but it's clear something needs to be done.

While we would love to get the answers to these questions, Google has not responded to our queries in the past regarding the review process in the Google Play store.
