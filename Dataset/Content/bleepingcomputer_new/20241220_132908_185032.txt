
	   
Recently I was alerted by Djordje Lukic, a malware analyst for Zemana, of a new variant of the Shopperz adware. He told me that this variant now uses a rootkit to prevent the viewing and removal of the adware and that Zemana is able to detect and remove it.

A rootkit is a computer infection that that attempts to hide itself, other files, or computer data so that they cannot be seen by the user or operating system. It does this by intercepting requests by the operating system and reporting back what it wants, even if it's not true.

Obviously, I decided to install it on a test machine and see what it can do and was immediately greeted by injected advertisements in various web sites that I visited.


Shopperz injecting ads into Nytimes.com

Also when I tried to open one of the folders associated with the infection, I received an error message that the folder was not accessible because the parameter is incorrect.

To search for the presence of rootkits I started up GMER, which immediately detected that two drivers that were hidden from the operating system. This indicated that rootkit activity was present.


GMER Detecting Rootkit Hooks

Once I disabled both of those rootkit services and rebooted, I was then able to access the the C:\Program Files\Giolmujiwecder folder as shown below.


Folder after Rootkits Removed

Adware is getting out of control and it's baffling to me that there is no government agencies (Poke Poke -> FTC) doing anything about this. We have adware taking unauthorized screenshots, using WMI to hide their hijacking methods, performing man-in-the-middle attacks, installing cryptocoin miners, and now using rootkits.  Something needs to be done to rein in the activities of adware purveyors as we are crossing into the realm of full computer infections rather than just showing advertisements.
