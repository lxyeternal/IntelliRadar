
	   
Mobile security experts with Intel Security (McAfee) have discovered a rash of Android apps available through the Google Play Store that were stealing Instagram credentials and uploading the data to a remote server.

Following Intel's discovery, Google has removed these apps from the Play Store and has banned the developers. The names of these apps, pictured below, are: Insta Takipci Kazan, Takipci Keyfi, Free Takipci, FollowInsta, and Instalker.


Names of malicious apps (via Intel Security)

The apps promised free followers, better Instagram analytics, or a way to schedule posts and automate various account actions.

Instead, users installed an app that immediately asked the user to log in with his Instagram credentials, in order to sync the two apps, but secretly collected his username and password, sending the data to a remote server.

Network log showing the apps exfiltrate Instagram credentials (via Intel Security)

This is not the first time Instagram users have been targeted with this very same method.

In November 2015, Apple removed an app named "Who Viewed Your Profile - InstaAgent" from the App Store because of the same behavior.

Not even six months later, Google faced a similar incident and was forced to remove two apps named "Who Viewed Me on Instagram" and "InstaCare - Who cares with me?," also for stealing user Instagram credentials.

Crooks use these stolen credentials to take over accounts and post image ads. In the case of profiles with tens of thousands of followers, hijackers even attempt to extort the owner, giving him access back to his account for large sums of money.

These five apps targeting Turkish users are part of a trend. In recent yeas, the Turkish malware and cyber-crime scene has flourished, with an active hacktivist scene, numerous cases of ransomware originating from the country, and even of a mysterious DDoS-for-points game played by hacker groups.
