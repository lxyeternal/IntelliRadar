
	   


The Maryland Department of Labor (Maryland DoL) published a press release today explaining that sensitive information of roughly 78,000 customers including names and social security numbers was accessed by an unauthorized party.

Maryland's Department of Information Technology (Maryland DoIT) is currently investigating the data breach which exposed info stored "on the Literacy Works Information System and a legacy unemployment insurance service database."

So far, Maryland DoIT's investigation did not find any evidence that "any personally identifiable information was downloaded or extracted from Labor servers."

As disclosed by the department, the two impacted databases contained the following types of records:


The LWIS files impacted were from 2009, 2010, and 2014. These files possibly contained first names, last names, social security numbers, dates of birth, city or county of residence, graduation dates and record numbers. The files impacted on the unemployment insurance service database were from 2013 and possibly contained first names, last names, and social security numbers.


While no evidence of the exposed data being exfiltrated from the department's servers or that it was misused in any way, affected customers are being offered two years of free credit monitoring through Experian’s IdentityWorks and will also "receive a letter with information on how to sign up for the credit monitoring service."

Also, all affected clients can reach out to the Maryland DoL for more details on how to enroll in Experian’s IdentityWorks program using a dedicated hotline by calling 410-767-5899 or e-mailing dataincident.labor@maryland.gov between Monday and Friday, from 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM.

"We live in an age of highly sophisticated information security threats," said Acting Labor Secretary James E. Rzepkowski in a WMAR-2 News interview. "We are committed to doing all we can to protect our customers and their information."

"We strongly urge those impacted to be vigilant about unusual activity on their accounts, and to take advantage of the credit monitoring being offered by the state."

In related news, the Department of Human Services (DHS) in Oregon also notified over 640,000 of its customers in June that their personal health information was exposed to unauthorized access in a data breach.

The security incident occurred on January 8, when nine of the agency's employees fell for a phishing email that made it possible for the attackers to access their mailboxes.
