
	   

Gooligan evolution (via Check Point)

Android malware known as Gooligan has compromised and stolen login tokens from over one million Android devices, Check Point researchers announced today.

The malware was first seen in 2014, and initially, it didn't include the ability to steal Google login tokens.

Since it first appeared, the malware has been detected by different security firms under different names such as Ghost Push, MonkeyTest, and Xinyinhe. In Google reports, you'll find it referenced as Ghost Push, but we'll be using the name Gooligan for this article, as this is the name under which Check Point detects this threat.

Gooligan mostly used for adware and pay-per-install schemes

According to the security firm, this malware uses malicious apps hosted third-party app stores to infect users. Once Gooligan has a foothold on an infected device, it contacts an online command and control (C&C) server and downloads a rootkit package that gains boot persistence and also includes four or five Android exploits that root the device.

After getting root privileges, Gooligan installs apps from the Google App Store as part of affiliate pay-per-install schemes, gives fraudulent ratings to apps on the Google App Store, and installs adware that clicks on ads for the malware author's profit.


Gooligan modus operandi (via Check Point)

Gooligan steals Google login tokens

All interactions with the official Google Play Store app are possible because Gooligan steals the local Google account login token stored on every Android device.

This token allows the malware to take actions in the name of the user, even if Gooligan doesn't know the user's password. The token also allows the malware to access various Google services, such as Gmail, Google Photos, Google Calendar, and more.

Check Point has set up a special website that allows users to check and see if their email address associated with their Android device has been stolen.

According to statistics the security firm has obtained, most users affected by this recent version of Gooligan that steals login tokens are from Asia (57%), followed by the Americas (19%), Africa (15%), and Europe (9%).


Gegraphical spread of devices infected with Gooligan (via Check Point)

Check Point says that Gooligan makes around 30,000 new victims each day. This is three times the number Cheetah Mobile has mentioned about Gooligan infections in a report released in mid-October (Cheetah Mobile uses the term Ghost Push to describe Gooligan).

Adrian Ludwig, Director of Android Security at Google, has published an official statement on Google+ after Check Point released its report on Gooligan. Ludwig says that Google found more than 40,000 apps associated with Gooligan in 2015 and 110,000 more in 2016.

Recent Gooligan version that can steal login tokens appeared in mid-2016

According to historical data on Gooligan's evolution, the version that can steal account login tokens was released over the summer, after a half of year of inactivity, previous Gooligan campaigns going silent at the end of 2015.

Check Point says that it uncovered this new malware version via its mobile security product (Check Point ZoneAlarm) and because of the large number of users who complained on the Google Play Store about apps appearing out of thin air on their devices. The company's investigation led back to Gooligan.

At the time of writing, Check Point engineers say that the only way a user can remove the malware from their device is by re-flashing the Android OS, a very complicated operation that very few users can carry out on their own, without the help of a mobile software technician.
