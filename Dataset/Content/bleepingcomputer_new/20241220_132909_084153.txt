
	   


Canadian government sites used to provide access to crucial services for immigration, taxes, pension, and benefits have been breached in a coordinated attack to steal COVID-19 relief payments.

The online portal referred to as GCKey is a critical single sign-on (SSO) system used by the public to access multiple Canadian government services.

Over the weekend, the Office of the Chief Information Office of Government of Canada released a statement advising the public of the cyberattack the GCKey system had experienced.

Credential stuffing attacks involve the attackers trying previously leaked username-password combinations against another website in an automated fashion, in an attempt to find accounts that share the same credentials.

That is one of the reasons security professionals strongly advise against using the same username-password combination across different websites. Should one such website be compromised, the attackers would now be able to re-use the leaked credentials on others as well.

"Affected GC Key accounts were cancelled as soon as the threat was discovered and departments are contacting users whose credentials were revoked to provide instructions on how to receive a new GCKey," read the government statement.

Used by over 30 federal departments, GCKey also acts as an alternative access route for users signing-in to the Canadian Revenue Agency (CRA) systems.

The statement also acknowledged, approximately 5,500 CRA accounts have been targeted in the GCKey attack and in another recent “credential stuffing” attack aimed at the CRA.

No multi-factor authentication (MFA)

Those who frequent Canadian government websites would be familiar with many of them offering multiple sign-in possibilities. For example, My Service Canada allows account access via GCKey, your bank, or provincial accounts.


Canadian government services allowing sign-in via multiple routes

 

Similarly, the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) enables sign-in via either your GCKey or CRA account.

BleepingComputer can verify when accessing some departments, such as CRA or IRCC, GCKey does not have multi-factor authentication enabled in the workflow.

When signing-in from a new computer, the user would be asked a security question (e.g. pet's name), however, there is no mechanism prompting the user for a 2FA code, for example, to be sent via a text message (SMS).

The username, password, and security questions all constitute a single factor, "something you know."


GCKey Sign-in workflow without multi-factor authentication

In our tests, BleepingComputer also didn't find any security captchas in use. This could have made it possible for bots to conduct automated credential stuffing.

COVID-19 relief funds hijacked

Lack of multi-factor authentication means, an attacker getting their hands on a victim's GCKey or CRA account, via credential stuffing or identity theft can carry out sensitive government transactions.

Recently, as a part of a nationwide coronavirus relief effort, the Canada Emergency Response Benefit (CERB) was instated by the government. 

The benefit provided funds of up to CA$2,000 to eligible residents.

As can be observed on the CERB website, the screening questions can be answered in a certain way to access the system via the "My Service Canada" account which uses GCKey.


Canadian CERB website allows sign-in via My Service Canada (GCKey)​​​

This is something adversaries wasted no time in exploiting.

Reports of attackers gaining unauthorized access to Canadian residents' tax accounts to siphon funds have sprung up.


Source: Twitter

Another impacted resident, Farivar Ahmadzadeh of Toronto told CTVNews how fraudsters managed to take out CA$10,000 on his behalf and redirected the funds to rogue accounts. 

Whether this was an unrelated case of identity theft or facilitated via credential stuffing, the recent findings have exposed vulnerabilities in the GCKey system entrusted by Canadian federal bodies.

While multiple access routes create convenience for citizens, they also expand the attack surface attackers can prey on. All it takes is the weakest link in the chain being exploited which can have devastating consequences for everyone.

In the meantime, users should ensure their GCKey and CRA accounts have not been impacted and consider changing their passwords, as a safety measure. As stated in the government statement, "If you have immediate concerns, please call 1-800-O-Canada."
