This is especially interesting considering that Lazarus (also known as HIDDEN COBRA, Guardians of Peace, ZINC, and NICKEL ACADEMY) which became active during 2009 traditionally targeted only entities from countries that oppose the North Korean regime.

Some of its most successful attacks against prominent victims targeted American entertainment company Sony Pictures Entertainment Inc and a number of banks all over the globe, which netted the hacking group tens of millions of US dollars, getting away with at least $140 million after breaching Bangladesh Bank and Far Eastern International Bank of Taiwan.

Lazarus operation timeline

Multiple attacks against cryptocurrency exchanges and mining marketplaces were also attributed to Lazarus between 2017 and 2018 by Russian cyber-security vendor Group-IB, apparently netting the group more than $550 million.

This new operation that aims to compromise Russian organizations uses Office documents as the initial infection stage and, as part of the last stage, drops the group's KEYMARBLE backdoor Trojan.

According to a US-CERT malware analysis report, this Trojan is used by the APT for "accessing device configuration data, downloading additional files, executing commands, modifying the registry, capturing screen shots, and exfiltrating data."

The infection chain observed by the Check Point research team consists of three steps but, at some point in time, the threat group decided to skip the first and drop their backdoor faster by removing the second one: 

1. A ZIP file which contains two documents: a benign decoy PDF document and a malicious Word document with macros.
2. The malicious macro downloads a VBS script from a Dropbox URL, followed by the VBS script execution.
3. The VBS script downloads a CAB file from the dropzone sever, extracts the embedded EXE file (backdoor) using Windows’ “expand.exe” utility, and finally executes it.

After deciding to skip the second step, the attackers have modified the malicious macros in the Word document to directly deliver and execute the KEYMARBLE Trojan.


Infection flow

The Russian targeting Lazarus campaign uses malicious Office documents delivered as ZIP files, with a decoy PDF document named NDA_USA.pdf containing a StarForce Technologies NDA agreement, which is a Russian software company which provides copy-protection software.

This way, the initial malicious payload looks a bit more legitimate, tricking potential Russian victims into also opening the Word document loaded with the malicious macros that make possible to initiate the infection process.

To deliver the KEYMARBLE backdoor onto its targets computers, Lazarus will use compromised servers that store it in the form of CAB archives camouflaged as JPEG images. This also leads to a secondary "benefit" by lowering the anti-malware detection rate, "from five vendors to a mere two vendors, who detected this file as malicious on VirusTotal" for a given sample.


Sample detection rate

The KEYMARBLE backdoor is just one of the many other malware families Lazarus has in its arsenal, with the US-CERT having identified 15 different malware strains between May 12, 2017, and October 2, 2018.

This is definitely in line with what multiple security companies led by Novetta discovered as part of Operation Blockbuster, when dozens of malware families were detected and linked to Lazarus operations.


Lazarus malware toolset

As detailed in Check Point's analysis of this latest campaign attributed to Lazarus, once the KEYMARBLE backdoor is successfully deployed on a compromised machine, it will enter a loop waiting for one of 18 possible commands from the hacking group:

After the initial beacon the malware will enter an infinite loop where it will anticipate to get command codes from the server. These will be passed on to a dispatcher function, where each command will be handled by an appropriate handler. The command is received in two parts – first the server will send a message carrying the command’s data length, and only then it will issue the actual command code.

While historically Lazarus has demonstrated that destroying their tracks together with every other piece of data on the systems and networks it successfully breaches is not beyond their capabilities, so far the current campaign seems to only be focused on gathering information with the help of the KEYMARBLE malware.

Up to this date, Lazarus has been detected while carrying out highly complex operations within at least 11 countries around the globe according to FireEye.
