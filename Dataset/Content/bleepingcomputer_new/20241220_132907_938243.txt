
	   


An unusual new phishing campaign is probing email inboxes via attacks using the targets' company-branded Microsoft 365 tenant login pages to add more legitimacy to the scam.

The attackers are also using Microsoft's Azure Blob Storage and Microsoft Azure Web Sites cloud storage solutions to host their phishing landing pages, a common tactic used by phishers to trick their targets into thinking that they're seeing an official Microsoft login page.

Using Azure Blob Storage object storage solution [1, 2] to host their phishing pages allows them to take advantage of the fact that they will automatically get signed with an SSL certificate from Microsoft.

Automated scraping of company-branded resources

"[The campaign] utilizes a novel method of scraping organizations’ branded Microsoft 365 tenant login pages to produce highly convincing credential harvesting pages," detail researchers part of Rapid7’s Managed Detection and Response (MDR) services team.

They spotted the coordinated phishing attacks in mid-July after analyzing an incident affecting one of their customers and discovered that threat actors behind them go the extra mile by adding an automated email check for each of the potential targets.


Phishing email sample

The potential victims' emails are checked against huge lists of validated email addresses before redirecting them to the phishing forms, which allows the crooks to scrape their targets' company-branded tenant login pages containing custom backgrounds and banner logos, and have them "dynamically inserted into the phishing landing page."

"Further examination of the domains included in the validated email addresses points to a phishing campaign at least initially targeting a spectrum of industry verticals, including financial, insurance, medical, telecom, and energy," add the Rapid7 researchers.

Having their victims' company logo and branded background added to the phishing landing pages allows the crooks to "create a semi-targeted and rather convincing credential harvesting page tailored to the user’s organization."

Also, "in the case that a validated organization does not have a custom branded tenant page, the phishing kit is designed to utilize the default Office 365 background image."


Phishing kit's branded login page scraping API

Phishing campaign appears to still be active

The phishing kit for this campaign together with the entire infrastructure used by the attackers to validate their targets emails and to generate branded landing pages is still hosted on the phisers' domain.

The server timestamps for the lists of validated email addresses used as part of these attacks also hint at the campaign still being active seeing that the threat actors operating it are actively updating them—eight of them have been updated today, at different times.

The domain was registered during November 2018 and recently updated on July 24, 2019, with the hosting being provided by a Lithuanian provider, which matches a growing trend observed by Rapid7 during the last few weeks of attackers abusing Lithuanian infrastructure.


Updated email address lists

Companies targeted using Microsoft Office 365 should implement the following measures advises Rapid7, to make sure that their staff does not fall for this or other phishing campaigns specifically targeting Microsoft users:


• enable multi-factor authentication via Office 365 or a third-party solution for all employees
• enroll staff in phishing awareness training programs designed to help employees spot and report phishing attempts easier


Cloud storage regularly abused by phishers

Besides the novel usage of scraped enterprise-branded login pages, the phishing campaign spotted and analyzed by Rapid7 follows a much larger trend of cloud storage services being used to host phishing kits.

For example, Edgewave researchers found phishers abusing Microsoft's Azure Blob Storage in February with the end goal of stealing Microsoft and Outlook account credentials via—now highly common—very convincing landing pages secured with the windows.net domain's SSL certificates to appear legitimate.

MinervaLabs' researcher Omri Segev Moyal shared with BleepingComputer several custom Office 365 rules two months later, rules that can be used to block phishing attacks abusing Microsoft's Azure Blob Storage for landing page hosting.

However, it's not only Microsoft's cloud services being used by scammers to make their phishing pages look more legitimate. In April, phishing kits were discovered on the web-based GitHub code hosting platform, abusing the service's free repos to deliver phishing pages via github.io domains.

Cloudflare's IPFS gateway was also abused by phishers to secure their scams using TLS certificates issued by Cloudflare as BleepingComputer discovered back in October 2018.

Last but not least, another series of phishing attacks which strived to swipe Google and Facebook credentials from unsuspecting victim utilizing Google Translate as camouflage was observed by Akamai's Security Intelligence Response Team (SIRT) during early February.
