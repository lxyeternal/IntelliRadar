In November 2018, during the holiday shopping season, researchers from security firm Check Point noticed a marked increase in phishing scams promoting e-commerce related phishing sites. This year it's even worse, with a 233% increase over last year's holiday season and it's only the middle of November.

"12 months ago, in November 2018, we witnessed a significant increase in the amount of ecommerce related phishing websites being accessed directly, or from links sent via email. And this year we can already see a similar trend. With only half of November over and even before the peak of Black Friday and Cyber Monday, use of e-commerce phishing URLs has more than doubled since last November’s peak – in fact, it’s up by 233%," the Check Point researchers stated.

Increase since November 2018

As part of this research, fake lookalike sites were found that attempt to impersonate legitimate online retail sites, but offer significant discounts in order to incentivize people to make a payment or enter their credit card details.

Scamming a deal on Ray-ban sunglasses

One of the campaigns noticed by Check Point involved an email phishing campaign and an elaborate lookalike site that impersonated the legitimate Ray-Ban site.

This lookalike site was hosted on a domain named xwrbs[.]com, which was created on November 6th, 2019. Just one day later, Check Point observed it being used in phishing emails sent to thousands of users.

These phishing emails pretended to be a Black Friday promotion where consumers can purchase custom Ray-Ban sunglasses at 80% off the normal price.


Ray-Ban Phishing Email

If a user clicked on the enclosed link, they would be brought to the www.xwrbs[.]com website, which to almost any visitor would look like the real site.  While the domain was a bit strange, the site itself looks any other online retail store with fully functional product listings and checkout pages.


Ray-Ban Phishing Site

One strange thing that a user may notice is that when they go to checkout, the page requires them to make a payment via PayPal. This is a bit odd as most online stores for major brands accept credit cards and other forms of payments. 

Even still, many consumers may disregard that suspicion in the back of their head for a great deal on sunglasses.

When users make a payment, they will be brought to the legitimate PayPal site to enter their login credentials and confirm their purchase.

It is not known, though, if the scammers will steal the money and run or send the purchaser a pair of cheap knockoffs hoping that they will never know the difference.

Protecting yourself from phishing scams during the holiday

With Black Friday almost upon us and holiday shopping goes into high gear, it is important for consumers to be aware of scams and learn how to spot them.

To stay protected, Check Point offers the following suggestions:

If a deal is too good to be true, then it probably is. Any email that offers incredibly deep discounts on a popular item is most likely a bait-and-switch or some sort of scam.
	Be aware of lookalike domains that look similar to a brand's normal domain name, but may contain spelling errors or minor mistakes.
	Instead of clicking on links sent in emails, use search engines to find a brand's legitimate web site and browse the deals directly on their site.
It is a scary world out there, especially during the holiday season when even scammers want to cash in.

If you pay attention to emails and the sites that they lead to, even with all of these scams, you can still shop online and do it safely.
