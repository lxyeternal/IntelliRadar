
	   


The City of Albany, the capital of the U.S. state of New York, was hit by a ransomware attack on March 30, with city officials working over the weekend to respond to the incident.

At the time, the extent of the damages suffered by the city's computing systems is not yet known but, according to a press release available on the official site of the New York's Capital City, all city services will be available to the public except "Birth Certificates, Death Certificates, or Marriage Certificates."

Also, "All City employees will report to work during normal business hours on Monday, and City buildings will be open to the public at 12:00 p.m.  City Court services will operate during normal business hours."

Mayor Kathy Sheehan will also hold a press conference "on Monday, April 1st at 12:30PM at Albany City Hall to provide an update on the availability of city services" as detailed in a press release on the Albany's official website.

This is yet another attack in a series of ransomware cyber incidents impacting companies and cities from all over the world, with Norsk Hydro, for instance, one of the largest aluminum producers worldwide having been forced to switch to partial manual operations because of a LockerGoga ransomware attack.

Also, just last week, the parking garage of the Canadian Internet Registration Authority (CIRA) was also impacted by ransomware allowing employees to park for free, while a ransomware attack that hit the computers of Jackson County, Georgia, brought government activity to its knees until county officials paid the cybercriminals $400,000 to receive the file decryption key.

BleepingComputer has reached out to the City of Albany for comment but had not heard back at the time of this publication.

Update April 01 13:33 EDT: According to Times Union's Steve Hughes who attended the press conference held by Mayor Kathy Sheehan:


No evidence personal data was taken. Employees getting credit monitoring if they want it.
	City still evaluating system impacted, appears payroll services are affected. Employees tracking hours on paper. Won’t say if city paid ransom.
	Says city was alerted by IT system that was wrong, still tracking cost to city.


Update April 01 13:40 EDT: As detailed in a Facebook post made by Gregory McGee, Vice President of the Albany Police Officers Union (APOU):

APOU members currently do not have access to the scheduling system, departmental email, and any other service or program that operates by internet connection. This means you cannot see your schedule, request days off, and you cannot input overtime. What this all means for the residents of the City of Albany is that at the start of each shift, it is unknown who is working and what manpower the department has.[..]
Also, the attack appears to be affecting the computers in the patrol cars in terms of incident and accident reports. Calls for service may take longer than expected to complete due to the fact officers do not have the tools at hand to provide the appropriate level of service. One has to ask the question of why a police department with sensitive information is on the same network that was so easily attacked.
