19-year-old Santiago Lopez known as ​@try_to_hack was the first one to surpass the target of $1 million in earnings on HackerOne in March 2019.

He has a history of 1676 security flaws found in the online assets belonging to companies like Twitter, HackerOne, Automattic, Verizon, private companies, and even to the U.S. government.

This brings the total to six researchers having earned over $1 million in bounties since the platform started connecting penetration testers and security researchers with businesses all over the world.



$62 million in bounties paid in total

The hackers earn monetary rewards each time they report a security weakness in the software of organizations that run bug bounty programs on the HackerOne platform.

"Every five minutes, a hacker reports a vulnerability. Every 60 seconds, a hacker partners with an organization on HackerOne," says HackerOne. "That’s more than 1,000 interactions per day with hackers and companies or governments working towards a safer internet."

HackerOne’s 2019 Hacker-Powered Security Report also published today with findings based on over 123,000 resolved security issues, more than 1,400 customer programs and over $62 million in bounties paid to hackers from over 150 countries shows that more severe vulnerabilities are being discovered than ever before.

Seeing that more than 25% of all found flaws were classified as high to critical severity in the past year alone and that the bounty is bigger the more severe the flaw, hackers are earning more and more money.



$21 million in bounties paid last year

The average critical vulnerability bug bounty paid to researchers increased 48% over last year’s median across all involved industries to $3,384; up from $2,281 which represents a 71% jump over the 2016 average of $1,977. 

"The most competitive programs today like Google, Microsoft, Apple, and Intel offer individual bounty awards as high as $1,500,000 for critical issues," says HackerOne.

In total, security researchers that reported vulnerabilities through HackerOne bug bounty programs earned $21 million in the past year, representing an increase of $10 million over the previous year.
