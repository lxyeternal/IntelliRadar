
	   


A new phishing campaign is underway that utilizes EML attachments that pretend to be a received voicemail and prompts you to login to retrieve it. This campaign also uses a clever tactic of tricking you into entering your password twice in order to confirm that you are providing the correct account credentials.

According to research shared with BleepingComputer by email security firm EdgeWave, a phishing campaign is underway that pretends to be a voicemail received through RingCentral. 

These emails are using subjects such that include "Voice:Message", "Voice Delivery Report", or "PBX Message" and an example of one of these phishing emails can be seen below.

Fake Voicemail Message Phishing Email

An interesting aspect of this campaign is that they use EML attachments that pretend to be a delivery notification from RingCentral and contain Preview, Listen, and Save Audio links that all go to the same URL.

By using EML attachments, when a user clicks on them in Outlook they will be displayed as a preview in the Outlook client rather than being opened in their own Window as shown below. This makes it easier to coerce users into clicking on the links.


EML previewed in Outlook

In a phishing sample provided by EdgeWave, the links in the email would link directly to the phishing sites. In a later sample discovered by BleepingComputer, the campaign has switched to shortened http://x.co/ links as shown below.


Shortened Link

When a user clicks on the Preview, Listen, or Save Audio links they will be brought to a web page that pretends to be a Microsoft Account login that prompts you to login.


Fake Microsoft Account Login Page

An interesting feature about this campaign is that it will always state you entered the wrong password and prompt you to enter it again. They do this to trick you into entering your password a second time to confirm your password for the phishers.


Stating password was incorrect

EdgeWave has confirmed BleepingComputer's suspicion that this is being done to double-verify the password and that it is not currently a common practice. Phishing expert NullCookies also told BleepingComputer that only a "subset of kits do that". 

NullCookies also stated that continuously "showing an incorrect password alert can also be used to avoid redirecting to the impersonated company’s website." This gives the phishing scam additional concealment.

After entering your password a second time, the phishing page will play a mp3 recording of a voicemail. Doing so adds a degree of legitimacy to the email in order to prevent people from becoming too suspicious.


Fake Voice Mail Message

The actual voicemail played by the phishing campaign can be listened to below. Using this sample is actually clever as it does not contain a phone number that a recipient can call and is very generic in nature. Users who fall for this phishing campaign may just shrug it off as a wrong number.



As always, if you receive an email that contains links or attachments, always contact the sender before opening them. If you happen to click on an enclosed attachment link, always be sure to check the address in the browser's address bar to see if it looks suspicious.

If anything looks even the slightest bit off, close the browser and do not continue. If you have fallen for this phishing scam, make sure to change your passwords at an accounts that use the one you entered.
