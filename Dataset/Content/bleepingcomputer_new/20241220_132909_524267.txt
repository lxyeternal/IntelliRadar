The fraudsters used typosquatting to achieve their purpose - a technique by which an attacker uses a domain name - and sometimes interface, similar to that of a popular website but it has a typing error in it.

Spoofing Blockchain.com allows the perpetrators to capture the login credentials of cryptocurrency users and steal the funds in their wallets on the platform.

Bad links promoted through Adwords

"The investigation has grown from a single report of £17k worth of bitcoin stolen from a Wiltshire-based victim to a current estimate of more than four thousand victims in at least 12 countries. We expect that number to grow," reads the press release from the U.K. agency.

SW RCCU Detective Inspector Louise Boyce told ISMG that there is evidence indicating that the crooks used Google Adwords to promote links leading to the fraudulent copy of Blockchain.com.

The criminal group was formed of five men and one woman and they were arrested on Tuesday by SW RCCU and the Dutch police at their homes in Charlcombe and Lower Weston in Bath, Staverton in Wiltshire, and Amsterdam and Rotterdam in the Netherlands.

Law enforcement agencies seized a large number of electronic devices and equipment. Helping with the search in the U.K. were two dogs specially trained to sniff electronic gear such as computers and storage drives.

Europol also released information about this operation and said that it was the result of a 14-month investigation into cryptocurrency theft.

Cybercriminals rely on victims clicking on links without carefully analyzing them. This is why phishing continues to be a problem.

Users can avoid falling for thee typosquatting trick by checking if the domain name in the address bar is the correct one or by typing in the URL themselves instead of following links from potentially malicious sources.

This advice is particularly recommended when accessing online services for financial transactions.
