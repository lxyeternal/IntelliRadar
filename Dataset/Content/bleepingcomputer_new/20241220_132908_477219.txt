
	   


The U.S. Justice Department today filed a civil forfeiture complaint aiming to seize control of 280 Bitcoin (BTC) and Ethereum (ETH) accounts containing funds allegedly stolen by North Korean hackers in attacks against two unnamed cryptocurrency exchanges.

Based on information contained within a civil forfeiture complaint in rem filed today, the first attack took place in July 2019 when the North Koreans hacked one exchange and stole more than $272,000 worth of cryptocurrency and Proton Tokens, PlayGame, and IHT Real Estate Protocol tokens.

In September 2019 the North Korean threat actors hacked a U.S.-based crypto exchange, gaining access to its crypto wallets and to funds owned by its partners.

		The Justice Department says that the funds stolen in these two crypto exchange hacks and the $250 million stolen after hacking another exchange in 2018 were all laundered by the same Chinese Over-The-Counter (OTC) traders.

DPRK hackers are also tied to the theft of another approximately $48.5 million worth of cryptocurrency from a South Korean-based exchange during November 2019.

Two of the Chinese OTC traders were charged by the Justice Department and sanctioned by the Treasury for laundering more than $100 million worth of cryptocurrency for the North Korean hackers in March 2020.

They received $91 million from DPRK-controlled accounts to launder as part of the first batch of currency in April 2018 and an additional $9.5 million after the hackers were able to steal more funds from a second exchange.


Connections between North Korea and the two hacks (US DoJ)

The United Nations estimated last year in a confidential document that North Korea generated as much as $2 billion from at least 35 cyberattacks targeting banks and cryptocurrency exchanges across 17 countries.

Another U.N. report from 2019 said that North Korean hackers hitting Asian cryptocurrency exchanges between January 2017 and September 2018 were behind $571 million in financial losses.

"This revenue allows the North Korean regime to continue to invest in its illicit ballistic missile and nuclear programs," the Justice Department said.

The U.S. Treasury also signed sanctions against three North Korean hacking groups (Lazarus Group, Bluenoroff, and Andariel) engaged in stealing and financial assets to the government of North Korea.

"Today’s action publicly exposes the ongoing connections between North Korea’s cyber-hacking program and a Chinese cryptocurrency money laundering network," Acting Assistant Attorney General Brian C. Rabbitt of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division said.

"This case underscores the department’s ongoing commitment to counter the threat presented by North Korean cyber hackers by exposing their criminal networks and tracing and seizing their ill-gotten gains."
