Bloom will appear in a Canadian court today after RCMP officers charged him on December 22, last year, on charges of trafficking in identity information, unauthorized use of a computer, mischief to data, and possession of property obtained by crime.

Canadian police said they began investigating Bloom in 2016 after they learned that LeakedSource was being hosted on servers located in Quebec.

LeakedSource made around $247,000

The LeakedSource website launched in late 2015, was heavily advertised on HackForums, and came to infamy by the middle of 2016.

LeakedSource allowed anyone to buy a subscription and search through a compilation of data breach files. Users could search for another person's username, email, name, and other details. For some data breaches, LeakedSource allowed paying customers to view cleartext passwords.

The tool was crucial for low-skilled hackers as it allowed them to hack into accounts of users who reused passwords.

The site lived by gathering files leaked by hackers and adding them to its search engine. In past conversations with this reporter, the LeakedSource crew revealed they were actively attempting to break hashed passwords into their cleartext versions so they could be added to the site's search index.

RCMP officials say this business scheme allowed LeakedSource to earn around $247,000 from trafficking personal account information.

The site also became famous because it became the place where people would also found out about some breaches, even before the hacked websites would know about it. LeakedSource broke the news about breaches at sites such as Last.fm, VK, LinkedIn, Mate1, Shadi, Weebly, and others.

LeakedSource went down in January 2017

The service abruptly shut down in late January 2017. Some news publications claimed the FBI raided LeakedSource and seized its servers, but these articles were based on a post on a small-time gaming forum and were never proven to be true. The site claimed to contain more than three billion breached records when it shut down.

Even before the service went down, many websites popped up, trying to imitate LeakedSource's business model. Many LeakedSource clones appeared after the service shut down, many of which are still available today.

A Brian Krebs article claimed a US man Jeremy Wade was behind the service. This may still end up to be true, as LeakedSource always described itself as a team in conversations with this reporter.

The service has always been criticized because it granted access to the personal data and passwords of other persons, unlike Have I Been Pwned, a service that allowed users to search for their own data after proving their identity. This was the reason cited by RCMP officials for starting their investigation into LeakedSource.

On December 2, 2017, LeakBase, a site similar to LeakedSource, shut down and started redirected users to Have I Been Pwnd.
