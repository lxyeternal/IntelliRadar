The exposure lasted for six days in November and was caused by a bug in the Google+ People application programming interface (API), specifically built to allow access to profile data with the owner's consent.

Private data of users that did not give consent to apps to get their profile information was also exposed if they shared it with a profile that permitted such an app to access their profile details.

Google discovered the privacy-invading glitch following its routine testing procedures. David Thacker, VP of Product Management for G Suite, says that the issue was introduced via an update and it was found and fixed after a week.

In an announcement today, he stresses that the privacy gaffe was not the result of a compromise from a third party and that Google has no evidence "that the app developers that inadvertently had this access for six days were aware of it or misused it in any way."

Google's notification makes it clear that app developers using the People API could not view financial data, national identification numbers, passwords, or details that could be used to commit fraud or identity theft.

The Google+ People API offers access to the entire profile data that includes name, email address, occupation, age, skills, gender, birthday. A complete list of details is available on the page for the Google+ People API.

Google has started to contact the affected parties (consumers and enterprise customers) about the issue.

Google+ gets an early retirement

Google's social media platform could never rise to expectations and was already scheduled for termination next year in August.

With this new API bug, the second one since October, the company decided to rush the retirement of the platform to April 2019, while all Google+ APIs will shut down in the next 90 days.

This time should be enough for users to migrate to other platforms, but chances are many will still be taken by surprise come the retirement day.

To avoid this for as many users as possible, Google will continue to provide additional information and instructions on how to safely and securely migrate their data on the platform.

Enterprise users won't have to go through the same process because Google+ will still be available to them via the G Suite service.
