Security researchers track this group under different names (TA407, Cobalt Dickes, Mabna Institute). They all agree on its connection with the Iranian government and that its purpose is to steal intellectual property from universities across the globe.

Its phishing campaigns are more frequent from June through October and primary targets are universities in North America, many of them in the United States, and Europe.

In a report today, researchers at cybersecurity company Proofpoint highlight that Silent Librarian's wording in phishing emails has evolved over time.

The theme, though, remains largely the same: losing access to library resources being the most prevalent. Expiration of the account is a common lure.



The style in the message above is a classic for Silent Librarian, as researchers saw it used for years.

In another phishing message observed by Proofpoint the attacker sent a fake notification regarding lack of activity, which allegedly led to closing the account.

The victim was instructed to log into their library account to keep having access to the library resources. The language in the message looks pretty convincing, providing contact details if the sign in process did not go smoothly.



To make the emails credible, the attacker used branding images from the target university. A facade of legitimacy is further maintained by keeping track of the changes in authentication portals almost in real time, the researchers say, and integrating them in the phishing pages.

One example are weather notification banners, which have a short validity period. In tha cloned login page below Silent Librarian relied on a spoofed domain name, stolen branding, and an accurate weather forecast warning.



The methods used by this threat actor are far from sophisticated, but the continuation of these campaigns proves that they are efficient for the goal set by Silent Librarian.

The activity is ongoing despite the US Department of Justice charging nine hackers last year for this activity.

The indictment says that the hackers were either hired or affiliates of the Mabna Institute, a company in Iran that carried out coordinated cyber intrusions at the direction of an intelligence agency in the country.

The purpose was to steal intellectual property from universities and sell it through multiple websites.

In a report in September, researchers at SecureWorks said that Silent Librarian impacted at least 380 universities in over 30 countries. In July and August alone, the group targeted more than 60 universities.


"While other actors are more sophisticated in their obfuscation, infrastructure, and methodology, Silent Librarian remains both prolific and effective at compromising new accounts and stealing credentials that can be used for financial gain." - Christopher Dawson, Threat Intelligence Lead, Threat Operations at Proofpoint


They continue to use the Freenom service to register domain names for free, as noticed in a campaign in September when 16 .tk and .cf domains were used. A full list of domains observed since January is available in Proofpoint's report.

A typical Silent Librarian attack flow starts with creating a shortened URL using an account from a compromised university. This is sent to recipients from another university, who are expected to be users of the authentication portal.



Higher education institutions are the main target of this threat actor. They are recommended to implement and enforce two-factor authentication to make it significantly difficult for hackers to access protected resources.
