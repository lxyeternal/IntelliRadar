
	   


Netography, a self-governing network security platform designed to function as an enterprise security crow's nest received today $2.6 million in funding from Andreessen Horowitz as part of its seed round.

The startup company strives to allow security and network teams to work smarter by taking advantage of the huge quantities of real-time data network devices generate on a regular basis to automatically identify and block malicious traffic as a network defense measure.

More exactly, Netography implements an autonomous network security mechanism and telemetry tools that make it possible to stop security threats which usually go unnoticed.

Lyon previously founded Prolexic, acquired by Akamai

“After years of studying the evolving landscape, and building so many platforms, we realized the answer was right in front of us,” said Barrett Lyon, CEO of Netography.

“Flying blind from both a network operations and security perspective is almost criminal in this day and age of so much information. The era of ‘capture everything’ is in the past and now we can remotely solve these problems that first generation network traffic analyzers, DLP, and SIEM tools have struggled to address," also remarked Lyon.

DDoS mitigation pioneer Barrett Lyon is Netography's co-founder and CEO, while Dan Murphy is the CTO and the other company co-founder, both of them well-known anti-DDoS and network security experts.

As detailed in the press release, "Lyon and team founded Prolexic Inc., acquired by Akamai Technologies, Inc, technology that now powers much of the industry’s DDoS protection. He subsequently went on to found BitGravity (acquired by Tata Communications), XDN (acquired by Fortinet), and Defense.net (acquired by F5 Networks)."

Netography's official equity funding stage will allow the company to support its business until it will be ready for subsequent seed stages or until it will be able to generate its own cash.

Launch during the second quarter of 2019

The seed money can also be put to use for various preparatory operations such as product development and market research designed to improve its operations later on and to make it more attractive for future investors.

“No one understands networks, how they act, the information they provide, and how to protect them better than Barrett and his team,” said Martin Casado, Andreessen Horowitz General Partner, and software-defined networking pioneer.

“They have been ahead of the curve in securing evolving infrastructure for the past 20 years. Now they are completely flipping the security model on its head. Netography is making network devices once only managed by people smarter and capable of self-correction,” also stated Casado.

According to the press release, the Netography platform will be launched during the second quarter of 2019 and members of the Netography team, including the CEO, will be available for demos at the upcoming RSA Conference 2019, March 4-8 in San Francisco.
