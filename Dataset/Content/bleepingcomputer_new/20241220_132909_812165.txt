
	   


American Land Title Association (ALTA) published a warning about hundreds of title company records having been supposedly stolen as part of a phishing campaign targeting ALTA members.

ALTA is the U.S. a national trade association and it is representing over 6,000 title insurance firms, title and settlement agents, real estate attorneys, and independent abstracters since 1907 when it was founded.

"A person claiming to be an ethical hacker contacted ALTA via Twitter and provided files that contain approximately 600 data entries consisting of domain identification, IP addresses, usernames and passwords. The data contains information for non-title companies as well," says ALTA's warning.

However, as the national trade association added:


There is no indication the data comes from a specific system breach. There are no signs that the credentials are still active or how they were obtained. We believe this person is also contacting individuals and companies they can identify from the data.


ALTA advises possibly affected companies to monitor their systems for unauthorized access and to immediately alert their IT departments as soon as they have any suspicions of data being collected and exfiltrated by bad actors.

This would allow in-house IT experts to implement an "information security program and response plan" as explained by ALTA, in an effort to protect the companies' data and systems from data theft and leaks. 

As ALTA further recommends, potentially impacted firms can protect their systems from future attacks by:


scanning your systems and devices to make sure that you are free from malware
	update or patch your software and operating systems
	require your staff to update and change system passwords, especially those containing customer information and banking services


Suspicious emails received by ALTA members should be reported to the Federal Bureau of Investigation Internet Crime Complaint Center, with any further details about this data theft incident to be sent to ALTA's staff by emailing the association at vulnerabilities@americanlandtitleassociation.org.

ALTA previously alerted its members of a phishing attack during late-May that used emails appearing to come from the association, featuring a "Changes & Updates to Member Directory" subject line, and asking to review a malicious PDF attachment.

"ALTA encourages you delete the email. Do not open the PDF. In addition, you should block the domain of the email or the IP address that it is coming from. Once the scammers catch on, they will likely switch email domains," also said the May phishing alert.

The association also published a tutorial on how company employees can identify phishing emails, providing them with tips on how to quickly recognize suspicious emails by looking for misspellings, bad formatting and missing images, urgent requests for sensitive info, dubious attachments, as well as checking hyperlinks before opening them by hovering over them to see a preview of the URL they link to.


Pishing email posing as legitimate ALTA message 

The fraud alert issued by ALTA in December 2018 regarding LLC identity theft stands witness on the potential consequences of what cybercriminals could do with stolen title company data.

As part of that fraud scheme, the crooks would "obtain information about a loan in default or already in foreclosure" which allowed them to "create and record fraudulent instruments related to the loan in the land records."

"The fraudulent instruments may vest title to the perpetrators, which can allow them to sell or refinance the property," as explained by ALTA.

Update July 09: ALTA sent the following update to the initial alert:


The information sent to ALTA offers no indication of (1) how the credentials were obtained, (2) whether the credentials are current or active and (3) if the credentials provide access to any individual company networks, software applications, online email systems or other credential-based products or services.

Our analysis found:

182 unique email addresses (note that some email addresses are listed more than once with different associated passwords).
	154 unique domains (note that some domains were listed more than once with different associated email addresses).
If your company domain was found, ALTA will call your company's primary contact within the next 48 hours.
