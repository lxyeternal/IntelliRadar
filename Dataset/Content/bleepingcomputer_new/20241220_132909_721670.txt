
	   


Hackers kept busy this weekend exploiting vulnerable Salt instances used in various infrastructures for server management and automation.

Among the organizations that announced an intrusion are LineageOS, Vates (creators of open source Xen Orchestra), Ghost blogging platform, and DigiCert. Hundreds of servers, both masters and clients (minions) have likely been compromised at this point.

Bugs and exploit code are public

Salt versions before 3000.2 and 2019.2.4 are vulnerable to CVE-2020-11651 and CVE-2020-11652. F-Secure disclosed the two vulnerabilities last week saying that “any competent hacker” would need less than 24 hours to develop a 100% reliable exploit.

“This unintentional exposure provides a remote un-authenticated attacker with root-equivalent access to the salt master,” F-Secure explains.


CVE-2020-11652 is a path traversal vulnerability that allows reading files outside the intended directory. Combined with CVE-2020-11651, it allows an unauthenticated attacker to read and write access anywhere and to steal the secret key to authenticate to the master server as root.

Because exploit code is trivial to create, the cybersecurity company refrained from publishing it, to protect companies that are slow to patch. However, several versions have emerged in the public space (1, 2, 3, 4), which typically means that more attacks are to come.

In two days since the attacks started, over 120 comments have been posted on the Salt’s bug page, many describing signs of an intrusion and how to stop the illegal activity on the server.

It is worth mentioning that killing the attacker’s processes may not be enough and the server may need to be reinstalled. Immediate patching is recommended; Salt can be set up to get updates automatically from the official SaltStack repository.

Where this is not possible, F-Secure says that network security controls should be set up to restrict access to the salt master server (on default ports 4505 and 4506). Blocking access from the public internet is also recommended.

The US CERT also warned on the severity of the issues and beat the drum for updating Salt "ASAP to protect your cloud-based servers!" SaltStack provides tips for securing and hardening Salt environment.


source: US-CERT

Compromised targets

In some cases, exploiting the recent Salt vulnerabilities is part of a cryptocurrency mining campaign from Kinsing/H2Miner botnet that focuses on cloud environments. The miner (XMRig) runs under the names “salt-minions,” “salt-minions-bak,” and “salt-store.” There at least two samples, one of them with a low detection rate (1, 2).

In a tweet today, malware analysis company Intezer confirmed that H2Miner is attacking SaltStack instances using the two bugs reported by F-Secure. They said that it used to be hosted on Bitbucket and is not too different from previous samples.

LineageOS intrusion

The attack against the LineageOS main infrastructure, a custom Android operating system, happened on Saturday evening and forced admins to take offline all services. In the following hours, the team worked on assessing the damage and restoring them.

At the time of writing, only two services remain affected, stats and builds, although the latter had been paused since April 30 due to an unrelated issue, so hackers did not impact them. The end goal for this incident remains undisclosed.



DigiCert breach

Jeremy Rowley, Executive Vice President of Product at DigiCert, on Sunday reported that hackers compromised the Certificate Transparency Log 2 key used for signing certificate timestamps (SCTs).

“We are pulling the log into read-only mode right now,” Rowley informed. There is no indication that the attacker used the key on SCTs, most likely because they did not know they could access it. Instead, they started to run other services on the server.

Even so, Rowley said that any SCTs coming from that log after the time of the breach are suspect and the log should be removed from the trusted list. He also detailed that the hackers were able to access the CT log 2 because it was legacy infrastructure that did not run on a private network at the time it was deployed.

CT log 2 was scheduled for shut down on May 1. It received an extension because some clients still needed it. Its legacy status also means that it was operated separately from other CT logs.

Accessing the CT did not compromise the certificates since logs are operated on a separate environment than Certificate Authority operations.


source: Jeremy Rowley

Ghost blogging platform

The blogging platform Ghost is another victim of hackers exploiting Salt vulnerabilities, and its services went down. On Sunday, it informed that the breach affected Ghost (Pro) and Ghost.org billing services.

The investigation revealed that credit card information was not impacted and that there was “no direct evidence that private customer data, passwords or other information has been compromised.”

After identifying the security issue and its impact on Ghost services, the platform informed that the purpose of the attack was cryptocurrency mining. “The mining attempt spiked CPUs and quickly overloaded most of our systems, which alerted us to the issue immediately,” reads the notification.

Ghost says that it removed all traces of the coin miner, its systems are currently stable, and no additional issues were discovered.

Xen Orchestra

On Sunday night, the hackers exploited Salt vulnerabilities to make another victim, Xen Orchestra, a platform that provides tools to administrate Citrix Hypervisor (XenServer) and get a complete overview of the infrastructure.

The attack started after a subset of services on the infrastructure became unreachable almost at the same time. Another symptom was high CPU‌ usage, reads the security report for Xen Orchestra.


source: Xen Orchestra

The purpose of the intrusion was to run a coin miner on virtual machines via a rogue salt-minion process, the company found. Digging deeper, the researchers discovered that the payload was a version of ‘salt store,’ which runs “pretty basic” tasks specific for more efficient mining and it was not persistent.

These organizations are just a few examples of victims of the two Salt vulnerabilities published by F-Secure. Coin mining seems to be the main goal of the threat actor but more insidious payloads could be used instead.

According to Censys search engine for hosts and networks available online, there are currently more than 5,000 SaltStack servers exposed on the public internet that are potentially vulnerable to the two bugs already exploited bugs. So hackers have sufficient fodder unless Salt patching is accelerated.
