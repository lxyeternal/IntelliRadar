
	   


The servers of Baltimore City Hall and Amarillo, TX, Potter County were hit by ransomware attacks, with the former having shut down most servers while the latter already got some of its computing systems back online.

Some of the Baltimore City Hall government servers have been infected with an unknown ransomware strain which is reportedly spreading to other computers on the network according to CBS Baltimore.

Baltimore City core essential services (police, fire, EMS and 311) are still operational but it has been determined that the city’s network has been infected with a ransomware virus. City employees are working diligently to determine the source and extent of the infection. At this time, we have seen no evidence that any personal data has left the system. Out of an abundance of precaution, the city has shut down the majority of its servers. We will provide updates as information becomes available,


Another announcement from the city's Director of Public Works says that late water bill fees for both City and County customers have been suspended because of the ongoing network issues, while the City’s Finance Department can no longer accept cash payments. 

The just sworn in City Council president Brandon Scott also issued a comment on the current situation which closely resembles the one released by the Mayor's Office, also mentioning an ongoing investigation conducted by city employees.

At the moment there are no specific details on how the ransomware got into the city's computing system or on the ransom amount the attackers have demanded.

This is the second time in less than a year that Baltimore was hit by a ransomware attack, with the city's emergency call system being disrupted on March 28, 2018, and the staff having to switch to manual operations to handle all incoming emergency calls as detailed by NBC NEWS.

Potter County ransomware attack

As previously mentioned, Potter County already managed to get some of its computers back online, after it was impacted by a malware attack on April 22 according to NewsChannel 10.

Following the attack, all 550 employees were forced to use only paper and pencils since the entire computer network was shut down and off limits.

"This is what we’re using now. Paper and pencil, we’re going old fashion around here. Seriously, that’s what we’re having to do," said Potter County Sheriff Brian Thomas at the time. "All of our calls that we’re taking and all of our reports that we’re doing we’re having to do by paper and pencil."

"We had two viruses that hit the county. They are working on it now, we’ve called in some extra people to help," stated Potter County Judge Nancy Tanner. "We’re going to work on that and hopefully get it up by the end of the week."

The fact that the attackers used ransomware to infect the county's computers was disclosed today says ABC 7 News, with the county Sheriff stating that the Sheriff's Office managed to restore Internet access and the email system during the last week.

According to another local report from KAMR Local 4 News, Judge Tanner said that the County Clerk, District Clerk, and the Sheriff's Office were the ones hit the hardest by the ransomware, and that the county is continuing to collaborate with government agencies to investigate the security incident and to restore the lost data.

Additionally, as Judge Tanner told KAMR Local 4 News, it was discovered during the ongoing investigation that the ransomware "has likely been in the county's computer system since January."

Just like in the case of Baltimore, Potter County officials haven't disclosed any other details regarding the specific strain of ransomware used during the attack, the way it was used to infect the city's computing system, or the requested ransom amount.



Update May 08 11:25 EDT: According to a Baltimore Sun report from today, based on the ransom note found on the locked systems, Baltimore City's computers were encrypted by the RobbinHood ransomware.
