
	   


Microsoft is currently working on enhancing the way emails sent using the Office 365 Message Encryption (OME) service are seen by mail servers so that they are less likely to be marked as spam and sent to the Trash folder.

OME is built on Microsoft Azure Rights Management (Azure RMS), part of Azure Information Protection, and it allows Office 365 customers to send and receive encrypted email messages using Outlook.com, Yahoo!, Gmail, and several other email services using encryption, identity, and authorization policies.

Improved OME coming January 2020

"We are planning on rolling out a few updates to Office 365 Message Encryption that is aimed at enhancing the authenticity of Office 365 Message Encryption emails," Microsoft says.

Redmond will also add "an updated email layout to recipients who receive the branded experience," as well as the capability to capture non-delivery reports (NDR) from OME protected emails.

The OME online service updates the Microsoft Exchange team is planning to add come with a January 2020 launch date and will be generally available on a worldwide basis, for standard multi-tenant environments.



This month's OME enhancements

The company already launched several new capabilities to the Office 365 Message Encryption service during December, including support for PDF attachments and Shared Mailboxes, Mac pre-licensing.

With the addition of PDF attachment support to OME secured emails, Microsoft has expanded the supported list of document types that previously only included Office documents such as Word, PowerPoint, and Excel.

To enable this new feature, users can follow the detailed instructions provided on the Office 365 Message Encryption FAQ page.


Previewing an encrypted PDF attachment (Microsoft)

The newly added support for shared mailboxes now makes it possible for enterprise users to open and view protected content via cross-platform apps such as Outlook on the web, Outlook Desktop, Outlook for Mac, and Outlook for iOS and Android on Office in Windows and Mac, and Outlook on the web.

Outlook pre-licensing is also generally available now for Mac users, removing the need for clients to retrieve use licenses and enabling secured email content offline viewing.

More Office 365 security-focused changes

Redmond is also rolling out a new malware Zero-hour Auto Purge (ZAP) toggle to the Office 365 Security & Compliance Center.

This makes it easier to enable the feature that detects and removes phishing, spam, or malicious email messages that for some reason — usually happening when attackers enable their malicious payloads after delivery — already landed in an Office 365 user's inbox.

A new Office 365 feature dubbed 'Unverified Sender' is also in development and designed to help users identify potential spam or phishing emails that reach their Outlook client's inbox, after being rolled out into their environment.

Microsoft also enabled Authenticated Received Chain (ARC) for all for Office 365 hosted mailboxes in October, a new capability that improves anti-spoofing detection and checks authentication results.
