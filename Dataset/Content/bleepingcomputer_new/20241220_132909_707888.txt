
	   


In a short announcement today, Stack Overflow informs that it was the target of an attack that resulted in hackers accessing its production systems.

The website is currently online and the few public details offered in a brief message say that an investigation revealed that "some level of production access was gained on May 11."

User data not affected

It is unclear how the intruders were able to access Stack Overflow's internal network but the measures taken following the discovery of the breach include patching all known vulnerabilities.

"Our customers’ and users’ security is of the utmost importance to us. After we conclude our investigation cycle, we will provide more information," says Mary Ferguson, VP of Engineering at Stack Overflow.

Stack Overflow emerged in 2008 as a question and answer website for programming topics. Part of the Stack Exchange Network, it grew into a community of more than 10 million users, as of January 2019.

The knowledge platform is available in several languages (English, Spanish, Russian, Portuguese and Japanese). According to the website, more than 50 million visitors access it every month looking to solve coding problems, develop their skills, or to find job opportunities.

Stack Overflow is considered a trusted source for getting an accurate overview of the trends in the developer community as well as for salary information determined from the level of experience, location, education, and technologies.

Update 5/17/19 1:36 PM EST: Stack Overflow's Mary Ferguson posted an update on the breach saying that the intrusion occurred on May 5 and was possible due to a bug in a build deployed to the development tier of the website. This allowed the attacker to log into the development tier and escalate access to the production version of stackoverflow.com.

The attacker contained their activity to exploration until May 11, when they made a change to the system that allowed privileged access on production. This modification triggered an alert and their network-wide access was terminated, and an investigation started.

Ferguson said initially that no evidence was found to indicate that customer or user data was affected. In today's update, the VP of engineering informed that most of the website's user database remained intact; however, the investigation identified a number of privileged requests from the attacker "that could have returned IP address, names, or emails for a very small number of Stack Exchange users."

A later edit confirmed this suspicion, announcing that the requests impacted about 250 public network users, which will receive notifications of the incident.

Further measures related to this event include:


Terminating the unauthorized access to the system
	Conducting an extensive and detailed audit of all logs and databases that we maintain, allowing us to trace the steps and actions that were taken
	Remediating the original issues that allowed the unauthorized access and escalation, as well as any other potential vectors that we have found during the investigation
	Issuing a public statement proactively
	Engaging a third party forensics and incident response firm to assist us with both remediation and learnings
	Taking precautionary measures such as cycling secrets, resetting company passwords, and evaluating systems and security levels
	We will provide more public information after our investigation cycle concludes.
