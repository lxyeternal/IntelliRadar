
	   


Tech support scammers are targeting users through search ads for recipes on ISP start pages and custom search result pages that redirect users to browser lockers stating that their computers are infected.

Last month we reported how scammers had started to move to Azure cloud services to host their tech support scams. Doing so had to the benefit of the Microsoft support scams residing on a legitimate Microsoft domain and them being secured by a TLS certificate provided by Microsoft.

Since then, researchers at Malwarebytes have discovered that these tech support scams are being promoted through ad campaigns for recipe search on ISP start pages.

According to Malwarebytes, tech support scammers typically rely on malvertising in low quality ad networks where they can purchase cheap ads. They also tend to impersonate computer or software companies in order to trick users into clicking on the links.

In this particular campaign, the scammers are purchasing ads on the Google and Bing ad networks that target search keywords related to recipes.

"We see scammers actively targeting brands by trying to impersonate them," states Malwarebytes blog post. "The idea is to reel in victims looking for support with a particular product or service. However, in this particular campaign, the crooks are targeting folks looking up food recipes."

When Google or Bing displays these paid ads, they can be shown on normal search engines, but they tend to show more on ISP start pages or portals that display a greater amount of ads.


ISP Start Pages and Yahoo Search with Scam Ads

When the scammers create the ads, the ad links appear to be legitimate sites devoted to recipes, lifestyle, fitness, and general interests, which allows them to get past the ad quality reviews.

In reality, though, these sites are decoys being used purely to trick the ad networks as when you go to them directly, they show a site like the one below, but if referred by a search engine they will redirect you to a tech support scam.


Example Decoy Site

In an flow chart created by Malwarebytes, we can see how how this scam is conducted.  A user searches for a recipe and clicks on an ad, the user will be brought to the decoy site, which then redirects them to an Azure site claiming to be Microsoft support blocking your computer due to a security risk.


Scam Flow Chart

When the tech support scams are displayed, they will be shown with JavaScript alerts that make it difficult to close the page, speak to you, or make annoying sounds.


Tech Support Scam on Azure

For Firefox users, these pages are particular troublesome as they are will lock up the operating system by using 100% of the available CPU. When this happens, the only way to close the browser is to end its process in Task Manager.


100% CPU Utilization

Like other tech support scams, when Malwarebytes called them they found that the scammers tried to sell unnecessary services and software.

"To continue on with deception, the rogue technicians lied to us about the state of our computer and made up imaginary threats. The goal was to sell expensive support packages that actually add little value."

Older users a possible target

The Malwarebytes' Threat Intelligence Team feels that this campaign is targeted at an older demographic due to the ads showing in ISP start pages.

"This tech support scam campaign cleverly targeted an older segment of the population by using paid search results for food recipes via online portals used by many Internet Service Providers."

While I am sure there many young people who use ISP start pages, I have to agree that when I help a grandparent or older family member, their start pages are typically what is assigned by the ISP, whether that be AOL, ATT, COX, or Xfinity.
