
	   


Hackers are taking over high profile verified Twitter accounts to promote a cryptocurrency scam promising to give away up to 5,000 bitcoins to those sending between 0.1 BTC to 20 BTC to a "contribution" address.

"We're looking into this and will share a statement ASAP," a Twitter spokesperson told BleepingComputer.

The scammers are using the hijacked accounts to send the messages through Twitter's web app and, so far, they have managed to collect 11.39174745 BTC (which amounts to roughly $105,000) on just one of the Bitcoin addresses used in the scam.

One of the scam messages is designed to bait potential victims with the bitcoin giveaway:

We have partnered with CryptoForHealth and are giving back 5000 BTC to the community. See more here : http://cryptoforhealth[.]com

Another version, pushed through the accounts of Bill Gates and Elon Musk, promises to double all contributions within 30 minutes:

Everyone is asking me to give back, and now is the time.

I am doubling all payments sent to my BTC address for the next 30 minutes. You send $1,000, I send you back $2,000.

BTC Address - bc1qxy2kgdygjrsqtzq2n0yrf2493p83kkfjhx0wlh

Only going on for 30 minutes! Enjoy!

Tech companies, cryptocurrency exchanges, celebrities, and tech executives are among the high profile accounts the hackers have already managed to take over in rapid succession to promote their scam.

The Twitter accounts of @Uber, @Apple, @Bitcoin, @BarackObama, @JeffBezos, @JoeBiden, @elon_musk, @BillGates, @WarrenBuffett, @kanyewest, @wizkhalifa, @coinbase, @Ripple, @Gemini, @binance, @justinsuntron, @Tronfoundation, and @SatoshiLite have all been hijacked.



There is no information yet on how these Twitter accounts were hijacked but theories range between the compromise of a third-party application to the use of Twitter's password-reset form to gain access.

There are also reports that the attackers changed the email address associated with the hijacked Twitter accounts to block the rightful owners from taking back control of their profiles.

However, the most probable cause behind the attacks could be a hacked Twitter back end admin panel since Twitter subsequently removed tweets containing a screenshot of it, leaving behind a "This Tweet violated the Twitter Rules" message.

What is sure is that many of these hacked accounts have two-factor authentication enabled which means that the hijackers were also able to bypass this extra layer of security.
