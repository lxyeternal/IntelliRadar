Galaxy S10 phone models rely on ultrasounds to read the owner's fingerprint that unlocks the phone. The technology was described as "revolutionary" and was supposed to provide top performance.

Fix for silicone case is on the way

After fitting a new Samsung Galaxy S10 with a silicone case and registering one of her fingerprints, Lisa Neilson found that her husband could also unlock the phone.

The case is nothing special. It had been purchased from eBay for around $3.50.

The couple reported the problem to Samsung and a customer support representative told them it was indeed a security issue.

After an internal investigation, it seems that Samsung found the reason for this behavior and announced that a software patch will become available soon.

According to the BBC, the company said that it was "aware of the case of S10's malfunctioning fingerprint recognition and will soon issue a software patch."

Testing the limits

A silicone case may not be the only thing that unlocks a Samsung, though. A BleepingComputer reader owning a Galaxy Note10+, told us that the same thing can be achieved with latex gloves.

With the gloves on, they told us, the phone could be unlocked using either thumbs, although only one was registered in the phone.

Testing for BleepingComputer, they wanted to see if someone else could access the device with latex gloves on. The test failed; his friend having "significantly smaller hands" may be the cause.

Unconventional methods to unlock a Galaxy S10 emerged soon after the phone launched in March. User 'darkshark' probed the ultrasonic reader with a 3D representation of his unlock fingerprint and was able to access the device.


"It took me 3 reprints trying to get the right ridge height (and I forgot to mirror the fingerprint on the first one) but yeah, 3rd time was the charm. The 3D print will unlock my phone...in some cases just as well as my actual finger does."


darkshark used a high-resolution photo of his fingerprint, edited it for clarity in Photoshop, exported to 3dsMax to create a model with the fingerprint details. Then they used an AnyCubic Photon LCD resin printer to create the physical print.

Update [10/18/19]: Article updated with information from a BleepingComputer reader that tested the fingerprint scanner in their Galaxy Note 10+ using latex gloves.

Samsung released a statement today informing users that an update for the fingerprint scanner is planned for next week. Until the patch is applied, the company advises Galaxy Note10/10+ and S10/S10+/S10 5G users to stop using their front screen protective covers.

The problem stems from the scanner erroneously unlocking the devices after recognizing 3D patterns in certain silicone cases covering the screen.
