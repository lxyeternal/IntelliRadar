
	   


In Firefox 65, Mozilla is overhauling how users can configure the Content Blocking settings. With this version, the previously confusing configuration is replaced by three different modes that a user can select that offer varying degrees of blocking and customization.

The biggest change is that the Content Blocking settings under Privacy & Security. Firefox 65 will now offer three different modes that users can select; a standard, strict, and custom mode.

The Standard mode is "Balanced for protection and performance. Allows some trackers so websites function properly." In this mode, third-party tracking cookies are blocked and known trackers are blocked in Private browsing sessions.

Standard Content Blocking Mode

The Strict mode "Blocks all trackers Firefox detects. May cause some sites to break." When configured, the Strict mode blocks all known trackers in all Windows, including a regular browser session, and also blocks all third-party cookies.

This mode will cause sites or some of their functionality to not work properly if they rely on 3rd party cookies. For those sites, you can always allow them on an individual basis.


Strict Content Blocking Mode

Finally, the Custom mode lets you "Choose what to block." The Custom mode allows you to select how you want Trackers blocked, which block list you wish to use, and what cookies you wish to block. This option offers the greatest level of customization so that you can fine-tune the entire content blocking experience to your needs.

The default settings for the Custom mode will also display a warning that the configuration could cause some sites to not work properly. As always, if this mode causes problems with a site, you can disable blocking an individual site basis.


Custom Content Blocking Mode

Modifying settings for individual sites

While the Content Blocking settings allow you to configure the default settings that will be used browser wide, if you are experiencing any issues with a site, you can also configure these settings on an individual basis by using the Control Center.

To open a site's Control Center, click on the little i to the left of the address bar as shown below.


Open Control Center

When the Control Center opens it will display the current status of what is being blocked at the site. You can then override this by clicking on Trackers or disabling Blocking by clicking on the Turn off blocking for this site.



The granularity that Firefox is going to offer its users is very welcome. Most users, though, will just stick with the Standard setting that provides much less protection than the other modes.

For power users, though, the ability to customize how you would like to block cookies is going to be very welcome.
