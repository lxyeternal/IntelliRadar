
	   


Twitter on Wednesday announced that it would turn off its Tweet via SMS feature for an unspecified period following abuses that led to hackers posting from at least two high-profile accounts.

One of the victims was Twitter co-founder and CEO Jack Dorsey, whose feed got hijacked on Friday and posted racial slurs and even a fake bomb threat at the Twitter headquarters.

The most recent successful attack was on actress Chloë Grace Moretz' account, which sent out tweets suggesting that the same hackers were behind the deed, a group calling themselves Chuckling Squad.

Both hacks were possible due to vulnerabilities affecting the ability to tweet via SMS. The company reacted after the compromise of Dorsey's account by saying that it was investigating the incident.


We're aware that @jack was compromised and investigating what happened.
— Twitter Comms (@TwitterComms) August 30, 2019

A day later, Twitter concluded that the hack occurred because of a security oversight from the mobile carrier, which allowed an unauthorized party to tweet using the text messaging service. Simplified, the attackers spoofed the phone number of the Twitter user.

Service temporarily suspended

Twitter took note of the attacks and having pinned down the cause of the problem decided to suspend its SMS-based tweeting service until the issues are solved.

There is no estimation on when the ability to tweet via text message will be turned on again. The only timeframe offered was a vague "soon" but it appears that the measure was necessary to protect user accounts.

"We’re taking this step because of vulnerabilities that need to be addressed by mobile carriers and our reliance on having a linked phone number for two-factor authentication (we’re working on improving this)," reads a message from the company.

One thing is sure, though, Twitter plans to improve the feature and reactivate it "in markets that depend on SMS for reliable communication."


We’re temporarily turning off the ability to Tweet via SMS, or text message, to protect people’s accounts.
— Twitter Support (@TwitterSupport) September 4, 2019

Hackers having fun

The messages published by the hackers seem to have no particular purpose. They tweeted all sort of unrelated messages, from shoutouts to their friends and racial slights to hashtagging the name of the group.

From Chloë Moretz's account, they posted Jack Dorsey's alleged social security number as well as addresses, phone numbers and a call for providing nude pics.


credit: Feeling: Full, Chandu, The Daily Fold, Sam Lisker

It is important to note that these hacks were possible because of the phone numbers being available in the compromised accounts (for two-factor authentication) and of the active SMS-based tweeting function.

Users that don't need to push tweets via text messages can remove their phone number from their account and still benefit from 2FA protection by enabling this security measure through an authenticator app.
