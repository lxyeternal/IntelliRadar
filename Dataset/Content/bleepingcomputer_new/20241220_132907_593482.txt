
	   


Skylight Cyber released today an almost full list of MAC addresses used by the hacking group behind the Operation ShadowHammer to target ASUS customers with a backdoored version of the ASUS Live Update Utility.

The Skylight Cyber team was able to extract the MAC addresses targeted in the ASUS hack by reverse engineering the offline tool released by Kaspersky to allow ASUS users to check if their computer was one of the targets in the attack.

While initially, the Skylight Cyber security experts reached the conclusion that using their computer would take them 162.5 days to extract the MAC addresses from Kaspersky's offline checker, they managed to do it in less than an hour after renting an Amazon AWS p3.16xlarge instance that comes with eight NVIDIA V100 Tesla 16GB GPUs.

Qihoo 360 also took a look at the list of MAC addresses used in the ASUS supply chain attack and published a chart detailing the network interface controller (NIC) vendors behind the MACs targeted in the attack.

While the ones branded by ASUS, Intel, AzureWave, and LiteOn lead the rankings with their NICs making an appearance on 268, 157, 101, and 31 of the netbooks which were targeted by ShadowHammer, there are at least 10 other MACs from other manufacturers.

As stated by Skylight Cyber, "The attackers had prior knowledge either through ASUS own systems (uploaded telemetry) or they wanted to re-establish foothold on machines they have already visited in the past."



The ASUS hacking campaign was detected by Kaspersky during January 2019 and it ran between June and November 2018, allowing the attackers to add a backdoor within the ASUS Live Update Utility which was later distributed to ASUS users using the company's official update servers.

Kaspersky found evidence connecting the methods used during Operation ShadowHammer with the ones utilized in the supply chain attacks against CCleaner and NetSarang from 2017, with the threat actor behind the latter already identified as BARIUM — known users of the Winnti backdoor — by both Microsoft, ESET, and other security researchers.

After the ShadowHammer story broke, ASUS confirmed the hacking campaign and also said that "only the version of Live Update used for notebooks has been affected," with all other devices not being affected by the supply chain attack.



ASUS customers who want to update the ASUS Live Update Utility to the clean 3.6.8 version can do it by following the step by step procedure available HERE.

Users can also check if their ASUS computers have been targeted by the attack with the help of the offline checkers provided by ASUS and Kaspersky, or online using the web checker available on Kaspersky's website.
