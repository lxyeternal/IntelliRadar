
	   


The FIN7 hacking group has added new tools to its malicious toolkit, a malware loader that will deliver payloads straight into memory and a module that hooks into the legitimate remote administration software of ATM maker NCR Corporation.

Researchers with FireEye’s Mandiant group found that some samples of the new in-memory malware dropper they dubbed BOOSTWRITE can load more than one payloads, including the Carbanak backdoor associated with the FIN7 hackers.

The newly identified RAT called RDFSNIFFER arrives on the compromised machines after the BOOSTWRITE loader decrypts the embedded payload using encryption keys received from its operators on launch.

BOOSTWRITE uses a DLL search order hijacking technique to loads its own malicious DLLs into the infected system's memory which enables it to download the initialization vector (IV) and the decryption key needed to decrypt the embedded payloads.

"Once the key and the IV are downloaded the malware decrypts the embedded payloads and performs sanity checks on the results," states the report. "The payloads are expected to be PE32.DLLs which, if the tests pass, are loaded into memory without touching the filesystem."

While analyzing one of the spotted BOOSTWRITE samples, the researchers discovered that the loader was used to drop two payloads: the Carbanak backdoor and the newly found RDFSNIFFER module.



Even though most of the samples Mandiant saw were unsigned, one of BOOSTWRITE samples recovered more recently was signed with a code signing certificate issued by MANGO ENTERPRISE LIMITED. This sample flew under the radar of all VirusTotal anti-malware engines as it wasn't by any of them.

"By exploiting the trust inherently provided by code certificates, FIN7 increases their chances of bypassing various security controls and successfully compromising victims," states Mandiant.

In-memory loaded RATs

The RDFSNIFFER module dropped as a payload onto the computers of infected targets has remote access Trojan (RAT) features and it enables the FIN7 hackers "to hijack instances of the NCR Aloha Command Center Client application and interact with victim systems via existing legitimate 2FA sessions."

RDFSNIFFER loads itself into the process of NCR Corporation’s RDFClient every time the legitimate software is executed on the compromised machines.

This allows the malware to monitor or alter connections made using RDFClient, thus providing its operators with a highly efficient man-in-the-middle attack tool.

"This module also contains a backdoor component that enables it to inject commands into an active RDFClient session," says Mandiant. "This backdoor allows an attacker to upload, download, execute and/or delete arbitrary files."

		FIN7 has not stopped evolving

Since it was spotted during mid-2015, the FIN7 financially-motivated threat group (also tracked by researchers as Carbanak or Cobalt) has mainly targeted banks and the point-of-sale (PoS) terminals European and U.S. companies from various industry sectors using the multi-functional Carbanak backdoor.

Although some FIN7 members were arrested last year, the hacking group is still active and has since moved to use other malware strains including the ones just discovered by Mandiant.

For instance, after its members' arrest, Arbor Networks detected a phishing campaign that targeted bank employees from Russia and Romania, with the evidence pointing to a FIN7 operation.

In May, Kaspersky Lab’s Global Research and Analysis Team (GReAT) said that they also found evidence that the FIN7 group is still operational after analyzing "various campaigns that used the same Tactics Tools and Procedures (TTPs) as the historic FIN7."

"While these incidents have also included FIN7’s typical and long-used toolsets, such as CARBANAK and BABYMETAL, the introduction of new tools and techniques provides further evidence FIN7 is continuing to evolve in response to security enhancements," says Mandiant.

"Barring any further law enforcement actions, we expect at least a portion of the actors who comprise the FIN7 criminal organization to continue conducting campaigns."
