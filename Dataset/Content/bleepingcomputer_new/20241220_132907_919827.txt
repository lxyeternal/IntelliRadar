If a shopper is from a different country or uses Linux operating system, the script remains inactive, most likely as a defense against security researchers.

Although First Aid Beauty does not bear the Procter & Gamble marks, the company acquired the beauty brand this year for a reported $250 million.

Willem de Groot, a researcher at Sanguine Security that tracks online payment skimming and fraud, found that the malicious script has been hiding on the First Aid Beauty website since May 5.

He contacted company representatives and the support team about a week ago and received no response the script was still present at the time of writing.

The researcher told BleepingComputer that he contacted First Aid Beauty on Sunday and several times this week but received no reply.



We checked the website again at publishing time and it was no longer accessible, showing a 503 Service Unavailable error.

It is unclear how many visitors shop from First Aid Beauty but the site has had around 100,000 monthly visitors in the past six months, around %80 of them being from the U.S.

Being selective in picking its victims is what makes this MageCart scrip "fairly advanced." However, it is also heavily obfuscated and uses encryption, which suggests that the individuals behind it are familiar with this type of business.

de Groot told us that website owners typically discover these skimmers withing a couple of weeks but this one resisted for more than five months, "so the level of stealth for this actor is outstanding."



de Groot provides a deobfuscated version of the script that shows its functions clearly. The data it looks for includes the card number, expiration date, the name of the owner, and the CVV code used for card not present transactions.

Keep e-commerce software updated

First Aid Beauty uses the Magento e-commerce platform that was updated earlier this month to patch 56 security issues. One of them has the severity score of 10, the highest, while 11 others received a severity rating of 9.1.

In a report earlier this month, cybersecurity company RiskIQ notes that the MageCart or e-skimming threat has affected hundreds of thousands of websites and turned into an ever present phenomenon.

The threat is so large that the FBI issued a couple of days ago a warning about it aimed at small and medium-sized businesses as well as government agencies that process online payments.

Apart from applying the latest patched to installed software and keeping the antivirus product updated, merchants can defend against payment card stealing scripts by using the Content Security Policy (CSP) tool to permit JavaScript to load on their website only from a trusted list of domains.

Using Subresource Integrity (SRI) is another mechanism. It can block loading modified JavaScript code by checking a cryptographic hash for the legitimate resource.

Update [10/25/2019]: A few minutes after publishing we checked the website again and it was back up without the malicious script. The fact that e-skimmer survived undetected for such a long time is a concern that the company should address.

Update [10/25/2019, 17:35 ET]: Procter & Gamble returned a request for comments from BleepingComputer saying that they're working to identify and notify potentially affected customers. Full statement available below:


"Consumer trust is fundamental to us, and we take data privacy very seriously. As soon as we learned about the compromise of the First Aid Beauty site, we moved quickly to take the site down and minimize the impact to our consumers. We are currently investigating the source of the malware and working to identify and notify those consumers who might have been impacted to ensure we provide them the necessary support."
