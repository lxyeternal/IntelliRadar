
	   


Fredrick Brown, a former U.S. Army civilian medical records administrator, admitted on Tuesday to taking part in a fraud scheme that victimized thousands and allowed the fraudsters to steal millions mostly from elderly and disabled veterans.

The 38-year old man from Las Vegas, Nevada, pleaded guilty to one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud and a separate count of conspiracy to launder monetary instruments. 

The defendant is in federal custody awaiting sentencing on February 6, 2020, and faces up to 20 years in federal prison for each of his conspiracy charges.

Brown revealed that between July 2014 and September 2015, he stole personal identifying information (PII) of thousands of military members while working at the 65th Medical Brigade, Yongsan Garrison, South Korea, including but not limited to "their names, social security numbers, DOD ID numbers, dates of birth, and contact information."

To do this, he admitted to harvesting the PII by taking photos of his computer's screen while being logged into his Armed Forces Health Longitudinal Technology Application account.

Brown was one of the five fraudsters that were charged with coordinating an identify-theft and fraud scheme targeting "service members (active duty, reserve component, and National Guard) and veterans, their dependents, and civilians employed by the Department of Defense" as revealed in a 14-count indictment [PDF] unsealed in August.

"Brown further admitted that he subsequently provided that stolen data to co-defendant, Robert Wayne Boling Jr., so that Boling and others could exploit the information in various ways to access Department of Defense and Veterans Affairs benefits sites and steal millions of dollars," as the press release published today by the Department of Justice adds.

Per the August indictment, "over the course of the scheme, the Defendants stole and exploited the PII of thousands of military-affiliated individuals and caused millions of dollars of actual and attempted losses to military-affiliated individuals, the Veterans Administration, and banks and credit unions across the United States."

The others in the group used the identity information stolen by Brown to create and compromise military personnel Defense Self-Service Logon accounts.

This allowed them to access benefits information online via the Department of Defense portal, enabling them "to steal or attempt to steal millions of dollars from military members’ bank accounts" and "veterans’ benefits payments".

Codefendants in federal custody

"The Defendants used the stolen PII to target military-affiliated individuals in various ways, such as stealing from military-affiliated individuals' personal bank accounts and stealing pension and disability benefits paid to military-affiliated individuals by the Veterans Administration," says the indictment.

Additionally, the defendants used a various money remittances and bank accounts for financial transactions designed to conceal and dispose of the stolen funds.

Boling, Crawford, Kerr, and Seok, the other codefendants, are charged with multiple counts of conspiracy, wire fraud, and aggravated identity theft, and are either in federal custody or awaiting transfer to the Western District of Texas from the Philippines.

"The compromise of personally identifiable information can significantly harm our service members, veterans and their families and we will aggressively investigate such matters," said Principal Deputy Inspector General Glenn A. Fine.

"This indictment and the coordinated actions of our criminal investigative component, the Defense Criminal Investigative Service, demonstrate our commitment to swift action against those who attempt to enrich themselves through identify theft, money laundering, and conspiracy."
