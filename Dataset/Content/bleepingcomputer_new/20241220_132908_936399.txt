
	   


A 31-year old Ukrainian national named Oleksii Petrovich Ivanov who allegedly ran multiple malvertising campaigns between October 2013 through May 2018 was extradited to the U.S. from the Netherlands and is facing hacking charges.

Ivanov got arrested on October 19, 2018, by the Dutch authorities following an investigation conducted by the U.S. Secret Service Criminal Investigations in collaboration with the National High Tech Crime Unit of the Dutch National Police and UK's National Crime Agency.

The defendant was indicted on December 3, 2018, with "one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud, four counts of wire fraud, and one count of computer fraud," with the indictment—available HERE—being unsealed on May 2, 2019, when he entered the U.S. and got detained without bail.

"This defendant engaged in an extraordinary and far-reaching scheme to infect and hack computers throughout the United States and the world," stated U.S. Attorney Carpenito. "This ‘malvertising’ scheme is especially dangerous because it uses online ads to target millions of unsuspecting Internet users engaged in activities as routine as booking their next vacation."

According to the indictment, Ivanov and multiple co-conspirators supposedly ran malicious advertising campaigns with an international reach, employing stolen identities to create and use various fake companies to register domains that were later used to host and launch malvertising campaigns.

These campaigns were used to drop malware on the machines of users who got tricked into clicking the malicious ads. As detailed in the indictment, the group allegedly "caused unsuspecting Internet users to view or access their malicious advertisements on more than one hundred million occasions," in an attempt to cause millions of dollars of damage to their victims.

As exemplified in the Department of Justice press release: 


For instance, in June and July 2014, the defendant allegedly posed as “Dmitrij Zaleskis,” CEO of a fake United Kingdom company called “Veldex Limited” to submit a series of malicious advertisements to a U.S.-based internet advertising company for distribution, including two campaigns submitted on July 15, 2014 that were viewed or accessed approximately 17,328,129 times in a matter of days. 


After the ad campaign ran by Veldex Limited was repeatedly flagged and reported as spreading malware, the advertising company reached out to Ivanov but, instead of this leading to the campaign being terminated, the defendant managed to convince the firm to keep it running for months.

Additionally, once the ad campaigns peddling malware were in the end successfully flagged and removed, the group purportedly "switched to new online advertising companies and used new fake identities to buy more advertisements."

The investigators also discovered that Ivanov and co-conspirators apparently were behind a malicious campaign designed to infect over one hundred targets from the District of New Jersey with botnet malware, subsequently selling access to the created botnet.

"Cyber criminals who harm victims in the United States and around the world cannot rely on fake identities and international borders to evade justice," said Assistant Attorney General Benczkowski. "This case and today’s extradition demonstrate that the United States and its international partners will find cyber fugitives and bring them to face justice in the United States, no matter where they commit their crimes."
