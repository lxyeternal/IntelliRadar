
	   


Microsoft has responded to a complaint filed by Kaspersky Labs, which accused the OS maker of using its dominant market position to push its antivirus — Windows Defender — on Windows 10 users, on the detriment of third-party AVs.

Kaspersky Labs, through the voice of its founder, Eugene Kaspersky, accused Microsoft in the past, of:

⇾ Burying license renewal notifications for third-party AVs
⇾ Quietly activating Windows Defender when the third-party AV license expires, with no visible user warning
⇾ Quietly killing third-party AVs when users activate Windows Defender
⇾ Not permitting users to run more than one third-party AV at a time
⇾ Not providing sufficient documentation for AV vendors to create Windows 10 compatible AVs in due time
⇾ Deactivating third-party AVs not compatible with Windows 10 and quietly enabling Windows Defender

Kaspersky Labs filed a grievance with Russian authorities in November last year, and with the European Commission and German Federal Cartel Office, earlier this month.

In a blog post published yesterday, Microsoft said that most of these issues have been resolved with Windows 10 Creators Update, released earlier in April.

According to Rob Lefferts, Microsoft's Partner Director of the Windows & Devices Group, Security & Enterprise, Windows 10 will temporarily disable third-party antivirus programs until they become compatible with the version of Windows 10 the user is running.

When the incompatible third-party AV is updated, Microsoft says it re-enables it, and the AV will continue to work.

At the moment, Microsoft hints that Kaspersky is making a big fuss about nothing, as 95% of Windows 10 PCs run a third-party antivirus application that is compatible with the Windows 10 Creators Update. Microsoft alludes that it is entitled to disable incompatible AVs and turn Windows Defender, as this protects users from both security threats and OS crashes.

Microsoft details "always on" policy for Windows Defender

The Microsoft blog post details a series of improvements that Microsoft made to Windows 10 after the OS' launch in 2015 and since Kaspersky's initial complaints last fall.

Back in May, even Eugene Kaspersky himself noted some of these positive improvements, but his company pushed through with the official EU complaint in June regardless.

What Microsoft tried to convey through its blog post was its dedication to an "always on" philosophy regarding antivirus protection.

Currently, Windows 10 features only two slots for antivirus software, of which one is dedicated solely to Windows Defender. Microsoft sees and uses Windows Defender as a backup AV in situations where users can't afford to pay for third-party AV software, the third-party AV license expires but the user doesn't renew it, or the third-party AVs encounter technical glitches following Windows updates.

Either way, Microsoft's blog post cleared the air regarding some of Kaspersky's initial accusations.
