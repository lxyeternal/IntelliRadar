
	   


Residents of two buildings in Lappeenranta, Finland spent a few days in the cold over the past few days as DDoS attacks aimed at a service provider indirectly affected the smart building heating system installed at their residential complex.

According to Finnish media, the attacks were short-lived but had the unfortunate result of jamming the smart home management system, which restarted repeatedly and eventually shut down.

Yle Uutiset and Tivi reported that the attacks were aimed at the company managing the building's heating system, a local business called Valtia.

Valtia CEO Simo Rounela told Metropolitan.fi that the DDoS attacks lasted from late October to November 3, last week. Rounela also told Vice that the attacks targeted DNS servers used by the company.

This resulted in smart home management systems being unable to connect to its servers, resulting in reboots, and an eventual shutdown.

Due to the mercurial nature of DDoS attacks and because its engineers normally looked for local software and hardware issues, it took a few days until the company realized what was happening.

Rounela said that after disconnecting the smart home systems from the Internet, they began to work normally.

Company fixed issue before it became an inconvenience for residents

At this time of year, temperatures in Lappeenranta are below 0 degrees Celsius, and in the 20-30 range on the Fahrenheit scale.

Rounela said the attacks didn't cause too much discomfort for the residents of two buildings, and there wasn't any need to relocate any tenants.

This incident is reminiscent of attacks on Dyn, a managed DNS service, which indirectly affected a large number of services, such as Twitter, Imgur, Reddit, GitHub, Etsy, Soundcloud, Spotify and others, who became unreachable due to Dyn being bombarded with junk traffic.
