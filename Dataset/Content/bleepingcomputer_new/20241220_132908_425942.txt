
	   


An app styling itself as a more feature-rich unofficial version of Telegram was installed over 100,000 from Google Play only to provide minimum messaging services and to promote malicious websites.

Named MobonoGram 2019, the app used code from the legitimate Telegram messenger and added a few scripts that ran in secret on the infected device to help with persistence and with loading URLs received from the command server.

By the time security researchers found the malicious app, its developer - RamKal Developers, had already pushed five updates to the official Android store.

It is unclear how long MobonoGram 2019 remained on Google Play, but pushing this high a number of installations was possible by redirecting users from third-party repositories to Google's official market for mobile.

Services run no matter what

To ensure its long-term presence on the Android system, the developer made sure that the malicious service would run in the foreground because there's a smaller chance for it to be killed by the system even when low on RAM.



The developer also prepared for the scenario where the service is terminated and added a timer counting two hours from the event and then respawn the killed service.

Once running, the malware contacts its commanding servers to receive URLs to access from the infected device, a browser user agent to mask the origin of the request and three JavaScript codes.

Sites served based on region

According to a report today from Symantec, all user agent information received from the same server is different. Furthermore, the URLs change based on the geographical location of the device gleaned from its IP address.

Tests showed that the server responded with different types of websites when the device had a different country IP. For a device in the U.S., the researchers received a scam website informing of a fake winning. A gadget in Singapore got a similar site and others hosting adult content and games.



Another observation from the researchers was an endless loop to the same website as it made requests to itself. This would not only accelerate battery drainage but may also lead to crashing the device.

As for the three JavaScript codes, Symantec analysts believe that the intention was to commit click fraud and increase ad revenue.


"However, the clicking events were not seen in action, even though all JavaScript codes were indeed loaded. Nonetheless, we cannot entirely dismiss the possibility of the malware being used for click fraud or some other malicious end." - Symantec


RamKal Developers is not responsible just for MobonoGram 2019. The same developer published on Google Play another app, called Whatsgram, that had the same behavior.

Telemetry data from Symantec between January and May shows on their radar 1,235 detections that are related to this malicious app, detected as Android.Fakeyouwon; most of them were recorded in the U.S., Iran, India, and the United Arab Emirates (UAE).

This particular version of the malware, though, was detected mostly in Iran, the U.S., UAE, and Germany.



The malicious app has been removed from Google Play but it remains available from third-party Android stores. Users are advised not to install apps from unofficial markets as they are typically crawling with ill-intended packages. Restricting software installations to trusted source only can spare you of a good deal of trouble.
