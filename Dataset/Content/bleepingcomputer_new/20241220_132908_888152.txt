
	   


Security researchers across the globe united in a project dedicated to sharing URLs used in malicious campaigns managed to take down close to 100,000 websites actively engaged in malware distribution.

Called URLhaus, the project was initiated by abuse.ch, a non-profit cybersecurity organization in Switzerland. It started at the end of March 2018 and recorded a daily average of 300 submissions from 265 security researchers.

Chinese hosting networks are slow to react

The takedown activity involved the cooperation of the companies hosting the offensive websites on their infrastructure, some of them not rushing to respond to abuse reports.

"The three top Chinese malware hosting networks have an average abuse desk reaction time of more than a month!" abuse.ch writes in a report.

ChinaNet, China Unicom, and Alibaba did not rush into taking the appropriate measures, leaving the compromised websites active for one month and ~10 days, one month and 23 days, and one month and 2 days, respectively. The total number of malware URLs reported to them was close to 500.

The quickest to respond to URLhaus reports was Critical Case in Italy. They took 22 hours to get down 151 malicious URLs. Next was Unified Layer in the U.S,. who took down 127 malicious URLs in two days and a half. The full list with the top 15 hosting providers and how much they took to react is available below:

The average count of active malware distribution sites URLhaus sees on a daily basis is between 4,000 and 5,000.

Compromised websites mainly push Emotet

Emotet is at the top of the list of malware distributed through the websites submitted to the URLhaus project. This malware family is typically spread through emails carrying a document with a malicious macro.

When the document is launched, the macro downloads Emotet (also known as Heodo) from a compromised website and executes it.

Emotet started as a banking trojan but it is under constant development, adding new functionality like delivering other malware pieces, or checking if the victim's IP is blacklisted or on a spam list.

URLhaus collected about 380,000 malware samples over the past ten months and almost 16,000 of them were Emotet payloads, putting it in the top spot.

Gozi banking trojan falls in the second place with almost 13,000 payloads, followed by GandCrab ransomware with a little over 6,000 samples.

Update: The article was updated to show that the quickest response time came from Critical Case in Italy, followed by Unified Layer in the U.S.
