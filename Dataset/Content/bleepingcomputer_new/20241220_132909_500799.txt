
	   


The plan to distrust TLS certificates issued by Symantec is on track as Mozilla released its first version of the browser that reacts with a security warning when users land on any page bearing the seal of trust from Symantec Certificate Authority.

The new policy has been recently introduced in Firefox Nightly 63 and will pass into the beta version in early September. According to the Firefox release calendar, the merge with the stable release will happen on October 23.

Google has made the same move with Chrome 70 Canary, the nightly build for developers, on July 20. The insecure connection warning for all certificates signed by Symantec will transition into the beta version on September 13 and should get into the stable release on October 16.

Big names still have to update certs

The browser makers decided to distrust all TLS certificates from Symantec following a joint investigation into the CA's issuance practices. This resolution affects all entities under the Symantec CA umbrella, including GeoTrust, Thawte, and RapidSSL, the CAs it purchased and added to its tree of trust.

Website administrators that rely on certificates from any of these providers should replace them. Users of Firefox Nightly have opened a bug report to announce websites still running with a certificate chaining up to Symantec CA.

Among the big names on the list that have not replaced their certificate at the moment of writing is Sony PlayStation Store, Navy Federal Credit Union's online banking page, First National Bank of Pennsylvania's online banking, Estonian LHV Bank, Canadian telecom Freedom, La Banque Postale, La Banque Populaire Val de France, First National Bank in South Africa and Intel's Japanese website.

Gradual loss of trust

Google and Mozilla engineers discovered that Symantec Certificate Authority did not always follow the provisions and regulations of the industry to issue TLS certificates. The total of nonconforming certs amounted to 30,000.

Distrusting all certificates issued in Symantec CA's name is the last step in a three-stage process that started with Symantec CA demoting itself from the position of Root Certificate Authority to that of a Subordinate Certificate Authority that abides by the rules of a different party.

The second step is underway and consists in stable releases of Firefox and Chrome showing an untrusted connection error for certs vouched for by Symantec CA and issued before June 1, 2016.

DigiCert makes everybody happy

Under rapid fire from browser makers, Symantec explored the possibility of selling the certificate business straight from the start. When the chance presented itself, the company grabbed it with both hands.

On October 31, DigiCert announced it had completed its acquisition of Symantec's Website Security and PKI solutions.

The deal appears to be a win for everyone:

Symantec gets rid of its problems with browser makers and ends up with $950 million in upfront cash and a 30 percent stake in DigiCert stock
	Customers with certs undesired by Firefox and Google get new ones at no cost (all they have to do is follow the certificate replacement instructions from DigiCert)
	Browser makers solve the problem and deliver a warning for other Root CA trying to skirt the rules of the industry
	DigiCert improves its position in the market by adding enough clients to propel it from number six to number three TLS certificate authority on the planet
