
	   


Prison Rehabilitative Industries and Diversified Enterprises Inc (PRIDE) was hit by a ransomware attack on Saturday, December 7. The nonprofit organization's website and affected systems are still down.

PRIDE is an NPO from Florida that's helping inmates to learn vocational skills and to get certifications for easier reintegration once they get released.

"We make communities safer and save taxpayers money by training eligible inmates in vocational skills and transitioning them into the job market upon completion of their sentences," the NPO states.

Weekend ransomware attack

The ransomware attack that hit PRIDE's computing systems was observed by employees on Saturday after multiple systems including the NPO's website, payroll records, email, customer and vendor lists, and several other backend operations were taken down or became inaccessible.

PRIDE Chief Administrative Officer Dee Kiminki told Tampa Bay Times who first reported the incident that "certain data files within the PRIDE network have been encrypted and rendered unusable."

She also stated that the nonprofit organization is currently working with the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) as part of an ongoing investigation.

Florida Department of Corrections spokesperson Michelle Glady also says that the department doesn't believe that any of the information they had on the PRIDE systems has been compromised.

I don’t believe any of our information has been compromised. - Michelle Glady

BleepingComputer also reached out to PRIDE asking for additional details on the attack but had not heard back at the time of this publication. 

Ex-offender reintegration

The organization is self-funding itself by selling services and products made by the inmates it gets from the Florida Department of Corrections that enroll in its training programs.

"For over 30 years PRIDE has helped keep Florida communities safe by significantly reducing recidivism rates of ex-offenders," PRIDE says.

"Inmates receive real-world skills through over 40 job training centers offering hundreds of certifications that prepare them for re-entry as productive citizens."

According to PRIDE, the ex-offenders that participated in its training programs are statistically three times less likely than non-participant inmates to commit another offense after being released.
