
	   


The Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC) announced that it started investigating if Google's processing of personal data collected by the company as part of Ad Exchange online advertising transactions is breaching GDPR regulations.

Brave Chief Policy Officer (CPO) Dr. Johnny Ryan is the one who triggered the probe into Google's online advertising data collection practices with a formal complaint which says that the company's DoubleClick/Authorized Buyers advertising system is leaking personal data of website visitors to thousands of companies.

According to Dr. Ryan, "Google’s DoubleClick/Authorized Buyers advertising system is active on 8.4 million websites" and it "is a driver of Google’s $19.9B revenue from ads served on publishers’ websites and relies on broadcasting users’ personal data, unbeknownst to them."

Arising from the Data Protection Commission’s ongoing examination of data protection compliance in the area of personalised online advertising and a number of submissions to the Data Protection Commission, including those made by Dr. Johnny Ryan of Brave, a statutory inquiry pursuant to section 110 of the Data Protection Act 2018 has been commenced in respect of Google Ireland Limited’s processing of personal data in the context of its online Ad Exchange. 


Dr. Ryan filed complaints with the UK Information Commissioner and the Irish Data Protection Commissioner against Google and a number of other ad tech firms on 12 September 2018, together with Executive Director of the Open Rights Group Jim Killock and Michael Veale of University College London.

The complaints alerted the European data protection authorities "of a massive and ongoing data breach that affects virtually every user on the web" — a report on behavioral advertising and personal data is also available here.

“Surveillance capitalism is about to become obsolete,” said Brave's CPO. “The Irish Data Protection Commission’s action signals that now – nearly one year after the GDPR was introduced – a change is coming that goes beyond just Google. We need to reform online advertising to protect privacy, and to protect advertisers and publishers from legal risk under the GDPR”.

The Irish data privacy watchdog also stated that it will also look into Google's data retention practices:


The purpose of the inquiry is to establish whether processing of personal data carried out at each stage of an advertising transaction is in compliance with the relevant provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The GDPR principles of transparency and data minimisation, as well as Google’s retention practices, will also be examined.




GDPR is a user and data privacy regulation which was enacted in the European Union on May 25, 2018, and was very quickly put to use by NOYB (a European privacy enforcement non-profit organization) to file four complaints against Google, Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp on the same day for their use of "forced consent."

During November 2018, Google was targeted with GDPR complaints from multiple consumer groups according to The European Consumer Organisation for its deceptive practices on tracking user location.

In January 2019, Google was hit with a €50 ($56,8) million financial penalty in accordance with the GDPR's regulations by the Commission Nationale de l’informatique et des Libertés (CNIL) for violating GDPR's transparency and information obligations and for failing to obtain user consent for processing data for ads personalization purposes.
