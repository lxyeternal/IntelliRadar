
	   


The Checkers and Rally’s chain of double drive-thru restaurants disclosed a security breach which allowed attackers to steal payment card data from customers after infecting the point-of-sale (POS) systems in 103 locations from 20 states with malware.

Checkers "has worked closely with the third-party security experts to contain and remove the malware" and "engaged leading data security experts to conduct an extensive investigation and coordinated with affected restaurants and federal law enforcement authorities to address the matter" following the POS malware's discovery,

The malware was designed to collect information stored on the magnetic stripe of payment cards, including cardholder name, payment card number, card verification code and expiration date. Based on the investigation, Checkers has no evidence that other cardholder personal information was affected by this issue.
	Not all Checkers and Rally’s restaurants and not all guests who visited the impacted restaurants during the relevant time periods were affected by this issue.


According to the security notice, roughly 15% of the nearly 900 restaurants across the country were impacted by the security breach, with 102 being listed together with the estimated windows of exposure in the breach notice, while "One additional affected Checkers and Rally’s location will be issuing a separate notification."

While each of the restaurants mentioned in the data breach notification had their POS system infected with payment card data-stealing malware, not all the customers who visited those locations "during the relevant time periods are affected by this issue."

Despite this, Checkers advises all potentially impacted customers who visited the affected locations during the restaurants' estimated windows of exposure to contact their card issuers immediately if unauthorized charge are detected.


While we do not know if your specific payment card is affected by this issue, we encourage potentially impacted guests to refer to their payment card statements to identify the payment card they may have used at one of the affected Checkers and Rally’s locations. If you believe there is an unauthorized charge on your card, please contact your financial institution or card issuer immediately.


Checkers also says that it took measures to enhance the chain's security after the breach was contained and that it's "working with federal law enforcement authorities and coordinating with the payment card companies in their efforts to protect cardholders."

Customers who have additional questions regarding the security incident can call "1-844-386-9554, Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. CST and Saturday and Sunday from 10:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. CST" says Checkers Chief Administrative Officer & Executive Vice President Adam Noyes.
