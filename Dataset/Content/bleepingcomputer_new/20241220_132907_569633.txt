The issues were found on LeapPad Ultimate tablets, specifically created for children with precautions that offer an experience shielded from the dangers of the internet.

With minimum help from the parents, kids between 3 and 6 years old can explore various apps (videos, books, games) specific for their age that do not require online access.

Security researchers at Checkmarx discovered that the Pet Chat app on LeapPad Ultimate can expose the location of the table, and thus the kid's, under certain conditions.

The app is developed with children safety in mind and it enables kids to talk to one another in a chat room using preset phrases and emoticons.

However, the app also creates an ad-hoc WiFi network that broadcasts to wireless devices in its proximity using the SSID 'Pet Chat.' This can be used to identify the geographical position through the WiGLE wireless network mapping service.

Anyone scanning the area for WiFi signals and uploading them to WiGLE can collect details about the hotspot, including the MAC address, and the time of the scan.



"Attackers could check for isolated homes where children are using Pet Chat and try to launch more attacks," Checkmarx says in a report shared with BleepingComputer.

The way Pet Chat works could also be abused by an attacker within its wireless signal to send messages to the device it is installed on. This is possible because the communication protocol it uses does not require any sort of authentication.

This means that an attacker in range could communicate with a child using Pet Chat.



Lack of HTTPS puts sensitive info at risk

The researchers found that the LeapPad Ultimate tablet itself did not use HTTPS communication, meaning that any the data it transmitted was not encrypted.

By setting up a rogue access point, an attacker could intercept any information traveling from the tablet. This makes it susceptible to man-in-the-middle (MitM) attacks that could steal a parent's credit card info, emails, names, addresses, as well as details about the child using the tablet: name gender, date of birth.

The MitM attack would also work to spoof information displayed in LeapPad Ultimate's LeapSearch - a "child-safe web browser that provides access to safe web content."

"By injecting pieces of real data retrieved in the previous step, we created a “phishing version” of the LeapSearch portal which appears to be legit."

The researcher were able to modify the portal to the extent that it asked for sensitive information that is normally not required for online transactions.

An attacker could use this to learn the full number of the credit card or the security code for authorizing purchases online.



Checkmarx says that LeapFrog was quick to solve the issues and removed Pet Chat from its stores. However, the app may still be present on devices that are older than three years. In this case, parents should manually uninstall it.
