
	   


Starting September 1, 2020, new .gov sites will only be accessible via HTTPS as they will automatically be preloaded according to an announcement made by the U.S. Government's DotGov Program today.

The U.S. govt's DotGov Program is overseen by the U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) and it operates the .GOV top-level domain (TLD) and providing .gov domains to US-based government orgs, from federal agencies to local municipalities.

"Today, the DotGov Program announces our intent to preload the .gov TLD in the future," the DotGov Registrar said.

Entire .gov TLD to be preloaded in the future

After moving to the HTTPS protocol from HTTP, US government sites will secure their visitors' connections using Transport Layer Security protocol (TLS) by encrypting the exchanged data, alternating them if the exchanged data was altered, and protecting them from man-in-the-middle attacks.

DotGov says that it intends to preload the .gov top-level domain (TLD) to meet the public's expectation having their connection automatically secure when browsing a .gov website.

While this would be the easiest way to secure connections to .gov assets going forward, the TLD will not be submitted to the HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) preload list until a later date, yet to be announced, since all govt sites not currently offering HTTPS support would become immediately inaccessible.

HSTS is a web server directive that tells web browsers to only connect via secure HTTPS connections causing all insecure requests to the site to be automatically redirected to secure requests.

For this to happen, browsers bundle an HSTS preload list of all sites known to support secure connections so that the browser will never connect to them using an insecure protocol.

"Actually preloading is a simple step, but getting there will require concerted effort among the federal, state, local and tribal government organizations that use a common resource, but don’t often work together in this area," DotGov explained.

DotGov, CISA working to ensure future preloading compliance

By automatically preloading only the new .gov domains starting with September 1, 2020, the DotGov Program ensures that this move is "focused on transitioning historical domains, not new ones, and defaults to strong security going forward."

DotGov is currently working with the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) to make sure that .gov domains will be ready for preloading.

"We are teaming up with government-affiliated civic organizations to hold presentations and listening sessions in the coming months to get the word out about preloading .gov," DotGov added.

A DotGov HTTPS listserv is also available for government agencies to send feedback on this planned move, with current .gov account holders being able to join on request by emailing dotgovhttps@listserv.gsa.gov using their .gov email.

The U.S. government has also started requiring notarized signatures as part of the registration process for .gov domains starting March 10, 2020, as part of a move designed to block unauthorized orgs and individuals from registering such domains and using them as part of mail and wire fraud schemes.
