
	   


Chinese hackers deployed a new cyber-espionage tool on Linux servers belonging to a telecommunications network provider to steal SMS message content for specific recipients.

The threat actor's activity on the compromised machines extended to stealing call records from individuals of interest to intelligence services in China.

The campaign is attributed to APT41, a state-sponsored group of advanced hackers running espionage activity on behave of the Chinese government. The activity of this group traces back to 2012.

Discovered in August 2019 by FireEye during an incident response investigation, MESSAGETAP is the latest tool attributed to APT41. It was installed on Short Message Service Center (SMSC) servers - Linux machines that push SMS content to their destination or store the messages until the recipient becomes available.

After installation, the malware searches for two text files named 'keyword_parm' and 'parm' that contain lists of identifiers for messages of interest. Both are loaded in the memory of the compromised machine and then are deleted.

The two files include International Mobile Subscriber Identity (IMSI) number (imsiMap), phone numbers (phoneMap), or keywords present in SMS messages (keywordVec) that could interest the attackers.

FireEye highlights that the keywords are "terms of geopolitical interest to Chinese intelligence collection."

MESSAGETAP starts by monitoring all incoming and outgoing connections, parses the traffic (SCTP, SCCP, and TCAP layers), extracts the SMS messages and looks for those with the defined keywords.

The IMSI numbers of selected messages are compared with the data in the imsiMap, and the phone numbers with the data in the phoneMap list.



Any text that includes a keyword is encrypted using the XOR cipher and stored in CSV format. Messages parsed incorrectly are dumped to a different file in the same location under /etc directory.


"The inclusion of both phone and IMSI numbers show the highly targeted nature of this cyber intrusion. If an SMS message contained either a phone number or an IMSI number that matched the predefined list, it was saved to a CSV file for later theft by the threat actor."


Call records also targeted

SMS texts are not the only data cache APT41 was after. During the same intrusion, they also accessed databases containing call records and were able to query, save, and steal records.

According to FireEye, the details were associated with high-ranking individuals of interest to Chinese intelligence agencies.

With this level of access, APT41 hackers could see the phone numbers of the parties involved in the phone conversation, the time of the call, and duration.

This is not the only operation APT41 conducted this year against the telecommunications sector. FireEye observed a total of four organizations in the same activity sector targeted by the same group.

The cyber-espionage ventures from APT41 this year extend to entities in other fields of activity, like healthcare providers, travel services. The purpose appears to be getting sensitive records belonging to individuals of interest.

FireEye says that Chinese hackers have evolved their operations to target upstream data organizations with a critical role in routing end-user data. This is a trend dating from 2017.
