
	   
At least three banks operating in the UK have decided to temporarily suspend their mobile banking services for Samsung Galaxy S10 users.

The decision follows reports of a security issue impacting the fingerprint scanner in the devices used for unlocking the phone and authenticating into banking apps.

Banks disable fingerprint authentication on S10

National Westminster (NatWest) Bank and Royal Bank of Scotland (RBS) decided to pull their app from Google's Play Store for customers with Samsung Galaxy S10 phones.

As expected, customers started to ask questions and the banks motivated the decision quoting security concerns regarding the security of Galaxy S10 devices.

These are not the only financial institutions that took steps to protect their customers. Some users on Reddit, one of them in Israel, say that their banks just disabled the fingerprint authentication feature.

Another user in the U.S. reported that their bank did not let them use Samsung Pay at an ATM. It is unclear if this is also because of a bank's decision or a technical error, but Samsung Pay transactions can be authorized with a fingerprint.

A client of the Nationwide Building Society says that they were recommended to remove their fingerprint from the device.

The fingerprint scanner issue

About a week ago, a couple in the UK noticed that their Samsung Galaxy S10 unlocked when using a different fingerprint than it had registered.

After some poking around, they found that thee odd behavior was caused by a cheap silicone case that covered the screen of the phone. Trying the protective case on a different Samsung, they observed the same behavior.

The issue impacts Galaxy Note10/10+ and S10/S10+/S10, the company said in a statement last week. The problem is with the ultrasonic scanner reading the 3D pattern on the protective case instead of the user's fingertip.

One BleepingComputer reader told us that they were able to unlock their Galaxy Note 10+ with either thumb when they had latex gloves on, although only one print was registered on the phone.

Samsung informed that an update scheduled for this week will patch the problem. This correction should revert banks' decision to block access to the mobile app to their Galaxy S10/Note 10 customers and reinstate fingerprint authentication.
