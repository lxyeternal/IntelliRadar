
	   


A ransomware attack over the weekend has taken down the ticketing system and patron database for the Shakespeare Theatre of New Jersey and has also affected at least one other organization in the Madison area. 

This could not come at a worse time for the Theatre as they were getting ready to begin their first scheduled performance of Charles Dickens' "A Christmas Carol" on December 4th.

In a press release on the theatre's web site they explain that customer's data, including credit card information, was already encrypted and was not available to the attackers during the ransomware attack. Due to the attack, though, customer data, including ticketing information, is no longer available to the theatre.

While this has had a large impact on the theatre and their shows, they do not plan on letting this stop them and have stated that "The show must go on and this theatre must go on! "

The theatre also asks their patrons to be patient and understanding with the new ticketing process that requires purchased ticket holders to bring a copy of their tickets or confirmation email when attending a show.

"Dear patrons, please know that this is truly an existential crisis for us, but we are so determined not to let it threaten our existence. We have a great season planned for you in 2020, which we are announcing during the run of A Christmas Carol. We will do everything we can to make your holiday visit here a good one and we will be rebuilding our database in every spare moment we have, in every way we can, in every effort to continue to serve you well. But this will be a Herculean task, and we ask that you lend us your kindness, your patience, your understanding, and your help in rebuilding the system by which we contact and stay connected with all of you. This is a very difficult time, and yet we will do everything in our power to retain your loyal patronage and admiration."

Jessica Damrow Sherman, Director of Marketing and Public Relations for the Theatre, told BleepingComputer that they are still investigation how the attackers infected their server, but are not letting it stop their shows.

"The Shakespeare Theatre has been overwhelmed with support from our patrons, community and fellow theatres in the area. No matter what, the show must go on!"

Other local organizations affected

According to the Shakespeare Theatre's press release, other organizations and companies in Madison were also affected.

"The attack was widespread, hitting several companies of various types and sizes in our area and beyond."

The New Jersey Stage reports that the Museum of Early Trades and Crafts (METC), which is also located in Madison, was also affected, but no details are available as to whether the attack affected their services.

Sherman told BleepingComputer that the two organizations did not share the same support company or had their servers hosted at the same facility.
