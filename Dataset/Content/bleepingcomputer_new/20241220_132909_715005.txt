
	   


Over the weekend, a hacker gained unauthorized access to the Queensland EWN, or Early Warning Network, and used it to send a spam alert via SMS, landline, and email to the company's subscribers.

EWN is a service offered by Australian company Aeeris that allows Australian councils, or local governments, to send emergency alerts regarding extreme weather, fires, evacuation information, or incident responses.

The unauthorized alerts stated that "EWN has been hacked. Your personal data is not safe." They then went on to tell recipients to email support@ewn.com.au to unsubscribe from the service. An example of one of the alerts can be seen below.

According to a notice issued by EWN, a hacker gained access to their system on January 5th at 9:30PM EDT and began to send alerts to Queensland residents. When the EWN staff noticed the unauthorized alerts, they stopped the attack by turning off the system. By that time, though, a portion of the their subscriber database had received the alerts.


"At around 930pm EDT 5th January, the EWN Alerting system was illegally accessed with a nuisance message sent to a part of EWNs database. This was sent out via email, text message and landline. EWN staff at the time were able to quickly identify the attack and shut off the system limiting the number of messages sent out. Unfortunately, a small proportion of our database received this alert. Our systems are back up and running providing ongoing alerts for severe weather and natural hazard events. Investigations are continuing with police involvement.

Update 11am EDT 7th January: The unauthorized alert sent on Saturday night was undertaken by an unauthorized person using illicitly gained credentials to login and post a nuisance spam-notification to some of our customers. The link used in this alert were non-harmful and your personal information was not compromised in this event. Investigations are continuing with the Police and Australian Cyber Security Centre involved."


Acorrding to EWN, the third-party gained access to the service by using the credentials of an authorized person. It is not known how those credentials were stolen or how many people received the alerts.

BleepingComputer has contacted Aeeris with questions, but had not heard back by the time of this publication.
