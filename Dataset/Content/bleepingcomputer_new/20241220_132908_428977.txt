
	   


A new and upgraded variant of the JasperLoader malware downloader has been observed in the wild actively targeting Italian victims and featuring new capabilities such as new anti-analysis mechanisms, extra layers of obfuscation, and geofencing abilities.

Just as at the end of April, the last time it was analyzed by the Cisco Talos research team, JasperLoader "continues to feature multiple stages which are used to establish a foothold on systems, initiate communications with attacker-controlled infrastructure and implement the core functionality of the loader."

As Cisco Talos discovered, this malware's activity has been picking up during the last few months, with the operators behind the malspam campaigns which drop it on targets from Central Europe showing an obvious focus on potential Italian and German targets.

Even though overall JasperLoader's structure has not changed very much since April, its maintainers have been hard at work adding quite a few new modules and features to improve its evasion skills and ability to stay in touch with its masters.

The latest version has added sandbox and virtual machine detection which allows it to stay one step ahead of malware analysts and anti-malware solutions which would try to dissect it by automatically terminating itself when it's executed in a virtual environment.

The previously observed JasperLoader variant used several obfuscation techniques to make analysis more difficult leveraging "character replacement mechanisms and perform mathematical calculations at runtime to reconstruct the PowerShell instructions that will be executed on infected systems."


Decoy PDF document

A new layer of character replacement was added to obfuscate the underlying PowerShell even further as Cisco Talos research team discovered when taking a closer look at the inner workings of this new JasperLoader variant.

During the initial stage when the infection process is started with using a VBS script, the upgraded malware will now also display decoy PDF documents designed to keep the victims busy while the second stage is loaded in the background.

To make sure that the malware only hits a very specific range of targets from a very particular location, its creators have now added a kill switch which will automatically terminate the malware if it detects that the system language is set to Romanian — this adds to the checks for People's Republic of China, Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus locales from previous versions.


Locale-based kill switch

Fallback C2 communication

To top it all off, the new JasperLoader version "has also implemented a new bot registration and ID generation mechanism and utilizes different pieces of information to create a unique identifier for each system."

In addition, a fallback command-and-control (C2) domain retrieval mechanism is also included in the new JasperLoader variant which adds time-based fluxing capabilities.

When the default C2 server goes down, "the current date on the system is used to generate a series of failback domains that the malware will attempt to use for C2 communications."


Fallback C2 function

 

The JasperLoader malware downloader is used by malicious actors who want to make the job of dropping various payloads onto to their victims' machines simpler since they make it easier to maximize profits by quickly switching the pushed malware when adapting malware campaigns for different targets.

As uncovered by the researchers, JasperLoader has been distributed via multiple campaigns during the past months and it has been earlier used to drop the Gootkit banking Trojan — previously disseminated by DanaBot, Neutrino exploit kit, and Emotet — which acts as a backdoor and can be used by cybercriminals to steal financial information.

A full list of Indicators of Compromise (IOCs) related to several malicious campaigns distributing JasperLoader is provided by the Cisco Talos researchers at the end of their report.
