
	   


A 22-year-old Washington man was sentenced to 13 months in prison for renting and developing Mirai and Qbot-based DDoS botnets used in DDoS attacks against targets from all over the world.

Schuchman, also known as Nexus Zeta, pleaded guilty to the charges of being involved in the creation and operation of the Satori [1, 2], Okiru, Masuta, and Tsunami/Fbot botnets and was released to the United States Probation and Pretrial Services on September 3, 2019.

"Cybercriminals depend on anonymity, but remain visible in the eyes of justice," U.S. Attorney Schroder said in a DoJ release.

Canada and UK nationals also charged

According to court documents, these botnets were originally based largely on the Mirai botnet's source code developed by other individuals (Josiah White, Paras Jha, and Dalton Norman) who got five years of probation after helping the FBI catch other cybercriminals.

Schuchman and two criminal associates known as Vamp (Aaron Sterritt, a United Kingdom national) and Drake (Logan Shwydiuk, a Canadian national) upgraded their Mirai-based botnets with new features to make them more complex and effective.

The botnets were used to infect and control hundreds of thousands of networked embedded devices such as home routers, IP cameras, and other IoT devices worldwide.

Vamp and Drake "have also been charged for their roles in developing and operating these botnets to conduct DDoS attacks, following an investigation by the FBI with the assistance of other law enforcement partners," according to the DoJ release.

The three accomplices rented their successor botnets to others who wanted to launch DDoS attacks on various targets but they were also used by Schuchman and his accomplices to conduct DDoS attacks themselves.



Armies of bots and DDoS attacks

According to court documents, until August 2017 their botnet operated under the name Satori and enslaved approximately 100,000 devices, with Vamp as the main coder, Drake as the group's sales manager and customer support, and Schuchman as their exploit coder and buyer.

Around September or October 2017, they further improved their botnet and rebranded as Okiru. In November 2017, renamed the botnet again to Masuta after adding the capability to infect vulnerable Huawei and Gigabit Passive Optical Network (GPON) devices, soon amassing a 700,000 army of bots.

In January, Schuchman combined Mirai and Satori botnet components to expand the size of the bot army and focused on exploiting devices from Vietnam.

Around March 2018, the group further upgraded the botnet, rebranding it as Tsunami/Fbot and designing to target Goahead camera devices, gaming servers, and High Silicon DVR systems. During this stage, the botnet was able to launch attacks exceeding 100 Gbps.

In April 2018, the defendant created a Qbot-derived DDoS botnet, directly competing for customers with his former conspirator, Vamp.

"By July 2018, when Mr. Schuchman was first interviewed by the FBI, he was again working with Vamp and Drake to improve their active series of DDoS botnets," the court documents add.

Schuchman was sentenced by Chief U.S. District Judge Timothy M. Burgess to 13 months in prison, followed by a term of 18 months of community confinement and a three-year term of supervised release.

The investigation was conducted by FBI’s Anchorage Field Office with assistance from Akamai, Cloudflare, Google, Oracle, Palo Alto Unit 42, Unit 221B, and the University of Cambridge, among other partners.
