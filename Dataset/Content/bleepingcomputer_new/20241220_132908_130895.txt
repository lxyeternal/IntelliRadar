
	   


A cyberattack reportedly bearing the signature of Ryuk ransomware caused disruption over the weekend in printing and delivery of major newspapers in the US from Tribune Publishing and Los Angeles Times.

Among the publications affected by the attack include the Wall Street Journal, New York Times, Los Angeles Times, Chicago Tribune, and Baltimore Sun, Lake County News-Sun, Post-Tribune, Hartford Courant, Capital Gazette, and Carroll County Times.

Ryuk ransomware strain came to attention in August 2018 when it was reported to have made over $640,000 in Bitcoin to the group behind it. According to a tweet from MalwareHunterteam on december 3, Ryuk operators received from victims over 400 BTC in about four months. The price for one BTC on the day of the tweet was $3,947.70.

The malware is typically used in targeted attacks carried out through phishing, but it could also be planted through insecure remote desktop connections.


Ryuk ransom note

An analysis from security company Check Point shows code similarities with Hermes, a ransomware strain attributed to the North Korean hacker group Lazarus.

The ID Ransomware platform that can identify strains of file-encrypting malware also labels files encrypted by Ryuk as touched by Hermes ransomware.

Update: The ID Ransomware platform currently identifies Ryuk ransomware based on the extension of the encrypted files. If a user uploads a Ryuk-encrypted file with the extension 'ryk' without the accompanying ransom note, the detects Ryuk as the source of the encryption, not Hermes ransomware. h/t MalwareHunterTeam

A statement explaining the incident from Los Angeles Times informs that it was caused by a computer breakdown.

The publication offered more details in a later article where it says that the outage was due to a "malware attack, which appears to have originated from outside the United States and hobbled computer systems and delayed weekend deliveries of the Los Angeles Times and other newspapers across the country."

This resulted in delayed distribution of Saturday editions of the multiple newspapers, some of them sharing the same production platform.

In a statement on Saturday, Tribune Publishing said that “the personal data of our subscribers, online users, and advertising clients has not been compromised.”

The Los Angeles Times says that several unnamed sources close to the investigation point to Ryuk ransomware as the one responsible for the outage. A company insider said that files on infected computers from Tribune Publishing had the extension '.ryk.'

The Department of Homeland Security is aware of cyber incidents impacting multiple news outlets but it is unclear if these are related to Ryuk ransomware or other types of attacks.
