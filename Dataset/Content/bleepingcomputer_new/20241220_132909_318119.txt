
	   


A key member of a crime group behind the notorious Reveton Police Trojan that locked users out of Windows unless they paid a ransom has now found himself locked up in jail.

In 2012, many Windows users from all over the world suddenly found their computers displaying a message stating that law enforcement has locked them out of their computer due to the distribution of child pornography and copyrighted music, videos, and software. This screen would then prompt the victim to send a MoneyPak, MoneyGram, or Ukash payment in order to unlock the computer.


U.S. Variant of Reveton

In August 2018, a former Microsoft employee, Raymond Odigie Uadiale, was charged with laundering money that was obtained through Reveton victims. According to his plea agreement, Uadiale admitted to working with a cybercriminal known as "K!NG", who was involved in the distribution of the Reveton ransomware.

"He bought masses of advertising traffic from pornographic websites, using the online name K!NG, on behalf of the crime group, using fraudulent identities and bogus companies to pose as legitimate online advertising agencies in a process of social engineering.
