
	   


Intel addressed 25 vulnerabilities today as part of its June 2020 Patch Tuesday, with two of them affecting Intel's Active Management Technology (AMT) being rated as critical security flaws after receiving CVSS scores of 9.8.

These issues were detailed in the five security advisories Intel published on its Product Security Center, with fixes addressing them having been delivered to users through the Intel Platform Update (IPU) process before public disclosure.

The company also provides lists of affected products and support for vulnerable products at the end of the advisories, together with contact details for reporting other security vulnerabilities impacting Intel products or technology.

The two Intel Active Management Technology (AMT) critical vulnerabilities were addressed today in new firmware updates and were reported through Intel's Bug Bounty program.

Despite their critical rating, both of security flaws affect non-standard configuration and require Intel AMT to be configured to use Internet Protocol version 6 (IPv6).

Intel says that it is not aware of any customers using AMT with IPv6 but, if there are any who need this feature, the newly released firmware updates mitigate these bugs.

New CrossTalk side-channel issue

A new side-channel vulnerability named Special Register Buffer Data Sampling (SRBDS) by Intel and CrossTalk by the researchers who reported it is detailed in the INTEL-SA-00320 security advisory.

"As with all side-channel issues reported to date, Intel is not aware of any real-world exploits of SRBDS outside of a lab environment," Intel says.

CrossTalk is a transient execution attack allowing attackers to leak data from a micro-architectural buffer and the first cross-core transient execution attack since it focuses on the staging buffer  — a buffer shared between different CPU cores.

The new SRBDS domain bypass transient execution attack "may allow data values from special registers to be inferred by malicious code executing on any core of the CPU" according to Intel.



The CrossTalk vulnerability affects some client and Intel Xeon E3 processors but it does not affect other Intel Xeon or Atom processors. 

Intel has released microcode updates for affected processors to address the root cause. "On systems that have loaded the microcode with the mitigation, the processors are fully mitigated by default," Intel explains.

A deep dive whitepaper on SRBDS with a lot more details including an overview of the flaw, its impact and use cases, as well as mitigation measures is available here, while the researchers published their paper here.

SGAxe attack targets Intel SGX

A new attack dubbed SGAxe makes it possible to recover SGX attestation keys from Intel's quoting enclave, allowing network attackers to cryptographically impersonate legitimate SGX Intel machines.

SGAxe relies on the CacheOut attack (also known as L1D Eviction Sampling) disclosed by researchers at the University of Michigan, University of Adelaide, and Data61 in January 2020.

According to the researchers who discovered it, the SGAxe attack can be successfully used against devices using Intel's 9th gen Coffee Lake Refresh processors, fully updated with all the SGX countermeasures published so far by Intel.



Intel's Product Security Incident Response Team (PSIRT) published the full list of processors potentially affected by SGAxe here.

"The CacheOut researchers recently informed us of a new paper referred to as SGAxe," Intel Director of Communications Jerry Bryant said today.

"It is important to note that SGAxe relies on CVE-2020-0549 which has been mitigated in microcode (confirmed by the researchers in their updated CacheOut paper) and distributed out to the ecosystem."

More details on the SGAxe attack are available in the "SGAxe: How SGX Fails in Practice" research paper.
