
	   
A group known as NoTrove is driving massive amounts of traffic to survey pages, scams sites, and shady software download portals, so much so that one of the domains they used in their campaings peaked at #517 in Amazon's Alexa traffic ranking, according to a report released today by digital threat management firm RiskIQ.

The company says this group is today's biggest offender in terms of driving web traffic to all sorts shady sites.

NoTrove, a new type of malvertiser

In an email exchange with Bleeping Computer, RiskIQ threat intelligence analyst William MacArthur classified NoTrove as a malvertiser, but different.

Instead of redirecting users that click on their ads to exploit kits, the group sells their traffic to the highest bidders, such as scammers, affiliate programs, and traffic resellers.

One of the survey pages where NoTrove sends users that click on its misleading ads [Source: RiskIQ]

We've all seen these types of sites at one point of another, and many of us wondered why they exist in the first place, and when they're going to die off.

Group is active since 2010

According to RiskIQ, the answer may be "never," as the NoTrove group has enjoyed financial success in the past few years.

Using machine learning to aid their research, RiskIQ says they've tracked down evidence of the group's activity going back to 2010.

Over the years the group appears to have grown in size and sophistication. RiskIQ says they've identified over 3,000 server IPs where the NoTrove group hosted infrastructure used to redirect traffic to their clients (PUP sellers, survey sites, scams, etc.).

Furthermore, the group has gone through an equally large number of domains, RiskIQ reporting that the group operated over 2,000. Most of these appear to be random gibberish, but they're actually well organized.

bogzz.bestprizeland.8702.wsuchzz.pcloadletter.footbaths.xyzrjkzz.super-promo.7891223.comtcfzz.stream.7891223.com

Above are some of the domains used by NoTrove in their past campaigns. The first part of the URL, in red, is just random text, most likely different per various campaigns.

The second part of the URL, in green, is a label for the type of campaign. RiskIQ says it has seen 78 different variants representing different NoTrove payloads (scams, surveys, download sites, etc.), and the label served as a visual indicator for NoTrove operators.

The third part, in blue, is again a random string, representing the main domain, most likely registered using an automated system.

In most cases, the group hosts these domains on rented Linode or Choopa servers, changing IPs and servers at regular intervals.

NoTrove campaigns handle huge amounts of traffic

During the last year, RiskIQ says that one of the NoTrove domains they've been tracking has funneled so much traffic to its "customers" that it reached the #517 position in the Alexa traffic ranking.

That's more than Vice News, NFL.com, TechCrunch, HackerNews, and SlashDot, just to give an idea of the amount of traffic that landed on shady sites through that domain alone.

While the group does not push users towards exploits kits, banking trojans, or ransomware, NoTrove is a malvertising operator in the true meaning of the word.

"We consider NoTrove malvertising due to the nefarious nature of its activity and the success of its delivery method—leading normal website visitors to unintended places for its own gain," MacArthur explained in an email.

The impact of such a "benign" malvertising group cannot be quantified in computers infected with PUPs, hours wasted by users on their sites, or gigabytes of wasted bandwidth. Their impact is seen in the degradation of the overall quality of the Internet, and loss of user trust in the digital advertising ecosystem, hence the ever-increasing number of users employing ad blockers these days.
