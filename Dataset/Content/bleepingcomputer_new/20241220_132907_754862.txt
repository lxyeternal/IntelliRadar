A new sextortion scam campaign is aiming for the inboxes of targets from Ireland, with the scammers using fictitious recordings of their victims watching child pornography to scare them into sending payments of £5,000 worth of Bitcoins.

The crooks are asking their targets to pay up to avoid having videos of them watching illegal porn online recorded using a spyware implant released to the public, as ESET Ireland researchers found.

This scam is called sextortion, a type of scam that tries to scare you into thinking that you have been recorded while doing things that should stay private so that you will pay their extortion demand under threats that videos with you will be released otherwise.

Sextortion campaign targeting the UK

The sextortion emails' subjects are either "I know you are a pedophile" or "What the **** are you doing, pedophile?" to catch the targets' attention and make them open the messages.

Sending their victims into panic mode is further achieved using a message designed to shock and threaten them into being exposed in front of all their relatives and friends.

Previously targeted the U.S.

This looks like a variation of a previous sextortion campaign targeting U.S. victims in August and asking for $5,000 in Bitcoins, operated by an actor who calls himself 'N1ghTm4r3' (some victims have also received similar emails signed with 'Sp3ctr3'.)

The scam emails discovered by ESET Ireland have some parts of the content slightly tweaked and are specifically designed to target victims from the UK.

Just as the sextortion campaign that targeted the U.S., analyzed by software developer Lukas Lotz, the UK one also uses Unicode characters to bypass spam filters and seems to have been generated based on a simple email address.


'N1ghTm4r3' sextortion email targeting U.S. victims (SPAMREPORTS.REPORT)

"The BTC wallet seems to be customized in every email as well for each mail" Lotz found, with the actor behind the campaign probably being a "script kiddie that just sends autogenerated mails to mail-address lists."

Sextortion campaigns are running rampant

Sextortion as an email scam was first seen in the wild during July 2018, when crooks started emailing their targets claiming that they have them recorded on video while browsing various adult sites. To make their scams messages more believable, some of them also include the passwords that were leaked with the targets' accounts as part of a data breach dump. 

The number of extortion emails delivered each day continuously rising, given that the scammers behind these cybercriminal operations are coming up with new approaches to monetize their leaked credential databases.

According to a report published last month by Symantec, between January 1 and May 29, they have blocked roughly 289 million extortion emails from reaching their customers' inboxes.

Besides extorting money from people fearing that their adult site browsing history will be made public, the crooks have also recently started distributing various strains of malware, ranging from data-stealing Trojans to ransomware.

Sextortion scams are an extremely successful operation, with one of the campaigns, for instance, having raked in over $50,000 in just a single week in July.

If you are on the receiving end of an anxiety-provoking sextortion campaign, you should always keep in mind that it's just a scam and you should not believe anything these emails are saying. The recommended way to deal with them is to just mark them as spam and delete them.
