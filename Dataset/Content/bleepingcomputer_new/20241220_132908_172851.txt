Earlier this month, McAfee provided a look at the GandCrab Ransomware-as-an-Affiliate operation and how the Sodinokibi Ransomware recruited the top performers to build an all-star team of affiliates after GandCrab was shut down.

As part of this reporting, it was shown how an affiliate named Lalartu was vouching for the Sodinokibi Ransomware RaaS on an underground malware and hacker forum.

In a new report, researchers at McAfee continue their analysis of the Sodinokibi Ransomware by following affiliate's trail of money based on transaction IDs posted by the above affiliate.

Following the money trail

While researching the Sodinokibi Ransomware affiliate, McAfee found another forum post from the affiliate named Lalartu who previously had vouched for the RaaS.

In this forum post, the affiliate posted a screenshot of partial transactions ID for approximately $287,499.00 worth of ransom payments in 72 hours.


Transactions posted by Affiliate (Source: McAfee)

By looking at existing samples of the ransomware, McAfee was able to determine that the average ransom payment was between .44 and .45 bitcoins, which equates to about $4,000 USD. This average price also correlates with the ransom payments shown in the above screenshot.

With the help of the blockchain data analysis company Chainalysis, McAfee was able to retrieve the full transaction IDs from the affiliate's post and use them to map out the bitcoin transactions related to these ransom and affiliate payments.


Mapping Ransom Payments (Source: McAfee)
Click image to see full size

This information then became the starting point for McAfee's research into the bitcoin transactions of the Sodinokibi RaaS and its affiliates.

"Lalartu's payments served as a start and we took it from there," McAfee's John Fokker, Head of Cyber Investigations, told BleepingComputer in a conversation. "Looking at affiliates and based on the payment portions etc we went upstream to take a closer look at the Developer portions."

From the collected information, McAfee was able to see other ransomware payments being made and the 60/40 or 70/30 revenue split between the affiliate and the RaaS operators.

"We see victims paying to their assigned wallets; from there it takes an average of two to three transactions before it goes to an ‘affiliate’ or ‘distribution’ wallet. From that wallet we see the split happening as the moniker ‘UNKN’ mentioned in his forum post we started this article with. The 60 or 70 percent stays with the affiliate and the remaining 40/30 percent is forwarded in multiple transactions towards the actors behind Sodinokibi."

By digging into the bitcoin transactions for other Sodinokibi affiliates, McAfee was able to get a better insight into how the ransomware distributors are using their ill-gotten gains.

For example, the affiliate below transferred a small amount of an unknown service and a larger amount to the Bitmix mixer in order to try and make it harder to trace those coins any further.


Tracing an affiliate's transactions (Source: McAfee)

McAfee saw other affiliates using the bitcoins to purchase services on underground marketplaces. These markets accept bitcoins for illegal items such as drugs, weapons, and hacking services.

Even more concerning, one of the larger affiliates that McAfee was able to trace had a wallet containing 443 BTC or approximately $4.5 million USD showing that they are a big player.

"Following the traces of one particular affiliate, we ended up seeing large amounts of bitcoins being transferred into a wallet which had a total value of 443 BTC, around 4,5 million USD with the average bitcoin price."

Bitcoin transactions are clearly becoming less anonymous

While Bitcoin was created to be a decentralized method to send payments and to provide buyers and sellers a greater degree of privacy, with new services such as Chainalysis and others, the privacy is slowly fading away.

Using a large database of already identified bitcoin addresses, these services allow law enforcement to more easily track how bitcoins are being used.

According to a now deleted Reddit AMA from an alleged Chainalysis employee, these services can also be used to track a sender and recipient's IP address in some cases.

Using IP addresses, it makes it even easier for law enforcement to track down the machines used in a bitcoin transaction and potentially the associated users.
