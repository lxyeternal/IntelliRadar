
	   


The U.S. Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) is warning its members of a copycat site is impersonating them and potentially being used in phishing attacks.

FINRA is a not-for-profit organization authorized by the U.S. government to regulate brokers trading in equities, corporate bonds, securities futures, and options. 

In a new regulatory notice, FINRA is warning its members of a site that is masquerading as finra.org, but with an imposter domain of finnra[.]org.

finnra[.]org phishing landing page

Included on this fake site is a registration form that could be used to collect sensitive information that could be used in targeted phishing attacks against FINRA members.


Fake registration form on finnra[.]org

While BleepingComputer has not been able to find any phishing emails related to the finnra[.]org domain, using the DomainTools service, we were able to find another hostname named 'x32team.website' that also resolves to the same IP address.

The x32team.website hostname is affiliated with a group that created a YouTube video in 2018 that demonstrates how to build a malicious document to infect victims.

We could not find any other information related to this group.

According to DNS intelligence company DomainTools, the current IP address hosting the finnra[.]org site is also hosting the following domains.

It is not known if these are all phishing sites or if some are legitimate companies.

Some of the domains, such as bnk-us.com and us-govt.com, are suspicious and have not been configured yet on the server.

If you received any emails from finnra[.]org, you should immediately delete them. 

If you mistakenly registered at the site, you should also be on the lookout for targeted spear-phishing attacks utilizing the entered information.
