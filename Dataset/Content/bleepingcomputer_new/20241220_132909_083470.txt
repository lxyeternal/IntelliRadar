
	   


As text messaging has become a common form of communication within a business, Business Email Compromise (BEC) scammers have started to go mobile by utilizing SMS messaging to direct their targets.

A BEC phishing scam is when attackers contact a company's employee and pretend to be the CEO or another executive at the company. They then ask the victim to perform a task for them, which typically involves the employee transferring funds to an attacker's account or more purchasing gift cards whose codes and PINs are then sent to the attackers.

Traditionally, BEC scams are conducted via email by spear-phishing a target and then directing victims to perform various actions. As texting has become more comfortable and easier for directing people to perform a quick task, BEC scammers have started switching to SMS texts to direct their victims throughout the entire phishing process.

Initial Phishing Email

Once a victim responds, Agari states that the attackers know that they have a victim who has fallen for the scam, which places them in a strong position. They can then switch to using SMS text messages in order to get what they need done quickly and efficiently.

"Once a recipient replies with their cell number, the actor is immediately placed in a strong position. They’ve minimized the risk of the initial display name deception being noticed, and they have established a method of communication that, by its very nature, places greater expectations to make a more instantaneous reply than that of its email counterpart. In terms of operational security, the scammers have not increased their risks of being identified by any tangible amount. Even if the gang was operating from a country other than the US, it costs no more than a few dollars to set up a temporary US-based number, and there are many legitimate services which provide numbers relating to any state, and even any country, available both online and on Google and Apple’s respective app stores. "

In order to do this, BEC mobile scammers are utilizing services such as Google Voice, which offers a wide range of features that allow them to perform multiple attacks from the same U.S. phone number.

"Having access to a US number also enables the actor to create a Google Voice number, which still prove to be extremely popular with non-US based cyber-gangs. Features such as being able to create personalized greetings that can be assigned to specific callers makes the use of multiple personas easy to manage. You can also send and receive SMS messages directly from a computer, allowing the actor to remain within the environment from which they run the majority of their operations. There’s also the ability to record voice calls, and voicemails are automatically transcribed and forwarded to you contained within an email, which allows the gang to keep a detailed record of what has been said, helping to avoid any costly mixups. "

Using a service like Google Voice makes it easy for scammers to conduct operations against multiple targets at once by sending their texts via the desktop web site.


SMS Message via Google Voice

Once they start communicating with a target they can quickly direct them to go to a local store and purchase gift cards. They then ask the target to send the scratched off codes back to them via text. Using SMS texts makes this process easier as the attackers can ask the victims to send pictures of the scratched off codes rather than typing them in.


Phishing directing a target

Once the attackers have the codes, they quickly convert them into bitcoin using online marketplaces like Paxful. Once they have converted the gift cards to bitcoins, they can use other services to launder the bitcoins into a fiat currency.


Converting Gift Cards to Bitcoins

To protect against these types of attacks it is important for employees to be trained properly on how to react and report emails that request any sort of financial task. Furthermore, employees should confirm any request to transfer funds or perform some other monetary transaction.

This confirmation should not be done via email in the event that the account is compromised, but rather by calling and asking the person who allegedly sent the email. 

Business insurance may not cover BEC scams

BEC phishing scams have become a nightmare for corporations who fall for them as they could lead to the loss of significant revenue that may not be covered under business insurance policies.

For example, when Apache Corporation was scammed out of $7 million due to a BEC attack, a judge ruled that it was not covered by their insurance policy because they sent the money for a legitimate invoice to the wrong bank account.

On the other hand, when American Tooling Center was scammed out of $834,000, a judge ruled in their favor and stated it was covered under their policy because it constituted Computer Fraud.

Due to the different interpretations of policies by the courts, it is important that business not only prevent these types of scams, but also make sure their insurance coverage specifically covers BEC attacks.
