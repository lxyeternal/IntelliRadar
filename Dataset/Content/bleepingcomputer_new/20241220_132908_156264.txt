
	   


A scammer was able to successfully fooled the City of Saskatoon into transferring them a little over $1 million. They may not enjoy the wealth, though.

The city fell victim to classic business email compromise (BEC) fraud, also knows as email account compromise (EAC) - a phenomenon that last year caused over $1.2 billion in losses in the U.S. alone.

Money traced and blocked

According to local news, the city of Saskatoon wired $1.04 million to someone that impersonated the Chief Financial Officer of Allan Construction, a company contracted for the rehabilitation of a bridge.

This is achieved by using payment documents that look legitimate but were modified to include the attacker's bank account. The targets are employees in the financial department, who receive an email instructing them to make a payment.

The payment was made around August 7 or 8. Immediately after its discovery on August 12, authorities that include both law enforcement and financial organizations were notified in an attempt to reverse the transaction and recover the money.

According to city manager Jeff Jorgenson, most of the funds have been traced to several bank accounts, between 10 and 15, that have been frozen by court order.

Many of the accounts used by the fraudster are in Canada and had not been emptied, which explains how the city was able to freeze them. Had the funds been transferred to banks overseas, the cybercriminal(s) would have gotten a hefty payout.

The money recovery process is in early stages and the city received only an installment of $40,000 until now. It is unlikely that the full amount will return to the city, but Jorgenson is confident that they will get back most of it.

The administration started to tighten security controls for financial transactions in order to prevent future fraudulent events.

BEC is the business of specialized gangs

It is unclear who is the author of the heist. Typically, BEC scams are the work of gangs that are not only specialized in this sort of fraud but also experienced in running confidence tricks.

A recent report from the FBI warns that romance scam victims are often recruited as unwitting money mules for the perpetrators.

Email security company Agari tracked the activity of one BEC group from its inception 11 years ago and saw it grow from a one-man show to a gang with at least 35 members. Several such groups exist acoss the world.

Victims could lose huge amounts to this criminal enterprise. The FBI last week arrested a perpetrator that was part of a group responsible for stealing almost $11 million from the export sales office for Caterpillar industrial and farming equipment. Those transactions could not be reversed.
