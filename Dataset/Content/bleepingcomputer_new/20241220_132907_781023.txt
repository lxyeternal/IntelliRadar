
	   


The Echobot botnet is still after the low hanging fruit as a new variant has been spotted with an increased number of exploits that target unpatched devices, IoT for the most part.

A variant discovered this summer included more than 50 exploits that allow remote code execution. A security researcher noticed that a new version emerged with even more exploits, 77 of them.

Hoarding RCE exploits

Echobot is not new, original, or difficult to defend against but it stands out as an odd one for the exploits it integrates and the diversity of devices targeted.

Security researcher Carlos Brendel found the new Echobot variant and says on Twitter today that he saw an delivered from 10 different devices that had been compromised, a clear sign that the propagation mechanism is doing its job. The binaries, though, are hosted at a single IP address, a different one than seen in the past.

Just like with the previous versions, the malware dropper is on an open server in a file called Richard. Except for the number of entries, the new list of payloads is not much different than before. The image below shows the exploits received by the security researcher.


source: Carlos Brendel

It looks like the author of the botnet is hoarding all the exploits they can integrate as some of them are from 2009. Newer ones are from 2019 and target products that are not used by consumers, like Mitsubishi Electric smartRTU (remote terminal unit) or Schneider Electric U.Motion Builder, or the Yachtcontrol web app.

Growing list of RCE exploits

When researchers at Palo Alto Networks it was first disclosed Echobot for the first time in early June, the botnet relied on 18 exploits. That count grew to 26 about a week later, as discovered by Larry Cashdollar from Akamai.

In early August, Brendel found that the list of exploits had exploded to almost 60 exploits that enabled remote execution of code on a vulnerable device.

As far as the targeted processor architectures are concerned, the author does not discriminate and covers a wide range - ARM/4/5/6/7, x86, MIPS, PPC, SuperH, as visible in the image below. Devices on MIPSL are also targetet but an error in the dropper's location path renders Echobot ineffective for these systems.


source: Carlos Brendel
