
	   


Two apps in the Google Play that collectively had been installed over 1.5 million times used a creative method to load ads without even showing them to the user.

The stealthy technique completes with an automated ad-clicking process that contributes to increased revenue for the author.

Impressive figures

The tactic allowed Android developer Idea Master to keep in Google Play for about a year two of their apps engaged in this type of illicit activity.

It was last updated on August 23 and its install count is over 1 million. The average rating from almost 4,000 users is 3.7.

The other one is a fitness app named 'Beauty Fitness: daily workout, best HIIT coach.' Its install count is over 500,000 and had an average rating of 4.8 from 3,300 reviewers.



Users that have installed these two apps are advised to remove them, lest they want to experience the negative impact they have on the Android device.

Clever ad loading

The mischievous activity starts with a message from the system via the Android Notification Manager. Clicking it triggers the display of a hidden view with advertisements.

Martin Zhang and May Ying Tee of Symantec found that the developer used toast notifications to load the ads. This is a way to show unobtrusive messages to the user, such as a note that a certain activity occurred (sending a message, completing a download).



The two researchers explain today that keeping the ads invisible to the user is possible by positioning the Toast object outside the viewable screen area.

The sneaky dev achieved this with the help of the translate() and dispatchDraw() methods to create a Canvas in a blind spot position.



The researchers say that this tactic can also be used to load potentially malicious content on the phone.

When the ads were loaded, the two Idea Master apps started an automated ad-clicking process that generated revenue for the developer.



While users never see the ads, their experience on the device is impacted. The direct effects are slow performance and increased battery usage.

Higher costs due to increased mobile data usage are also on the list of negative consequences from this ghost-click technique.

The two apps managed to remain undetected for a long time because they were wrapped with a legitimate Android packer, typically used to protect intellectual property.


"Android packers can change the entire structure and flow of an Android Package Kit (APK) file, which complicates things for security researchers who want to decipher the APK’s behavior. "


Both apps have been removed from Google Play. However, not being available in the official Android store does not mean that the developer no longer benefits from the illicit ad revenue. Hundreds of thousands of users may still have either app installed and they will keep ghost-clicking until they are removed from the device.
