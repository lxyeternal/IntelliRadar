
	   


A 13-year-old is currently under investigation after he allegedly used a teacher's credentials to hack into his school district's computing system to steal fellow students' personal information and create a "hit list."

As detailed in the initial report from ABC 22, the teen is a student at the Columbus City Preparatory School for Boys and, according to the Columbus Police from Ohio, he "had hacked into his teacher's work account and created a site with a 'hit list' of names, school ID numbers, and dates of births."

Columbus City Schools also told ABC 22 that the kid got the personal information of roughly 60 other students connected to the school he went and used it to build a website he titled "User Names and Passes for Columbus Schools."

The police report also says that he "reportedly told another student that he wanted to shoot up the houses of the kids on his list." Statements from the school district's officials also come to support the teen's reportedly angry outbursts seeing that he also used "threatening words" against them.

Following the accusations made against the Columbus City Preparatory School for Boys student, a district spokesman and the school's principal contacted the families of all the students' on the so-called "hit-list" to inform them about the ongoing investigation.

While 13-year-olds might not be the most capable hackers in the eyes of many grown-ups, this is not the first time a kid this age was found to be behind many other similar events.

For instance, during September 2017, police from the Nara Prefecture, Japan, detained a 13-year-old boy from Osaka charging him with advertising and selling a prank mobile malware designed to block smartphone screens and make them unusable.

During the summer of the same year, Japanese police arrested another kid, this time a 14-year-old from Takatsuki, who created and uploaded ransomware online out of curiosity using bits and pieces of code he found on the web.

Teens also hacked airports and stole cryptocurrency

Earlier that year, in January, a 14-year-old US teen from Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, admitted he attempted to hack into the IT systems of the Brussels Zaventem Airport, the day after two ISIS bombings at the airport and at the metro station killed 32 people on March 22, 2016.

When he was questioned by the FBI about his motives, the boy said that he thought it would be a good time to hack the airport, seeing that the staff would be most likely busy with other tasks.

Also, in February 2018, a 17-year-old kid from Japan was arrested for allegedly creating malware that steals cryptocurrency wallet private keys, while 18-year-old teen Michaela Gabriella King from Murrysville, Pennsylvania, was charged by US authorities with launching DDoS attacks against the Franklin Regional High School using the BetaBooter DDoS stressor.

Last week, the Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department spokesperson said that a Japanese 18-year-old faces criminal charges for stealing $130,000 (around ¥15 million) worth of cryptocurrency after confirming being the one behind the hacking of the Monappy social media network and Monacoin web-based wallet.
