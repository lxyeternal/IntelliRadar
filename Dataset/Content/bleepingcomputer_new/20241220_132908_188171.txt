
	   
Last week, TrendMicro blogged about an interesting discovery where they observed a new ransomware family called CrypMIC that was impersonating CryptXXX. At first glance, CrypMIC looks so much like CryptXXX that when I first encountered it I just thought it was a new variant.  As you dig down deeper, though, you can start to see the differences.

Like CryptXXX, the CrypMIC variants are being distributed by the Neutrino exploit kit and is installed using a DLL rather than an .exe file.  To a victim, the wording in the ransom notes are almost identical, with the text ransom notes looking almost exactly the same.  It is only when we take a look at the HTML ransom note and the payment servers do we start to see a difference.

There are also technical differences, as outlined by the table from TrendMicro below, but these would not be readily apparent to a victim.

To see the differences, I have created side-by-side comparisons of the outward signs that a victim will see when infected with a CrypMIC or CryptXXX infection. These would be the text and html ransom notes and the payment server screens

For all intensive purposes, the text ransom notes notes are almost identical. The main difference is the personal ID provided by the two ransomware families.  For example, in CryptXXX the personal ID will be 12 hexadecimal characters like 1A1B1C1D1B1D. On the other hand, CrypMIC will be 4 groups of 8 hexadecimal characters separated by colons. For example, 1A1B1C1D:1A1C1D1F:1A1B1C1D:1A1C1B1E.
