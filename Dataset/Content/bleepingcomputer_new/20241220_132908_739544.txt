Over the past few weeks, Apple users have been bombarded with spam via a new method, with spammers leveraging Calendar event invitations.

The entire process is simple to explain. Spammers buy from data traders lists of email addresses belonging to Apple users that have been associated with iCloud accounts.

The spammer sends an event invite to a user's email address. iCloud picks up the event invite and handles it via its Calendar service, creating an event in the user's calendar with the details found inside the invitation, which usually are ads promoting unwanted services.

When the user opens his Calendar app, either on his Mac, iPhone or iPad, he finds his calendar flooded with all sorts of events that contain links to the spammers' products.

If a user declines an event, the spammer receives an event turndown notification, and the spammer knows the email address is associated with an active user, so he can reliably continue to spam the target.

This is where the problem lies since there's no option to dismiss event invites without automatically notifying the sender.

Spammed users are now pleading with Apple to add a "Dismiss without notifying sender" option.

Real problem: Why is Apple allowing total strangers to send you event invites

Some users have also criticized the company for allowing total strangers to add events to another person's calendar just by having their email address.

For its part, Apple Support has acknowledged the problem and says it's working on tracking down and blocking the accounts of event senders. No word yet on introducing an option to dismiss invites without a notifying the sender.

On Reddit and StackExchange, users have come up with an alternative method of dismissing the junk calendar events, until Apple finds a way to filter out all the calendar spam.

Step 1: Open the Calendar appStep 2: Open the Calendars listStep 3: Disable all calendars but the one where all the spam is.Step 4: Go to File > New Calendar and give it a name.Step 5: Move all the spam events to that new calendar.Step 6: Delete the newly created calendar. This should, in theory, delete all the spam events, without sending a notification to the spammer.

There's also a way to send all the spammy event invitations to your email inbox instead of automatically receiving them in the Calendar iCloud service. This way you can let your inbox's spam filters do all the work, and only add the events to your calendar you actually want to attend.

Step 1: In a desktop browser, log into your iCloud account.Step 2: Click the settings icon (⚙) in the bottom left corner of the screen.Step 3: Go to Preferences > AdvancedStep 4: Find the Invitations setting and change it to "Email to [address]" option.

If you think this is a small issue, here are some users voicing their displeasure on Twitter. This is just a fraction of the tweets we were able to find, meaning Apple has a big problem on its hands, one that it better fix quick.
