A free file decryption tool is available for users whose computers got infected with the latest confirmed versions of GandCrab. It can unlock data encrypted by versions 4 through 5.1 of the malware, and some earlier releases of the threat.

The tool is the fruit of the collaboration between security company Bitdefender, the Romanian Police, Europol, and other law enforcement agencies across the globe.

This is the third iteration of the decryptor in a year and its efficiency is limited only against GandCrab 2.x and 3.x. A Bitdefender spokesperson told BleepingComputer that their team are working to make "decryption possible for the few people affected by these versions as well." Save for these strains, the tool can recover files locked by any other version of the malware up to 5.1.

Bitdefender says since their utility was first released last year, it helped almost 10,000 users, who otherwise would have had to pay over $5 million in ransom fees to retrieve their files.

Victims that used the decryptor were spread across the globe, but most of them were in South Korea, followed by China and India. As far as active infections go, the United States is in the lead, followed by the United Kingdom.

Hours from releasing the new variant of the utility, over 500 users turned to it to decrypt their files. Many more are expected to download it in the following days and to decrypt their data.

A new challenge awaits

While version 5.1 is the latest one malware experts confirmed in the wild for GandCrab ransomware, the threat operators are quick at getting back in the game. Historically, the release of a decryption tool was shortly followed by a new variant of the ransomware.

This may be the case again, as reports online suggest that a new strain, already tagged as GandCrab 5.2, has already been available for several days.



Malware analyst Valthek, who created free decryption tools for GandCrab v5.0 through 5.04, says that the new version of the ransomware, which does not have a decryptor at the moment, may have emerged about three days ago. 

However, he is unsure in his assessment:



Bitdefender confirmed that there is a new GandCrab variant for which no decryption tool exists. This is not unexpected, as its operators are constantly looking for new ways to maintain and expand their business.

"Last year, some GandCrab affiliates began attacking organizations via exposed Remote Desktop Protocol instances, or by directly logging in with stolen domain credentials. After authenticating on a compromised PC, attackers manually run the ransomware and instruct it to spread across the entire network. Once the network is infected, the attackers wipe their traces clean and contact the victim with a decryption offer," the company writes in a blog post today.

The cybercriminals running the operation also started to target companies recently, using vulnerabilities in remote support software that service providers rely on to handle customer workstations.

Update [06.18.2019]: Law enforcement agencies around the world working with Bitdefender have released an updated version of the decryption tool that can unlock files encrypted by  GandCrab 1, 4, and 5 through 5.2.
