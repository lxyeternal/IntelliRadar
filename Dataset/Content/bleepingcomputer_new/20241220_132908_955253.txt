
	   


An adware campaign supported by as many as 182 mobile apps managed to push advertisements to millions of users on Google Play and third-party Android store.

Most of the apps made it to the official Android repository and just eight of them had been installed more than nine million times before they were finally removed.

The ad-spewing conduct was masked by the functionality related to games and phone camera; the sort of apps that are very popular among Android users. The software would function as expected by the user, for at least 30 minutes; then the malicious code would kick in blasting full-screen advertisements that could not be closed immediately.

The half-hour delay interval was present in the earlier code of the apps, versions 1.0.0 to 1.0.2. Later updates increased the delay time to 24 hours and added communication with a command and control server.

Keeping the malicious code from running for a longer period could help the campaign operators as their apps would be more likely to receive reviews from genuine users. This would explain the higher ratings for some of the apps.

This would also confuse security tools waiting for them to exhibit malicious behavior in a sandbox environment, says Jessie Huang, mobile threats analyst at Trend Micro in a report on Monday.



To last longer on the device, the icon would be hidden from the user, making it more difficult to remove them using the quick uninstall procedure - tap the icon and drag it to the 'uninstall' section appearing in the upper part of the display.

After the malicious code activates, the ads would show on the entire screen the moment the device was unlocked. They would continue to pop up at specific intervals, sometimes as frequent as five minutes.

A show count is present in the code, which allowed the operator to define how long the ad would be visible before displaying the close button. Trying to go back to a previous screen would show users the 'Open with' dialog as if they were trying to launch a file.



In total, Trend Micro found 111 apps on Google Play and the rest spread on third-party stores like 9Apps and PP Assistant.

Only 43 of the adware apps found in the official Android repository "were unique or had distinct features." The rest were duplicates of these or other iterations. None of them are currently available on Google Play.

According to the clues found in the package names, publishing times, code structure, style, and features, the researchers inferred that the adware campaign had started in 2018. This means that they've been present in Android store for at least half a year. Trend Micro discovered the campaign in mid-June this year and alerted Google.
