Australian ministers and their offices were not impacted by the attack because they use a different set of servers than the ones which were breached.

Our cyber experts believe that a sophisticated state actor is responsible for this malicious activity. Let me be clear though, there is no evidence of any electoral interference. We have put in place a number of measures to ensure the integrity though of our electoral system.


The Australian Prime Minister also commanded the country's cybersecurity intergovernmental and interagency hub to offer its support for parties and electoral bodies with support, and brief all state and territories cybersecurity agencies of the attack.

Liberal, National, and Labor parties targeted

"I've instructed the Australian Cyber Security Centre to be ready to provide any political party or electoral body in Australia with immediate support, including making their technical experts available," also said Morrison.

Alistair MacGibbon, the head of the Australian Cyber Security Centre, declared that it's still not known what information the attackers targeting Australia's Liberal, National, and Labor parties were able to access during their incursion within the Australian Parliament's network.

However, as asserted by MacGibbon during a press conference:


We know that this state actor has been interacting with those networks. The sophistication of their methodology to operate in those systems gives us the confidence to say it is a state actor. There are a limited number of countries but we have low confidence at being able to publicly state who we think it is.


The attack was not yet attributed to a specific attacker

Following the attack detected on February 8, the presiding officers said press release that they had to "reset all user passwords that have network access. All users have been required to change their passwords."

Furthermore, "We have no evidence that this is an attempt to influence the outcome of parliamentary processes or to disrupt or influence electoral or political processes. Accurate attribution of a cyber incident takes time and investigations are being undertaken in conjunction with the relevant security agencies."

The attack occurred right before the 2019 Australian federal election which will be held during May, thus raising concerns that any theft of sensitive information from MPs and their staff might be used in election interference campaigns.
