
	   


A cybercriminal group specialized in infecting online shops to steal payment card data. is responsible for compromising almost 700 websites and more than a dozen third-party service providers.

Named UltraRank, the gang has been active since at least 2015, using several web skimmers, malicious JavaScript code also known as JS sniffers.

The gang is selling the pilfered payment info through an affiliate card shop, making tens of thousands of U.S. dollars in weekly profit.

Security researchers at Group-IB say that UltraRank changed tactics and infrastructure several times over the years. This made it difficult for investigators to connect operations and caused their campaigns to be associated with separate groups.

In a technical report this week, the researchers provide evidence that UltraRank is the actor behind incidents attributed to Magecart Groups 2, 5, and 12.


"UltraRank went far beyond the notion of ordinary JS sniffer operators, having developed an autonomous business model with a unique technical and organizational structure" - Group-IB


In three long-term campaigns launched in 2015, 2016, and 2018, the gang was able to plant JS-sniffers on 691 individual websites with large traffic like sports events ticket resellers.



However, this figure is conservative considering the group's hacking of 13 providers of web services (design, marketing, development, advertising, browser notification) likely used by thousands of sites across the world.


"By injecting malicious code into the scripts of the products offered by these companies, which were subsequently placed on the web resources of online stores, cybercriminals were able to intercept customer bank card data on all online stores where the infected scripts were used" - Group-IB


Among these victims are the French online advertiser Adverline and The Brandit Agency, an advertising/marketing company that also develops websites running the Magento e-commerce platform.

Trail of clues

The three campaigns from this threat actor relied on JS sniffers that Group-IB calls FakeLogistics, WebRank, and SnifLite. They share some common features and infrastructure that allowed tracking down malicious activity to the group's first attacks:

similar methods to hide the server location and patterns for domain registration
	storing the same malicious code at multiple locations with different domain names
	mixing supply-chain and single-target attacks
The starting point in the investigation was the host "toplevelstatic[.]com" that hosted a JS sniffer used to compromise The Brandit Agency. The same domain stored files that were present in other locations and used in attacks against other online stores.


domain name in reverse

Although Group-IB is confident of UltraRank handling these three operations, the researchers told BleepingComputer that the actor may be involved in other campaigns, too.

Two other operations named by the researchers OldGrelos and LoadReplay used JavaScript and injector code similar to the WebRank sniffer family.



UltraRank turned a pretty penny from this activity. From statistics released by a forum for selling stolen card data, Group-IB learned that the hackers made up to $50,000 in a single week in late 2019.

Monetization was possible through ValidCC, a well-known shop that sells stolen payment data. The collaboration with the illegal store goes beyond selling the cards, though, as UltraRank also used its infrastructure to attack phishing websites impersonating ValidCC.



Technical evidence clearly shows the link between UltraRank and ValidCC. The connection is an SSL certificate for three domains used by the shop that was also present on UltraRank's infrastructure.

The cooperation between specialized marketplaces and gangs stealing bank cards from online shops shows that cybercriminals have organized and fine-tuned their operations to obtain the highest profits.

Victor Okorokov, threat intelligence analyst at Group-IB says that JS sniffers [Magecart] are an evolution of the tools for compromising bank card data, making the attacks less resource-intensive.
