The main activity of the two apps was not spying on users but aggressively pushing adware that covered the entire screen of the Android device.

Dangerous permissions

The two apps are Sun Pro Beauty Camera, which counted more than one million installations by the time it was discovered, and Funny Sweet Beauty Selfie Camera, which had been installed over 500,000 times.

Security researchers from Wandera analyzing how the two adware apps functioned, determined that they had permissions that did not fit with their advertised purpose.

Apart from the normal permissions required by any app that needs access to the camera, there were some concerning ones.

Among them was SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW, which allows the app to overlay arbitrary content. This could be used for clickjacking purposes or to trick users into typing sensitive information, like credentials or banking details.

According to the Android documentation, "very few apps should use this permission" because overlay windows are intended for system-level interaction with the user. For this reason, users need to explicitly grant it.

Another worrying permission is RECORD_AUDIO, which needs little explanation to understand its purpose. However, the recording would start without notifying the user.

This permission allows the app to capture audio using the device's microphone, an obvious privacy risk that landed it on the list of dangerous permissions.

To combat abuse, starting Android 6 (Marshmallow), apps using a dangerous permission need to ask for approval at run time. Once the request is granted, the app typically does not ask again.

Adware activity

Researchers tested the adware behavior of the app on a device with running Android Lollipop, which covered about 14% of the market in May and dropped to 8.65% in August.

Once launched, both apps created a shortcut and then removed itself from the app drawer. This ensures persistence on the device, as users would have to uninstall it from the Apps menu.

The more comfortable method to remove an app is to long-tap the shortcut but the action would only allow removing the shortcut from the screen in this case.

Although both apps display full-screen ads, they are triggered differently. SunPro Beauty Camera did not even need to be opened for the adware to take over the screen.


Users complaining about ads

Restarting the phone has no effect on this behavior as the full-screen ads still pop up and are difficult to close, the researchers say.

With Funny Sweet Beauty Camera, the barrage of unwanted promotions begins only when the app is used to download filtered photos on the device.

To protect against analysis, the author(s) of the apps packed the APKs with Chinese packer Ijiami. This does not mean that all Android apps wrapped with Ijiami are malicious since using a packer is common practice, to protect intellectual property.

Both apps were reported to Google on September 11 and have been kicked from the official Android store. However, they continue to make money and jeopardize user privacy on devices that still have them.

The recommendation is to check for their presence in the Android Settings menu, under Apps, and remove them from there.
