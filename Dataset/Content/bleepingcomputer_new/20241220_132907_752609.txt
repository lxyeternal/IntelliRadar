
	   
Researchers from Red Naga Security have discovered new Android spyware that was used to target government employees, which initially appeared to be tied to the HackingTeam, but might very well be the work of a fellow Italian company, also a maker of surveillance software.

The spyware first came to the attention of Red Naga researchers after a friend of a victim's contacted the company and asked them to take a look at some malicious code.

Spyware can record audio and video, take screenshots

An investigation revealed that the binary sample they received had all the traits of Android malware, such as improperly signed certificates, the fact that it disguised itself as an official Android update package (when it was clearly not), and the practice of requesting all available Android permissions when the binary was installed.

Under the hood, the malware could take screenshots of the user's screen, record screen activity, record audio and video from the user device, covertly talk with a remote C&C server, respond to specific SMS messages, steal geo-location data, and execute code on the device on command.

"There really isn’t much going on outside of the run-of-the-mill, boring, commercial spyware junk," the research team wrote.

"The secret sauce is likely found after talking to the C2 server and getting the extra payloads. "This would appear to be where exploits are being delivered, however it would seem these are set up and configured on the back end. Sadly I was unable to coerce the back end to give me anything worth analyzing."

Malware hosted on servers that previously ran HackingTeam ops

Nevertheless, researchers were able to discover the C&C server locations. A quick search for historical data on those IPs revealed they were once used to host HackingTeam C&C servers.

HackingTeam is a well-known maker of cyber-surveillance software, which they sold to governments around the world, even to oppressive regimes.

In June 2015, a hacker known as Phineas Fisher breached HackingTeam servers and released all their contents on GitHub.

After researchers and reporters sifted through the data and revealed the company's practice of selling spying software to oppressive regimes that then used it against its own citizens, HackingTeam lost its export license.

Malware most likely belongs to another Italian company

However, Lorenzo Franceschi-Bicchierai, an Italian reporter working for Motherboard had spotted something out of the ordinary in one of the certificates used to sign this most recent malware sample, which mentioned the term "raxir."



Raxir is a company with the same profile as HackingTeam. They create and sell malware with the purpose of aiding "criminal investigations."

The only difference, as Bicchierai discovered, is that Raxir never had an export license, and so it only sold the malware internally, to Italian law enforcement.

In fact, one of Raxir's main customers may be the Office of the Prossecutor in Naples, Raxir's hometown.



Red Naga researchers say that more than one sample of this malware is currently available in the wild. The victim who gave researchers the malware sample to analyze is a government employee and said he/she also filed a formal complaint and a legal investigation was started.
