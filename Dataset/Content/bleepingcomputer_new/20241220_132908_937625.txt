
	   


A botnet specialized in gift card fraud is using the infrastructure of nearly 1,000 websites to check the balance of several types of electronic gift cards in order to defraud legitimate card owners.

Distil Networks, a cybersecurity firm specialized in bot attack detection, monitoring, and mitigation, says the botnet, nicknamed GiftGhostBot, has started attacks on February 26, this year.

Attacks aimed at online stores

The operators of this botnet aimed their attacks at the infrastructure embedded in some of the targeted websites, most of which are online stores.

Operators of the GiftGhostBot botnet launch brute-force attacks against these endpoints. Their attacks rely on querying random gift card numbers. If the endpoint responds, this means the card is valid and holds more or fewer funds.

"Armed with that information, the [gift card] account number can be used to purchase goods, or sold on the darkweb for a fee," says Anna Westelius, security analyst for Distil Networks.

It is unknown how many gift cards attackers managed to compromise.

Average attack is 1.7 million requests per hour

The average value of a GiftGhostBot attack is usually around 1.7 million requests (gift card numbers checked) per hour. The security firm says that one of their customers who runs such an endpoint has seen up to four million requests per hour, nearly ten times their normal level of traffic.

In some isolated cases, the attacks can potentially inundate the website's servers with useless requests, leading to slowdowns or downtime, depending on the website's hosting bandwidth.

According to recorded statistics, Distil Networks says the attackers use, on average, over 6,400 unique device fingerprints per hour. Device fingerprints are more accurate when tracking distributed attacks, as IP addresses could be reused.

GiftGhostBot attacks can be thwarted

To stop GiftGhostBot attacks, Distil experts recommend that website owners implement a CAPTCHA system or add a rate limit for "Check-your-Gift-Card-Balance" pages/requests.

Gift card owners should contact authorities if they discover that their gift card was used for unauthorized purchases. Checking gift card balances more often also helps owners detect fraudulent transactions faster, and might give them an opportunity to cancel any operations.

Earlier this month, Distil Networks released the 2017 Bad Bot Report, which details the activities of "bad bots" against legitimate websites. Below are some of the report's conclusions:

97% of sites with unique content and/or product and pricing information were hit with scraper bots
	96% of login pages were hit with bad bots
	82% of signup pages were hit with bad bots
	90% of sites with login pages had bad bots traversing web pages behind those logins pages like payment portals
	31% of sites with web forms (e.g. discussion forums, review and contact us sections) were hit with spammer bots
