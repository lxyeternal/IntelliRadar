
	   


With ransomware attacks running rampant, nation-state actors targeting foreign companies and governments, and democracy under siege through misinformation campaigns, cybersecurity plays an increasingly important role in our society.

For those looking to get started in cybersecurity, there are many ways to start learning, including reading books, playing with malware in a virtual machine, or using crackmes to learn reverse engineering.

Unfortunately, the Practical Malware Analysis and The Art of Assembly Language books can only take you so far, and it helps to have access to experienced researchers and reverse engineers.

Not only training but also a community

Started in May 2020 by two well-known reverse engineers, Vitali Kremez and Daniel Bunce (aka 0verfl0w_), Zero2Automated is more than just an online training course.

Instead, it is a community consisting of a Slack channel where students and teachers can communicate with each other while they finish the 12 lesson course.

When purchasing the course, students will get access to two modules, a beginner focused course, Zero2Hero, and the Zero2Automated reverse engineering course.

Each week, Kremez and Bunce release new blog posts that dive deeper into the subject matter of the lesson currently being discussed.

As part of the course, homework assignments are given that cover tasks that a malware analyst would commonly run into when analyzing malware.

These tasks include extracting configurations from malware, bypassing malware protections, or creating an emulator for a loader.

"No malware analysis course like this one anywhere."

Over the past few weeks, I have had the opportunity to speak to some of the students currently taking the class.

In conversations, students said they loved the unlimited access to the course material, the teachers' knowledge, and the in-depth coverage they received.

Zero2Automated student Danus told BleepingComputer that in their opinion, there "are no malware analysis courses like this one anywhere else."

"What I like most is that vk and Daniel cover all aspects of malware analysis, not just reversing. Reversing is only 50% of the job. They teach thinking patterns, critical thinking, and assumption making," Danus told BleepingComputer.

Those I spoke to did say that students should have some basic knowledge about reverse engineering and malware analysis before getting started.

Without this knowledge, students would need to spend time learning from books first.
