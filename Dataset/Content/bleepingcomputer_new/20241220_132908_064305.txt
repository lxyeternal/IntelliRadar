
	   


The parking garage used by employees of the Canadian Internet Registration Authority (CIRA) allowed people to park for free after computer systems were infected by ransomware.

CIRA is a not-for-profit organization that manages the .CA country code top-level domain (ccTLD) and represents the Canadian domains internationally.

Attack impacts huge parking lot

The attack happened on Tuesday, but the problem persisted the next day when the computer systems allowed entry to CIRA employees without verification of access cards. Responsible for the temporary quick pass seems to be a strain from the Dharma ransomware family.

One of the entrances is at the TD Place Stadium, where barriers were up on Wednesday morning and anyone could get a parking spot for free.



On Wednesday evening, the parking systems at TD Place Stadium were still down as technicians were working to restore their functionality.

A picture sent to BleepingComputer by security analyst David Manouchehri shows a computer system being reinstalled, which indicates that backup files were not available.


courtesy of @DaveManouchehri

The huge parking space is operated by private company Precise ParkLink, so the attack is confined to its systems.

Spencer Callaghan, communications manager at CIRA, said in a blog post that his organization has no knowledge of the cyber security measures of the parking company but he highlighted that many businesses do not install anti-malware solutions and lack a formal patching policy.

"Hackers are starting to exploit those gaps at companies of all sizes and industries. The problem is no longer exclusive to large corporations or data-rich organizations. The tools hackers use are cheap, easy to find, and simple to use, which makes hacking for fun or profit easier than ever," said Callaghan.

BleepingComputer reached out to the parking lot operator to learn more details about the attack and the measures taken to prevent cyber incidents in the future but has not received a reply at the time of publishing.

The ransom note displayed on the screens of infected systems points to Dharma ransomware. Furthermore, the email address "sqlbackup2@mail.fr" present in the message indicates that the variant uses the extension .ETH on the encrypted files.



Dharma ransomware is typically installed manually on systems that have Remote Desktop Services exposed on the internet. Attackers scan the web looking for computers running RDP and try to brute-force their way in.

If the method was used in the case of Precise ParkLink, it is possible that this was not a targeted attack and hackers simply infected the vulnerable computers discovered in the scanning process.

For the time being, there is no free decryption tool for Dharma ransomware. However, keeping regular backups in a safe place ensures a quick restore of the affected systems.
