
	   


A new phishing campaign uses Google search query redirects to send potential victims to a phishing landing page designed to collect Microsoft Office 365 credentials via encoded URLs.

The phishers behind these attacks use URL Encoding (also known as Percent Encoding), a technique that makes it possible to convert ASCII characters in URLs with % signs followed by two hexadecimal digits.

This allows the threat actors to hide the phishing page URL from secure email gateways (SEGs) that scan emails for malicious links and content to block potentially dangerous messages.

Phishing email sample

"The phishing email is simple and originates from a compromised email account of a relatively well-known American brand, informing recipients that they have a new invoice awaiting payment," found Cofense.

The targets are being sent to the phishing page via a hyperlink using a URL build from two parts. The first is https://google.lv/url?q= and the second part is the encoded URL.

When the users click on the "View invoice" button at the bottom of the email, they will be sent to a redirect notice Google page telling that they are being sent to the now decoded phishing domain and that they can return to the previous page if they don't want to proceed further.

"This is enough to fool basic URL and domain checks by perimeter devices, a simple yet effective way for threat actors to ensure delivery of malicious payloads," adds Cofense.


Encoded and decoded malicious URL

When they finally land on the phishing page, the target will see a cloned Microsoft Office 365 login page that will ask them to enter their account's email address and password to sign in.

Various other phishing techniques have been spotted in the past by security researchers while monitoring previous phishing attacks.

For instance, crooks have used copyright infringement notices to bait Instagram users into giving away their credentials by asking them to urgently fill out a 'Copyright Objection Form' within 24 hours to avoid having their accounts suspended.

Another series of phishing attacks infected U.S. taxpayers with the Amadey botnet malware through fake income tax refund emails that also bypassed a secure email gateway in the process.

Phishers also used CAPTCHAs to block email gateways' automated URL analysis feature from reaching and analyzing the phishing landing page used to harvest Microsoft accounts.
