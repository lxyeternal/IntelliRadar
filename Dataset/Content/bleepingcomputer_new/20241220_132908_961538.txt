
	   


A mobile clickfraud campaign used 22 Android apps to trick online advertisers into paying the higher price for advertising on iPhone 5 to 8 Plus devices.

Their capabilities did not limit to ad-fraud, as the developer added downloader capabilities, which allow getting arbitrary files from the developer's server on the mobile endpoint.

Also, the developer made efforts to keep them running on the phone by launching them at boot time and restarting them three minutes after being shut down from system settings.

The most popular of them, a flashlight called Sparkle, got over one million installs.

One explanation for faking the user-agent string to show an Apple device is that advertising networks pay more on average for ads delivered to this platform compared to Android.



Sophos Labs malware researcher Chen Yu says that some of the deceptive apps had been available in the Android marketplace for over one year until Google booted them during the week of November 25.

According to the research, most of the apps were created during or after June 2018, when the clickfraud code appears to have been added to the older ones, too.

Simulating user activity, bogus app names, and device model

"The ad calls do not result in the expected, disruptive, full-screen ads that would otherwise annoy the user of the device and draw attention to the app. Instead, malicious ad calls are made in a hidden browser window, inside of which the app simulates a user interaction with the advertisement," Yu explains.

The only impact this has on the user is a faster battery discharge and increased data usage. Otherwise, everything has normal functionality, as reflected by users in reviews.



Detected as Andr/Clickr-ad by Sophos, the malware communicates with the command and control (C2) server to get the advertisements. The server replies back with the parameters for downloading the ad and with instructions to use a specific iOS app.

Sometimes the instructions also include fake phone model information. This means that the server can control the ad requests launched by the malware for any mobile app and device, and aim for better monetization.

"ua":"Mozilla/5.0 (iPhone; CPU iPhone OS 11_2_6 like Mac OS X) AppleWebKit/604.5.6 (KHTML, like Gecko) Mobile/15D100"
"model": "iPhone",
"make": "Apple",
"pkg":"com.takatrip.ios",
"app_name":"Takatrip"

Yu says that fraudulent activity occurs at times specified by the server and also impersonates 249 Android models from 33 brands, a diversity that covers the most popular devices on the market.

It is worth noting that this behavior was observed only on Android apps; their iOS counterparts did not exhibit ad-clicking functionality.

Together, the 22 fraudulent apps caught by Sophos had been installed more than two million times.
