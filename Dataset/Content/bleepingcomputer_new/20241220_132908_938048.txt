
	   
Another day, another mega breach. Today, LeakedSource announced that almost 100 million, or 98,167,935 to be exact, accounts for the popular Russian portal Rambler.ru were leaked online. What makes this worse was that Rambler was storing account passwords in plain text rather than being encrypted. This means that anyone who was able to hack their database had full access to the user's passwords without having to crack them first.

It appears that on February 17th, 2012, someone was able to hack Rambler and download the entire database table that contained Rambler account information. This table included numerous data fields including email addresses, unencrypted passwords, and ICQ addresses.

LeakedSource performed an analysis of the passwords and the results are unfortunately not surprising. Even though mega breaches are becoming more and more common, users continue to use weak passwords. For example, the most common password in the Rambler.ru breach is asdasd, which was used by 723,039 account holders.

According to LeakedSource, they partnered with Russian journalists due to language barriers in order to confirm whether the account dump was valid. Maria Nefedova who works for xakep.ru contacted three of her friends to confirm if their passwords matched what was in the leaked database. Each one of them was able to confirm that the passwords were indeed the ones that they use on their rambler.ru accounts.

With data breaches becoming very common, users need to devise a way to use strong unique passwords at every site that they register an account. Whether it be by using a password program or by using a unique password creation method, it is becoming imperative that existing password education is not sufficient and users need to take online security seriously.
