Over half of the 415 vulnerabilities found in industrial control systems (ICS) were assigned CVSS v.3.0 base scores over 7 which are designated to security issues of high or critical risk levels, with 20% of vulnerable ICS devices being impacted by critical security issues.

As detailed in Kaspersky's "Threat landscape or industrial automation systems H2 2018", "The largest number of vulnerabilities affect industrial control systems that control manufacturing processes at various enterprises (115), in the energy sector (110), and water supply (63)."

During the last year, Kaspersky Lab ICS CERT unearthed 61 security issues in both industrial and IIoT/IoT systems, with vendors having closed only 29 of them until the end of 2018.

Out of all vulnerabilities identified by Kaspersky, 46% could lead to remote code execution, as well as provide unauthorized control of the compromised ICS device or help potential attackers trigger a denial-of-service (DoS) condition rendering the equipment unusable.

"Importantly, most vulnerabilities (342) can be exploited remotely without authentication and exploiting them does not require the attacker to have any specialized knowledge or superior skills," says the report.


The number of ICS vulnerabilities by type

The highest number of ICS vulnerabilities were found in "engineering software (143), SCADA/HMI components(81), networking devices designed for industrial environments (66), PLCs (47)."

Other ICS components vulnerable to cyber attacks during 2018 also include industrial computers and servers (5%), industrial video surveillance systems (4%), several field level devices, as well as protection relays.

Industrial computers and servers were mainly impacted by vulnerabilities discovered in computer processors such as "Meltdown, Spectre, and Spectre Next Generation (Spectre-NG)" showing that security issues found in consumer-grade computers can also impact ICS devices.


The number of ICS vulnerabilities by typeSome of the statistical data presented in the report was collected anonymously with the help of its distributed antivirus network dubbed Kaspersky Security Network (KSN) from industrial infrastructure at organizations which used the company's security products on SCADA and data storage servers, data gateways, stationary and mobile workstations of engineers and operators, and HMI devices.

Kaspersky also used vulnerability info published on the US ICS-CERT website during 2018 as a source of statistical data, while vulnerability analysis was based on public information from US ICS-CERT, CVE, Siemens Product CERT, and research from Kaspersky Lab ICS CERT.
