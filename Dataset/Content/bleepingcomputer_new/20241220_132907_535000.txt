
	   


JD Finance apologized to the users of its Android application on Sunday after one customer found out that screenshots taken of his account on the China Merchants Bank app were being added to the cache of the JD Finance app while it was running in the background.

According to the user, the screenshots containing sensitive banking information were intercepted by the JD Finance app without permission and, as reported by SCMP, the post came with the following question: "Why is JD Finance keeping a screenshot of my bank card? What you want to do [with it]?"

JD Finance, the financial services business arm of JD.com, a Fortune Global 500 member and a direct competitor of e-commerce and retail Chinese giant, confirmed that its Android app was indeed adding screenshots of banking apps to its own cache according to an official statement posted on Weibo on Sunday, but argued that the snapshots were stored locally and were never sent to its servers.

The storage of screenshots is to enable our clients to pass on images more quickly to the customer service department. As long as users do not send them to us, the cached screenshots are only saved in users’ mobile phones. JD Finance has never uploaded users’ screenshots or photos to our app without permission.


As shown in a video posted on the Chinese Weibo social media platform and gathered 4.5 million views over the weekend (video is available HERE but it requires Flash for playing), the Android JD Finance app would indeed send user screenshots of banking apps to its cache folder, renaming them using a predefined filename template and removing the extension.

Later on, multiple other Weibo users conducted their own tests which led to the same results, confirming the fact that JD Finance's app was definitely collecting banking info into its cache folder.

In a statement sent to jqknews, a JD Finance Customer Service spokesperson stated:


If the user opens Jingdong Financial App and takes a screenshot, we will think that the user may want to complain or suggest to our customer service. In order to facilitate communication between users and customer service, we display picture prompts in the upper left corner of the user interface. Users can further choose whether to contact customer service and send pictures.


JD Finance said that its development team found that the issue was impacting Android app versions higher than 5.0.5 and that it never uploaded any of the screen snapshots to the company's servers without permission. After receiving the user reports, the company also decided to disable the app's "photo assistant" feature in response to customers' user privacy concerns.

Multiple other Chinese smartphone apps are under scrutiny due to personal data collection "including user location, contact lists and mobile numbers" as disclosed by a report of the China Consumers Association from December, with 91 apps out of 100 suspected of excessive data collection, South Morning China Post reports.

In addition, "four government agencies including the Office of Central Cyberspace Affairs Commission and the Ministry of Public Security issued a joint announcement in January to rectify "excessive collecting" of personal information of users by Chinese apps," according to Bloomberg.
