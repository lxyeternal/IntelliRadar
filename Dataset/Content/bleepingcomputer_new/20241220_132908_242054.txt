14 bug bounty programs start in in January 2019, while another one is set to begin on March 1.

From January 7, 2019, researchers can submit security flaws for Filezilla, Apache Kafka, Notepad++, PuTTY, and VLC Media Player via the HackerOne bug bounty and vulnerability coordination platform. midPoint, a platform for identity management governance, is another product the EU wants to be more secure and offers rewards for vulnerabilities reported through HackerOne, starting March 1, 2019.

The Free and Open Source Software Audit

EU's FOSSA project started between 2015-2016 at the initiative of Julia Reda, Member of European Parliament (MEP) from the Pirate Party, and Max Andersson, MEP from the Green Party. She announced the bug bounty plans on in a post on her blog late last week.

The project began with an inventory of the free software used by the European Commission, and a review of how developers deal with the security part in their products. The initiative was extended in 2017 for three more years adding bug bounties on the list of measures intended to strengthen the security of the software products.

"We also planned a series of Hackathons that will allow software developers from within the EU institutions, and developers from Free Software projects, to work more closely together and to collaborate directly on their software," Reda writes on her blog.

The next phase is launching the bug bounty programs, with rewards varying according to the severity of the security issues reported and the importance of the software they affect.

Reda says that a future stage in FOSSA "should also include some direct work on improving Drupal," but gave no details about this. She also agrees that a better approach would be to financially support and motivate developers to build more secure products but that won't happen any time soon because of EU's burdensome public tendering process which favors large consultancy firms rather than developers.
