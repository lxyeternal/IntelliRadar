
	   


Hackers working for the Chinese government deployed attacks against the largest number of industry verticals in the first half of the year.

The sectors of most interest to them rarely align with those from other states running hacking operations, according to a report from threat intelligence and cyber attack response services company Crowdstrike.

Pandas all over the place

The company tracked sophisticated cyberattacks against its customers from multiple state-sponsored actors as well as financially-motivated cybercriminals, who hit various industry sectors in the first six months of 2019.

The Pandas - as Crowdstrike calls all nation-state adversaries linked to China, attacked company customers from eight out of 19 industry sectors.

By comparison, Russian attackers, the Bears, focused on only one vertical: non-governmental organizations (think tanks included). It's important to note that the observations are based only on attacks against Crowdstrike customers.

Similarly, threat actors from Vietnam, the Buffalos, targeted only the automotive industry, while Iranian hackers (the Kittens) set sight on the aviation, and the transportation and logistics sectors.

Intrusions from Chinese attackers spread across the following industries: chemical, gaming, healthcare, hospitality, manufacturing, pharmaceutical, technology, and telecommunications.



Most popular tools

The tools Crowdstrike observed in attacks from all threat actors include legitimate utilities, the most prevalent five being PsExec, ProcDump, PC Hunter, 7-Zip, and Nmap.

Tools intended for penetration testing activity were also used for malicious purposes, the top five of them being Mimikatz, the PowerShell Empire framework, Cobalt Strike framework, reGeorg, and Powerkatz (a PowerShell version of Mimikatz).

As for the malicious payloads, the one Crowdtrike saw most often was China Chopper, a web shell discovered nine years ago and known to still be used with little modifications by several threat groups.

Cybercriminals more prolific

Surprisingly in Crowdstrikes report is a balance shift regarding the attackers' motivation. This year, 61% of the hacking campaigns were associated to cybercriminal activity and the rest of 39% was attributed to state-sponsored actors.

Things were different in 2018 when state hacker activity was more frequent, accounting for 75% of the intrusions seen by the company. Cybercriminals were responsible for the rest of the campaigns.



The company explains that this does not mean that one of the two types of threat actors reduced their activity. Instead, this should be seen as an escalation in targeted campaigns from one type of adversary over the other.
