The fake app can also run overlay attacks on Google Play, WhatsApp, Skype, Viber, Gmail, and bank apps with the purpose of phishing payment card details and login credentials.

Sneaky tactic to steal PayPal funds

The app is available from unofficial third-party stores and goes by the name Optimization Android. Once launched, the app closes and hides its presence. It offers no functionality.

To transfer the funds to the attacker's address, the malware mimics user interaction with the PayPal app.

Researchers found that the malicious app takes advantage of a built-in accessibility service. This is necessary to send a message to the user to launch the PayPal app if it is installed on the device.

The prompt for enabling the accessibility service is disguised as a message to activate statistics for the fake battery optimization software.



"Once the user opens the PayPal app and logs in, the malicious accessibility service (if previously enabled by the user) steps in and mimics the user’s clicks to send money to the attacker’s PayPal address," malware analysts from ESET explain in a blog post.

The notification to launch PayPal can be disguised as anything, as long as the user reacts to it, such as an urgent request to verify the account.

While checking the malicious activity, the app tried to transfer 1,000 euros, the entire action taking about five seconds. An unprepared user would have no chance to stop the fraudulent transaction.

Two-factor authentication offers zero defense in this scenario because the login was a legitimate action fulfilled by the user with the correct secondary authentication challenge.

The video below shows how sneaky is the malicious app and how quickly it completes the transfer to the attacker's address:



The researchers noticed that the accessibility service is active on every PayPal launch, meaning that the attack can occur multiple times.

Running overlay attacks

The researchers say that the malicious app also runs a phishing game to steal card data from Google Play, WhatsApp, Skype, Viber, and Gmail's login credentials. Other apps targeted are from Uber, Netflix, and several banks.

Although these apps are part of the default list, ESET says that the developer can update the record at any time. Hunting the email credentials is probably to delete the messages from PayPal that inform the account owner of the money transfer.



"Unlike overlays used by most Android banking Trojans, these are displayed in lock foreground screen – a technique also used by Android ransomware." 

The victim cannot use the back button to get rid of the overlay screen and their only choice is to fill in the form. A trick the victim can turn to is to type false information.

Apart from these capabilities, the malicious app can intercept, send, and delete text messages, get the contact list, make and forward calls, access the list of installed apps, as well as install and run apps
