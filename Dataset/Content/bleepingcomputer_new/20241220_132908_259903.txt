Contributors and those who provided new ransomware info this week include: @CheckPointSW, @fwosar, @BleepinComputer, @DanielGallagher, @nyxbone, @malwrhunterteam, @demonslay335, @PolarToffee, @JAMESWT_MHT, @Seifreed,  @CryptoInsane, If you are interested in ransomware or security, I suggest you follow all of them on Twitter.

August 14th 2016

PokemonGo Ransomware installs Backdoor Account and Spreads to other Drives

With the popularity of PokemonGo, it was inevitable that a malware developer would create a ransomware that impersonates it.  This is the case with a new Hidden-Tear ransomware discovered by Michael Gillespie that impersonates a PokemonGo application for Windows and targets Arabic victims.

New TorrentLocker (Crypt0L0cker) variant released that Encrypts files with the .ENC Extension

A new TorrentLocker version has been released that changes the extension of encrypted files to .ENC. This campaign was first spotted by Emsisoft security researcher xXToffeeXx and appears to be targeting Italian victims by impersonating a bill from the Italian energy company Enel.



August 15th 2016

The Shark Ransomware Project allows you to create your own Customized Ransomware

A new Ransomware as a Service, or RaaS, called the Shark Ransomware Project has been discovered by security researcher David Montenegro.  The Shark Ransomware Project offers would-be criminals the ability to create their own customized ransomware without needing any technical experience and by simply filling out a form and clicking a button. For this service, the Shark RaaS developers keep 20% of the ransom payments and give the rest of the payment to the distributor/affiliate.



August 16th 2016

Check Point releases working Decryptor for the Cerber Ransomware

Check Point released a short-lived,  but useful while working, decryptor for the Cerber Ransomware. The files types that were able to be decrypted are those that end with the .CERBER and .CERBER2 extensions.  



Criminals earn $195K in July with Cerber Ransomware Affiliate Scheme

Check Point Software, along with IntSights, have released a very detailed report on the Cerber Ransomware and its Ransomware as a Service affiliate system. The revenue generated by the Cerber affiliate system is staggering, with Cerber generating $195,000 in profits for July and the malware developer taking a 40% cut from this total. This equated to the malware developer making $78,000 in July and a forecasted $946,000 for the year! For those interested in learning how these affiliate systems work, I strongly suggest you read the report.

New Korean Ransomware Discovered

A new Hidden Tear ransomware that targets Korean victims was discovered by Michael Gillespie. This ransomware does not seem to be working properly as the TOR site listed in the lock screen goes to a CrypMIC decryption site.



Fabian Wosar continues to defeat Apocalypse Decryptor. Ransomware dev continues to throw Tantrums.

The creators of the Apocalypse ransomware are starting to throw tantrums due to Fabian Wosar's continued decryption of their ransomware. Every time a new Apocalypse variant is released, Fabian has been able to update his decryptor to defeat them.

It has gotten so bad that the Apocalypse devs constantly insult Fabian in their ransomware and have created a fake email address with his name in it.


So apparently the Apocalypse degenerates use "fabiansomware@mail.ru" now. Doubt it's necessary to say it, but I am not involved with them.
— Fabian Wosar (@fwosar) August 16, 2016
 

August 17th 2016

Cerber Ransomware Developers make changes that defeat Check Point's Decryption Service

In just one day, the developers behind the Cerber Ransomware have made changes that blocked Check Point Software from decrypting Cerber victim's for free.  At the same time, the Cerber devs have added a captcha to their payment system in order to login. This captcha would make it so that services could not automate the exploitation of flaws on their servers.



Decryptor created for the Smrss32.exe Ransomware

A decryptor has been made by BloodDolly and Michael Gillespie for the Smrss32.exe. If anyone is infected with this ransomware and wants to get it decrypted, please see this post and follow Michael's instructions.



August 18th 2016

New FSociety Ransomware pays homage to Mr. Robot

Today Michael Gillespie discovered a new EDA2 variant that I have dubbed the FSociety Ransomware based on the image used on the infection's wallpaper. Fans of Mr. Robot, will instantly recognize the image as the logo of the show's infamous hacking group called FSociety.



Bart Ransomware now uses the .Bart Extension

The Bart ransomware is no longer storing a victim's files in password-protected ZIP files. They are now encrypting the files and using the .bart extension rather than .bart.zip.  Unfortunately, a sample has not been found yet, so more details will be coming soon.

August 19th 2016

New DetoxCrypto Ransomware pretends to be PokemonGo or uploads a Picture of your Screen

A new ransomware called DetoxCrypto has been discovered by MalwareHunterTeam that is currently being distributed as two different variants. The first variant looks like a generic ransomware but contains the interesting feature of taking a screenshot of your Windows screen when it is installed. The second variant is trying to take advantage of the immense popularity of PokemonGo, by pretending to be PokemonGo for Windows.
