
	   


The U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) issued a public service announcement today regarding the increasing number of high-impact ransomware attacks against public and private U.S. organizations.  

"Since early 2018, the incidence of broad, indiscriminant ransomware campaigns has sharply declined, but the losses from ransomware attacks have increased significantly, according to complaints received by IC3 and FBI case information," says the FBI.

"Although state and local governments have been particularly visible targets for ransomware attacks, ransomware actors have also targeted health care organizations, industrial companies, and the transportation sector."

Whatever you do, don't pay the ransom

FBI urges all individuals or organizations that have been infected with ransomware not to pay the ransom but, instead, to contact their local FBI field office and report the incidents to ic3.gov as soon as possible.

The FBI will get in touch with the victims after receiving their reports and will work with them to discover what is the best course of action going forward.

"Regardless of whether you or your organization have decided to pay the ransom, the FBI urges you to report ransomware incidents to law enforcement," says the FBI PSA.

"Doing so provides investigators with the critical information they need to track ransomware attackers, hold them accountable under U.S. law, and prevent future attacks."

It is important to provide IOCs, even anonymous ones, to the FBI as soon as possible in order to aid ongoing ransomware investigations. By not reporting them as soon as possible, they quickly diminish in value to investigators.

The FBI also advises the U.S. organizations currently targeted by a heavy barrage of ransomware attacks to follow these best practices:

• Regularly back up data and verify its integrity
• Focus on awareness and training
• Patch the operating system, software, and firmware on devices
• Enable anti-malware auto-update and perform regular scans
• Implement the least privilege for file, directory, and network share permissions
• Disable macro scripts from Office files transmitted via email
• Implement software restriction policies and controls
• Employ best practices for use of RDP
• Implement application whitelisting
• Implement physical and logical separation of networks and data for different org units
• Require user interaction for end-user apps communicating with uncategorized online assets

U.S. hospitals and schools under attack

The DCH Regional Medical Center, Northport Medical Center, and Fayette Medical Center hospitals from West Alabama are three of the U.S. organizations that got infected with ransomware just this week, with the attacks disrupting their IT systems and only allowing limited access.

All three of them said in a joint press release published on Monday that they had to completely close down and that they're only accepting the most critical new patients.

Following another ransomware incident from early August, Californian medical practice Wood Ranch Medical said on September 18 that it will be closing offices on December 17 due to the extensive loss inflicted on their patient healthcare records.

U.S. schools are also targeted, with hundreds of them announcing that they've fallen victim to ransomware attacks since the start of 2019 according to reports published this week by Emsisoft and Armor.



"A total of 54 education victims, potentially impacting over 500 individual K-12 schools have been reported in the US since January 2019," says Armor.

On the other hand, Emsisoft's report which also takes into account colleges and universities says that "1,051 individual schools, colleges and universities" were potentially affected by "at least 62 incidents."

Emsisoft also states that "from Q1 to Q3 there were a total of 491 ransomware attacks on healthcare providers" while another "68 state, county and municipal entities have been impacted since the beginning of the year."

To put the severity of the ransomware risks into perspective, Louisiana's Governor John Edwards declared a state of emergency in late July after a huge wave of ransomware attacks targeted school districts in the state.

"The cybersecurity incident has already impacted school systems in Sabine, Morehouse, and Ouachita parishes in North Louisiana," stated a press release published at the time.

Legislation passed in reaction to increased ransomware activity

In response to this year's rampant ransomware attacks against U.S. state and local governments, as well as businesses and organizations, the Senate passed the 'DHS Cyber Hunt and Incident Response Teams Act' just last week to authorize the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) to maintain incident response teams for helping private and public entities defend against ransomware and cyber-attacks.

As per the passed legislation, these teams will be responsible for:


• assistance to asset owners and operators in restoring services following a cyber incident;
• identification of cybersecurity risk and unauthorized cyber activity;
• mitigation strategies to prevent, deter, and protect against cybersecurity risks;
• recommendations to asset owners and operators for improving overall network and control systems security to lower cybersecurity risks, and other recommendations, as appropriate;


These teams will provide both advice and technical support on how to strengthen IT systems against ransomware and other types of malware or attacks to any entities that ask for their assistance.

Federally-resourced cyber response teams will also assist organizations as part of the incident response phase if they fall victim to ransomware or any other types of cyber-attacks.
