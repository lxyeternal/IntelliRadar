
	   


A Thanos ransomware campaign targeting mid-level employees of multiple organizations from Austria, Switzerland, and Germany was met by the victims' refusal to pay the ransoms demanded to have their data decrypted.

Thanos ransomware is a Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) operation advertised on Russian-speaking hacker forums that allows affiliates to customize their own ransomware through a builder offered by the developer.

Some Thanos ransomware samples have previously been tagged as the ransomware strain dubbed Hakbit due to different encryption extensions used by affiliates, Recorded Future's Insikt Group says that they're the same malware.

GuLoader used to drop the Thanos payloads

The attacks started with a low-volume phishing campaign spreading malicious Microsoft Excel attachments sent from email accounts registered on the servers of the GMX free email provider according to the Proofpoint Threat Research Team which spotted these attacks.

The phishing campaign focused on customer-facing employees with public business contact information such as client advisors, attorneys, insurance advisors, managing directors, directors, and project managers.

"Users targeted were employed in mid-level positions across the pharmaceutical, legal, financial, business service, retail, and healthcare sector," Proofpoint found.

"The largest volume of messages we observed were sent to the information technology, manufacturing, insurance, and technology verticals."

The phishing messages delivered attachments camouflaged as invoices and tax refund documents which dropped the GuLoader (aka CloudEyE and vbdropper) malware downloader.


Phishing email sample (Proofpoint)

To make sure that the malicious attachments are opened on a device that the ransomware payloads can encrypt, the attackers instruct the targets to download the attachments to their computers.

"Please note that due to technical reasons the Excel document is not displayed correctly on mobile devices," the emails read. "We ask you to download the invoice on your computer and open it."

Once the downloader gets dropped on the phishing victims' computers, it is executed to download and launch a Thanos ransomware payload which comes with built-in file theft and automatic spreading.

Recorded Future said earlier this month that the documents Thanos ransomware steals by default are '.docx', '.xlsx', '.pdf', and '.csv', but ransomware affiliate can add or remove any extensions when building the ransomware executable.

Thanos uses the PSExec program to download the ransomware executable to computers that will get encrypted using AES-256 encryption.


Thanos ransom screen (Proofpoint)

Victims refuse to give in to Thanos' demands

After encryption, the ransomware warned the European victims that they were hacked via a ransom screen and dropped a ransom note with instructions on how to pay the €250 worth of bitcoins ransom.

Unfortunately for the Thanos ransomware operators, the German, Austrian, and Swiss companies that got their computing systems encrypted following the phishing attack dismissed their ransom requests.

"As of June 16, 2020, our researchers have found no transactions showing payment of the ransom to the bitcoin wallet" used as part of the campaign tracked by Proofpoint's Threat Research team.

Two weeks ago, Thanos ransomware was the first to weaponize RIPlace, an anti-ransomware evasion technique tagged by Microsoft as not satisfying the company's security servicing criteria.
