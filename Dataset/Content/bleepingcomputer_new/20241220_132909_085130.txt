
	   


David W. Kent pleaded guilty last week of hacking his former company to boost his current business, which he then tried to sell back to his former firm, together with the stolen data.

According to court documents, in March 2000, Kent founded the website Rigzone.com, a site that provided jobs & networking services to professionals working in the oil and gas industry.

Following a successful business development strategy, Kent sold Rigzone to the DHI Group in August 2010 for $51 million. As part of the deal, Kent agreed to stay with the company as its president.

Following the expiration of a two-year non-compete clause, in October 2013, Kent launched the website Oilpro.com, a service of the same nature as Rigzone.

Kent orchestrated Operation Resume Hoard

Unknown at the time was that Kent fueled Oilpro's growth by secretly accessing Rigzone's database without authorization and stealing information on Rigzone users.

Stolen data included names, resumes, contact info and more. The FBI says that Kent used this information to sent email invitations to Rigzone users, asking them to join his new service, Oilpro. Internally, Kent referred to this as "Operation Resume Hoard."

From April 2014, when Kent set off Operation Resume Hoard in action, and until January 2016, the Oilpro database had grown to almost 540,000 members.



Rigzone employees first suspected that something was wrong after they created accounts on their own site, only to receive email invitations to join OilPro.

The employees quickly suspected that someone had accessed their database because they never finished filling in their resume, so their profiles were never listed on the public Rigzone portal.

Their fears were confirmed later on when they created fake public accounts, which eventually ended up on the Oilpro portal. Authorities were called in to investigate in early 2016.

Kent hacked Rigzone twice

The FBI discovered that Operation Resume Hoard included two phases. A first phase occurred in April 2014, when Kent scraped data on 96,000 users, and a second stage took place in July 2015, when Kent accessed resumes for over 700,000 users.

The FBI says that 17.7% of the total of 796,000 Rigzone users received an email to join Oilpro. Around 35.7% of Rigzone users who received these emails joined Oilpro. In total, the FBI found that over 111,000 users had profiles on both sites.

The FBI also uncovered that Kent hired freelancers specifically for the data scraping and resume import operations.

Furthermore, Kent also recruited at Oilpro a former Rigzone employee, who still had access to Rigzone's Google Analytics dashboard. This yet unnamed employee stole data from the Rigzone Google Analytics account and forwarded it to Kent.

Kent tried a third hack when attempting to sell Oilpro to Rigzone

Starting with October 2015, Kent initiated talks with Rigzone's CEO, in an attempt to sell his new website to his former company, hoping for a similar payout.

Three days after his latest meeting with Rigzone executives, on December 11, 2015, Kent made his biggest mistake, when he attempted to hack his former company once more.

By that time, Rigzone started to suspect that something was off, and had patched the resume_writer.asp file, the point of entry for Kent's second hack. But this time around, Kent also failed to mask his IP and attempted to breach Rigzone using an IP address associated with Oilpro.

During subsequent meetings with Rigzone execs, Kent described Oilpro's growth as the result of classic marketing strategies. Kent even emailed Rigzone execs a spreadsheet showing Oilpro's user growth, which included significant spikes in the months following his first and second intrusions.

Kent faces up to five years in prison

Following an FBI investigation, an arrest warrant was issued on March 23, 2016. Kent was set free on a $250,000 bond after pleading guilty in court.

Kent was charged with one count of intentionally accessing a protected computer without authorization, which carries a maximum penalty of five years in prison.
