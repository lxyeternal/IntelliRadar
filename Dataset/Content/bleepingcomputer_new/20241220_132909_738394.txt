
	   


Unidentified hackers have stolen $31 million  (₽2 billion) from customer accounts at Russia's Central Bank, but officials have managed to recover $26 million (₽1.66 billion) from the stolen funds, said the bank in a report released today, first reported by Reuters.

Officials say the hackers stole the money after they used customers' credentials to initiate the transactions, which were then sent to foreign banks.

The report doesn't specify how Russian Central Bank officials discovered the hack, but they did so in time to froze some of the funds while being exchanged through different bank accounts, in order to lose their trace.

Bank officials recovered $26 million, but the report didn't specify if the hackers made off with the rest, or the money is still being tracked and could be recovered in the future.

The attack is very similar to one from earlier in the year, when hackers attempted to steal almost $1 billion from the Bangladesh Central Bank, but only managed to siphon off $81 million.

Most of the money was sent to casinos in the Philippines. Until now, the Bangladesh Central Bank managed to recover $18 million from the stolen funds.

Russian secret service warns of cyber-attacks against banks

The Russian Central Bank report comes on the same day that the FSB (Federal Security Service) had issued an alert that foreign intelligence agencies might try to destabilize Russia's banking sector with rumors of a fake crisis, fake news about bank failures, SMS alerts, and cyber-attacks.

FSB said its agents uncovered servers hosted in the Netherlands, owned by a Ukrainian web hosting company, which were supposed to be used in the alleged campaign.

The FSB said this covert campaign was meant to start on December 5 and its role was to destabilize Russia's economy. FSB officials said they were ready to take any necessary measures to counter this threat.
