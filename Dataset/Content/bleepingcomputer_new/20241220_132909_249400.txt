The server in question is located at 5.101.66.85, an IP assigned to a hosting provider in Saint Petersburgh, Russia, according to Heimdal Security, the company that discovered the connection. The link is also obvious on VirusTotal.

On Twitter, Peter Kruse, founder of CSIS Security Group and an expert who collaborated with Heimdal in the past, said the connection goes beyond the shared server, but has not provided any other details.

While evidence is thin at the moment, most security researchers won't be surprised by these findings, as many believe that the people behind the Necurs, Dridex, Locky, and Jaff operations are interconnected, if not the same group.

The main connection behind these three operations is Necurs, today's biggest spam botnet. In the past, this botnet has been used to spread the Dridex banking trojan and the Locky ransomware.

Distribution of the Locky ransomware stopped last December, and in early May this year, Necurs started distributing the Jaff ransomware.

Necurs is also the main source of spam for the Dridex banking trojan, a malware family specialized in stealing banking logins and online credentials.

The PaySell store , which experts discovered, is selling access to hacked bank accounts, PayPal profiles, eBay accounts, and profiles at various online shops (listed below). The PaySell store is also selling access to private user information such as Social Security numbers and W-2 tax information.

Furthermore, there's a section in the store where users can buy access to a user's hacked computer. Only Windows systems are included.



The information advertised on the site is the type of information Dridex can collect from its victims.

Banking trojan operations are usually made up of different groups, each specialized in a different field. There are groups that handle spam operations, malware coding, distribution via exploit kits, on-the-ground money mule operations, selling hacked data, and more. We've seen this in the past with many crews, such as the ones behind Gozi, Lurk, and others.

Finding a store selling access to hacked data on the same server used for distributing Jaff can't be just an accident. As the investigation progresses, researchers might find solid evidence that PaySell is actually one of the online stores through which the Dridex crew is monetizing some of its stolen goods.
