
	   


The website of Luas.ie, the tram rail system operator in Dublin, Ireland, has been taken offline today after someone replaced its content with a ransom note demanding one bitcoin not to publish customer data.

The tram operator did not offer any details about the incident, at first blaming "an ongoing issue" for the trouble, and saying that technicians were working to fix it.

Users were also initially advised not to load Luas website or click anywhere on it. The company informed that it would use Twitter for any travel updates and gave a number for customer queries.

Before the website was taken down, it showed a message from a perpetrator bothered by the operator's lack of response to their messages informing of security problems.

The note demanded payment of one bitcoin (currently $3,845) in five days, to prevent the publishing of "all data."

You are hacked
some time ago i wrote that you have serious security holes
you didn't reply
the next time someone talks to you, press the reply button
you must pay 1 bitcoin in 5 days
otherwise I will publish all data and send emails to your users
btc address: 3FsR4CTUmumBJK12Zk8QRwdpPTJEY11aSX

Luas website is purely informative but it does offer customers the possibility to sign up with their email address to receive promo alerts and info. The required fields include full name, email address, the Luas line used along with the journey's start and end points, and the password.

Later on Thursday, Luas announced that its website had been compromised, offering no specific details except that its homepage showed a "malicious" note from the perpetrator and that solving the issue would probably take the entire day.



At publishing time, the website is still offline, showing an error that indicates that the hosting server cannot be reached.

The tram system operator is unlikely to pay the ransom as the bitcoin wallet provided by the attacker is still empty and there are no transactions tied to that address.

Chances are this is nothing more serious than a defacement attack, which comes with risks of its own since users land on a page they consider safe and could be tricked to access malicious online locations or download malware.

Luas public transportation has not been affected in any way by this incident.
