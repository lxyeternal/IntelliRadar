
	   


Joe Siegrist,  Founder, VP & GM of LastPass announced yesterday that the company's main product, the LastPass password management service, will be free for everyone to use, on all devices.

Founded in 2008, LastPass has evolved from a desktop tool to an all-around password management utility, with versions targeting all major operating systems, from Mac to Windows, and from Android to iOS.

Until yesterday, users had the option to install a LastPass app on their smartphone, tablet, or PC, and automatically sync passwords to the LastPass secure cloud for free, from that initial device alone.

Starting yesterday, LastPass has removed this requirement, and now passwords are synced to any device the user logs into with his LastPass account.

The LastPass Premium plan will continue to be available for $1 per month, but the multi-device password syncing feature was its main draw, and users might not be that interested in paying for the other features, which include a password sharing feature among family members, 2FA support, desktop fingerprint identification, priority tech support, and 1GB of free encrypted file storage.

LastPass going free is the reputational boost the service needed

While most experts see LastPass and Dashlane fighting for the top spot on the password management application market, the app's reputation has taken a hit in the past several months.

First, there was the massive security breach in the summer of 2015, when an unknown attacker managed to gain access to the service's servers and stole user email addresses, password reminders, server-per-user salts, and authentication hashes.

The attacker couldn't get his hands on encrypted password vaults, but a security breach is a security breach, and a slew of negative headlines didn't help LastPass' marketing efforts.

Following that, there were countless of security flaws, which even if fixed in due time by the LastPass team, left a big dent in the application's reputation, which now many people view as more insecure than before.

Here we list work by Martin Vigo & Alberto Garcia Illera, Sean Cassidy, Mathias Karlsson, and Google's Tavis Ormandy.

With LastPass going free for everyone, the service is now once again appealing, despite the bad stretch it's been going through. My recommendation is to give it a try. With countless data breaches that during this year alone have exposed over one billion user records, it may be a good idea to start practicing a good password hygiene and start using more complex password strings, which applications like LastPass can help you remember.
