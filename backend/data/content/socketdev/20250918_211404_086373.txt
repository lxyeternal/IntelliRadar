npm has been plagued by phishing attacks on popular maintainers over the past few months, leading to a series of high-profile package compromises. The most recent incident targeted prolific package maintainer qix and DuckDB, among other accounts, essentially giving attackers the keys to widely used projects in the JavaScript ecosystem.
Socket CEO Feross Aboukhadijeh joined the Risky Business Weekly podcast today to unpack these incidents and discuss their impact on open source software supply chains. He noted that by download count, this was among the largest npm compromises ever seen.
“On the one hand, this is by download count probably the biggest supply chain attack that has happened in the npm ecosystem and maybe even beyond that," he said. "We’re talking 2-3 billion downloads per week in the affected packages.”
Despite the enormous potential impact, the attackers haven't achieved much so far in terms of payout.
“The impact has been pretty disappointing," Feross said. "The attackers managed to snap up about $500 worth of Ethereum and maybe 50 bucks worth of some other miscellaneous cryptos. For having all that access, this is what they did with it — somewhat disappointing.”
He warned that the outcome could have been far worse if the attackers had been more careful. Although the most recent payload was clumsy and quickly detected, Feross emphasized that organizations should not underestimate the danger. These kinds of compromises show just how much is at stake if attackers choose to act with more subtlety.
