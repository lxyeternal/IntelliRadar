In this Soapbox edition of the Risky Business podcast, Patrick Gray sits down with Socket founder and CEO Feross Aboukhadijeh for a deep dive into reachability analysis and its role in vulnerability management. The conversation starts with the familiar problem of scanners overwhelming teams with alerts, flagging CVEs without any indication of whether the vulnerable code can actually be reached in a real application. Feross explains why this is such a difficult challenge to solve, touching on the halting problem, the limits of static analysis, and why dynamic languages like JavaScript and Python make the work especially complex.
Most tools that claim to do reachability struggle with transitive dependencies or monorepos, often leaving teams with incomplete or misleading results. Feross contrasts this with Socket’s two complementary approaches: Tier 1 reachability, which analyzes the full application and dependency tree and can cut noise by up to 90 percent, and Tier 2 precomputed reachability, which works from manifest files alone and is much easier to roll out at scale. Both methods make vulnerability data more actionable, separating real risk from false alarms.
They dive into what these techniques reveal about large-scale codebases in practice. Feross shares what Socket has seen in Fortune 50 environments, from “phantom dependencies” that aren’t declared but still get imported, to the massive sprawl of modern dependency trees where companies end up running dozens of versions of the same package. He also explains how Socket’s acquisition of Coana brought in decades of academic research on reachability, particularly in dynamic languages, and how it's reshaping organizations' approach to vulnerability prioritization.
